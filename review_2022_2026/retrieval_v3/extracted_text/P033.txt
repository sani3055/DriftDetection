ABSTRACT  Anomaly detection is a critical aspect of various applications, including security, healthcare, and n etwork 
monitoring. In this study, we introduce FusionNet, an innovative ensemble model that combines the stre ngths of multiple 
machine learning algorithms, namely Random Forest, K-Nearest Neighbors, Support Vector Machine, and Mu lti-Layer 
Perceptron, for enhanced anomaly detection. FusionN et's architecture leverages the diversity of these algorithms to achieve 
high accuracy and precision. We evaluate FusionNet' s performance on two distinct datasets, Dataset 1 a nd Dataset 2, and 
compare it with traditional machine learning models , including SVM, KNN, and RF. The results demonstra te that FusionNet 
consistently outperforms these models across both datasets in terms of accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score. On Dataset 1, 
FusionNet achieves an accuracy of 98.5% and on Dataset 2, it attains an accuracy of 99.5%. FusionNet's remarkable ability to 
detect anomalies with exceptional accuracy undersco res its potential for real-world applications. This  study highlights the 
significance of FusionNet as a robust model for ano maly detection and provides insights into its super ior performance over 
traditional models. The results emphasize the promi sing prospects of FusionNet in security, healthcare , and other domains 
where accurate anomaly detection is crucial.

I.     INTRODUCTION 
The healthcare landscape is undergoing a remarkable  
transformation, fueled by the convergence of cuttin g-edge 
technology and medical science [1]. The Internet of Medical 
Things (IoMT) is at the forefront of this digital r evolution, 
representing a new paradigm in which medical equipm ent, 
sensors, and healthcare systems are all networked a nd 
exchange information that can improve patient care and 
clinical results [2]. The advent of IoMT heralds a more 
efficient and patient-centered era of healthcare by  allowing 
for remote monitoring, individualized care, and imm ediate 
access to vital health data [3]. The need to protect the security 
and privacy of sensitive healthcare data is one of the most 
significant concerns presented by this rapid integr ation of 
technology into healthcare [4]. 
The healthcare industry increasingly relies on the smooth 
flow of patient information to make educated decisi ons and 
provide prompt, life-saving care; as a result, the security of 
IoMT networks has emerged as a significant problem [5]. 
Malicious actors, such as hackers looking to obtain  private 
patient information, malware assaults on medical devices, or 
even interruptions in essential healthcare services , can take 
advantage of the networked nature of IoMT devices a nd 
healthcare systems to exploit security flaws. For t he sake of 
both patient and provider trust and professional in tegrity, it 
is crucial that healthcare data remain private, int act, and 
easily accessible. 
The existing body of research on Intrusion Detectio n 
Systems (IDS) for the Internet of Medical Things (I oMT) 
gives essential insights into numerous approaches, such as 
deep learning, machine learning, and fuzzy logic, t hat have 
been utilized to improve the security of IoMT netwo rks [6]. 
Researchers have performed surveys to evaluate the efficacy 
of existing IDS technologies in healthcare settings , and they 
have proposed new methodologies to enhance the 
effectiveness of intrusion detection [7]. The poten tial to 
improve the cyber security of IoMT networks has bee n 
shown by these studies. However, a prevalent weakne ss 
across the research is the lack of realistic deploy ment and 
scalability studies, which are important for real-w orld 
application. This void emphasizes the necessity of additional 
research to thoroughly address these difficulties [8], [9]. 
The urgent necessity to close the knowledge gap bet ween 
theoretical developments and practical application in IoMT 
Intrusion Detection Systems is the driving force be hind this 
study's innovative research [10]. While there has b een 
substantial development in the existing research to  improve 
the precision and originality of IDS, there is a di stinct lack

Deep learning, machine learning, and fuzzy logic ar e only 
few of the methods that have been discussed in the literature 
on Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) in the Interne t of 
Medical Things (IoMT) to improve cybersecurity. Stu dies 
have been done to assess the efficacy of current ID S 
techniques, and additional ways have been proposed to 
bolster IoMT network security. The results generall y 
highlight increased intrusion detection performance and new 
solutions. However, a major issue is the lack of pr actical 
implementation and scalability assessment. These studies lay 
the groundwork for tackling the urgent problem of protecting 
private healthcare information in IoMT, but they al so 
highlight the necessity for more extensive, real-wo rld 
evaluations. 
IoMT Intrusion Detection Systems have made great st rides, 
but there is still a lot of room for improvement in  terms of 
research into how to actually put these systems int o practice 
and how well they scale. Most research has concentr ated on 
bettering detection accuracy and developing novel 
methodologies, but there has been little systematic  
deployment and assessment in actual healthcare settings. The 
rising number and variety of healthcare devices in IoMT 
networks also presents unique issues, which have re ceived 
very little attention in the literature. To assure the efficacy 
and reliability of IDS in IoMT, given the sensitivi ty and 
criticality of medical data, and to design solution s that can 
adapt to the changing healthcare landscape, closing  this gap 
is essential. 
B.  Materials and Methods 
In this section, we delve into the methodology empl oyed in 
our research to detect anomalies in the Internet of  Medical 
Things (IoMT) using two distinct datasets: the ICU- IOMT 
dataset and the WUSTL EHMS 2020 dataset. Anomalies in 
IoMT can have significant implications for the secu rity and 
integrity of sensitive healthcare data. To address this critical 
concern, we leveraged ensemble models based on Mult i-
Layer Perceptrons (MLP), which are a type of artifi cial 
neural network known for their effectiveness in com plex 
pattern recognition tasks. Our methodology for anom aly 
detection in IoMT is multifaceted, and it encompass es data 
preprocessing, model selection, and evaluation tech niques 
tailored to the unique characteristics of each data set. We 
recognize the importance of adapting our approach t o the 
specific nuances and intricacies of the data source s to 
achieve accurate and reliable anomaly detection. Th e ICU-
IOMT dataset and the WUSTL EHMS 2020 dataset are 
invaluable resources, each offering unique insights  into the 
realm of IoMT. The former, originating from ICU set tings, 
provides real-time monitoring data from a high-stak es 
medical environment. The latter, sourced from the W USTL 
Electronic Health and Medical Systems (EHMS) datase t of 
2020, offers a broader perspective on healthcare da ta in an 
increasingly digital world. In the following sections, we will 
detail our data preprocessing techniques, the architecture and 
configuration of our MLP-Ensembled Models, and the 
evaluation metrics employed to assess the performan ce of 
our anomaly detection system. Our goal is to contri bute to 
the development of robust and efficient methods for  
safeguarding IoMT networks against potential securi ty 
threats and anomalies, ensuring the security and pr ivacy of

Proposed Model: FusionNet: 
The FusionNet model is an ensemble approach that 
amalgamates the strengths of multiple machine learn ing 
algorithms to enhance the predictive accuracy and robustness 
of the intrusion detection system. FusionNet fuses the 
outputs of Multilayer Perceptrons (MLPs) with those  of 
Random Forest (RF), k-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), Supp ort 
Vector Machine (SVM), and another MLP layer in a 
sequential manner. This multifaceted model capitali zes on 
the distinctive capabilities of each constituent al gorithm, 
resulting in a comprehensive and high-performance intrusion 
detection system. Figure 4 shows the architecture o f

D.  Conclusions 
In conclusion, our study aimed to evaluate the perf ormance 
of the FusionNet model, an ensemble of various mach ine 
learning algorithms, in the context of anomaly detection. We 
compared FusionNet's performance with three traditi onal 
machine learning models: Support Vector Machine (SV M), 
K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), and Random Forest (RF) o n 
two distinct datasets, Dataset 1 and Dataset 2. Fus ionNet 
demonstrated exceptional performance on Dataset 1, 
achieving an accuracy of 98.5%, a precision of 99%,  recall 
of 98%, and an F1 score of 97%. It outperformed the  
traditional models in all performance metrics.SVM, KNN, 
and RF showed lower accuracy compared to FusionNet, with 
SVM achieving 81%, KNN achieving 78%, and RF 
achieving 80%. The precision, recall, and F1 score for these 
models also exhibited relatively lower values compa red to 
FusionNet. FusionNet maintained its impressive 
performance on Dataset 2, achieving an accuracy of 99.5%, 
a precision of 99%, recall of 99.8%, and an F1 scor e of 
99.7%. Once again, it outperformed the traditional models 
by a significant margin. SVM, KNN, and RF, on the o ther 
hand, showed a comparatively lower accuracy on Data set 2, 
with SVM achieving 85%, KNN achieving 78%, and RF 
achieving 86%. The precision, recall, and F1 score for these 
models on Dataset 2 were also lower in comparison t o 
FusionNet. These results indicate that FusionNet 
consistently outperforms traditional models across both 
datasets in terms of accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score. 
FusionNet's remarkable ability to detect anomalies 
accurately demonstrates the power of ensemble model s in 
combining the strengths of multiple machine learnin g 
algorithms. The outstanding performance of FusionNe t can 
be attributed to its unique architecture, which com bines the 
capabilities of Random Forest, K-Nearest Neighbors,  
Support Vector Machine, and Multi-Layer Perceptron.  This 
fusion of different machine learning techniques all ows 
FusionNet to excel in detecting anomalies with high  
accuracy, making it a robust choice for various ano maly 
detection applications. In conclusion, our study suggests that 
FusionNet is a promising model for anomaly detection tasks, 
showing superior performance compared to traditiona l 
models. Its ability to maintain high accuracy and p recision 
across different datasets underscores its potential  for real-
world applications in security, healthcare, and other domains 
where anomaly detection is crucial. Further researc h and 
fine-tuning of the FusionNet model may lead to even  better 
results and broader applications in the future.
