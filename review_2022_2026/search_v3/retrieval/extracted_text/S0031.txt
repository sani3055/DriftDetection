A federated and transfer learning framework 
for high-accuracy, explainable intrusion 
detection in next-generation data centers
Mbarek Marwan1*, Mohammed Chemmakha1, Abdelkarim Ait Temghart2 and Mohamed Lazaar1
Discover Artificial Intelligence
Abstract
Cybersecurity is a critical concern for next-generation data centers, where fast 
and reliable Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) are essential. While traditional deep 
learning models deliver strong performance, they often face limitations related to 
data privacy, latency, and dependence on centralized architectures. Accordingly, 
we propose FedTran, a novel IDS pipeline that supports collaborative learning 
by leveraging secure model updates and shared learned representations, while 
rigorously ensuring privacy preservation and data confidentiality. First, data 
imbalance is handled using SMOTE–Tomek Links, followed by feature selection 
through metaheuristic optimization techniques, specifically the Growth Optimizer 
Algorithm (GOA), Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA), and Particle Swarm 
Optimization (PSO). Second, FedTran adopts a kappa-weighted fusion approach 
to integrate pretrained ResNet50, InceptionV3, and DenseNet121 models within a 
transfer learning (TL) framework. By mapping tabular features to structured RGB 
images, the proposed IDS model facilitates hierarchical and spatial feature learning 
using CNN-based architectures. Third, Federated Averaging (FedAvg) facilitates 
decentralized, privacy-preserving collaborative training across distributed edge nodes. 
In this setting, improved generalization and reduced overfitting are achieved through 
complementary mechanisms, including data distribution handling, regularization 
techniques, early stopping, and ensemble learning, thereby limiting sensitivity to 
dataset-specific noise. On the UNSW-NB15 dataset, the proposed method achieves 
96.06% accuracy with FedAvg and 99.98% accuracy with the TL model, significantly 
surpassing the conventional CNN in both predictive accuracy and training efficiency. 
Furthermore, the integration of a SHAP-based XAI module allows FedTran to maintain 
high detection accuracy while offering transparent and interpretable insights for 
effective security decision-making.

1 Introduction
Edge data centers, a distributed IT architecture that brings processing closer to the data 
source, reduce latency and lower bandwidth costs, thereby improving user experience 
and application performance [ 1]. In this setting, typical hyperscale clouds are primarily 
designed to process essential tasks and store data for long-term retention. By contrast, 
edge nodes are geographically positioned close to enterprises, or even deployed on-site, 
to support ubiquitous IoT and 5G adoption [ 2]. Moreover, edge data centers serve as a 
viable extension to the currently dominant cloud platform by providing distributed com-
puting capabilities and an open IT architecture. Resource-constrained devices, such as 
sensors and actuators, can benefit greatly from this model by connecting wirelessly to 
nearby networks, allowing them to collect and exchange data efficiently [ 3]. Although 
existing cloud–edge architectures are capable of meeting next-generation network 
(NGN) capacity and performance requirements, they continue to face several chal -
lenges, including data security and privacy protection, bandwidth limitations, and cache 
management constraints [ 4]. Besides, the major vulnerabilities and numerous potential 
cybersecurity threats have increased due to the rising connectivity and accessibility of 
susceptible smart devices. Specifically, real-time and low-latency processing of IoT data 
necessitates the use of lightweight privacy-preservation systems for both authentica -
tion and encryption techniques [ 5]. In light of this fact, it is of utmost importance to 
build appropriate security measures that accurately identify security vulnerabilities and 
mitigate them. Unfortunately, the lack of fundamental security protections allows cyber-
attackers to launch different kinds of attacks, such as denial of service (DoS), transmis -
sion control protocol (TCP) floods, IP spoofing, SYN flooding, buffer overflow, and ping 
of death [ 6]. Basically, host-based IDS models are used to monitor system activities for 
signs of suspicious behavior, whereas network-based IDS models are designed solely to 
analyze network traffic [7]. To achieve effective detection capability, a hybrid IDS is used, 
combining a model that detects deviations from the system’s normal profile with another 
that compares incoming patterns against recorded signatures.
Recent research has explored the potential of deep learning (DL) methods to develop 
IDS models capable of distinguishing between various attack types and normal network 
traffic. In general, DL models require large amounts of labeled training data to achieve 
strong performance and avoid overfitting. Federated learning (FL) and transfer learning 
(TL) provide effective mechanisms to overcome these constraints by supporting cross-
domain knowledge transfer and privacy-preserving collaboration among edge devices 
without raw data exchange [ 8]. Importantly, edge nodes store their data locally to pre -
serve privacy, instead of relying on centralized techniques that require large amounts of 
data and incur significant computational overhead. Accordingly, communication among 
edge nodes is coordinated through the cloud, which distributes the required model 
parameters and subsequently receives the updated parameters from each node. These 
updates are then aggregated by the central cloud to construct the global IDS model.
Based on these considerations, by leveraging well-known CNN models that are 
already trained on datasets stored in the cloud, transfer learning (TL) enables the reuse 
of these pre-trained models and allows them to be fine-tuned using a smaller amount 
of labeled data at the edge nodes. The basic idea of federated learning (FL) is to send 
only local model parameters to the central cloud server while allowing individual edge 
nodes to train IDS models on limited local datasets [9]. In an FL model, none of the edge

By adopting a decentralized IDS architecture, TL and FL techniques enable scalable

reducing both latency and power consumption. To keep pace with evolving secu -

exchanges model updates obtained during the training phase. Under these circum -
stances, we can develop deep learning models based on TL or FL to share knowledge 
across edge nodes and enhance accuracy, security and efficiency.
However, intrusion detection system (IDS) models often operate as black boxes, pro -
viding limited insight into the rationale behind their AI-driven decisions. Explainable 
artificial intelligence (XAI) addresses this limitation by providing transparency and 
interpretability for machine learning predictions [ 10]. In this context, we apply Shap -
ley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) and Permutation Feature Importance (PFI) to analyze 
the influence of individual dataset features on prediction outcomes, thereby improving 
the interpretability and explainability of the proposed IDS behavior. This helps overcome 
key limitations of AI-based IDSs and provides insight into classification results. In addi -
tion, XAI techniques support effective hyperparameter tuning, including the selection 
of network depth, number of neurons, activation functions, optimizers, learning rate, 
batch size, and training epochs. This capability significantly reduces training time while 
improving overall model performance.
The major contributions of this article are as follows:
(1) We use a hybrid technique for data augmentation and feature selection allows the 
strengths of both approaches to be integrated into a single efficient model. Such 
preprocessing for IDS classification is intended to improve model performance by 
enhancing data quality and reducing both noise and training time.
(2) We propose an efficient transfer learning (TL) algorithm based on CNN model 
for an IDS model, in which models are first trained on large datasets from related 
domains and then fine-tuned on the target dataset to address the problem of data 
scarcity. Moreover, an ensemble TL approach is employed to improve deep learning 
performance by combining multiple models.

2 Related work
As IoT devices have grown rapidly, security issues at the different layers of communi -
cation between these devices and edge data centers need to be addressed. To this aim, 
recent research has focused on deep learning-based intrusion detection systems to mini-
mize cybersecurity risks and protect against cyber threats. Among deep learning neural 
networks, several CNN-based methods are employed in IDS systems to protect against 
Zero-Day attacks. In [11], mean-convolutional layer and SoftMax classifier were used to 
learn and detect abnormal content features. To achieve a global optimum, the combina -
tion of CNN and recurrent neural networks (RNN) is an efficient approach to build an 
IDS model with high detection capability [12]. The IDS system developed by Zhang et al. 
[13] was based on the GoogLeNet Inception and CNN models. Yin et al. [ 14] combined 
an enhanced deep capsule network with an attention mechanism to develop an IDS 
model that improves feature representation and accurately distinguishes between nor -
mal and attack events. After using SMOTE to handle an imbalanced dataset, the authors 
in Lin et al. [ 15] relied on an RNN and a long short-term memory (LSTM) network 
to detect various types of attacks with high accuracy and efficiency. In the same line, 
the authors in Shone et al. [ 16] used a deep autoencoder and random forest for feature 
extraction. Next, CNN-based multi-class classification is used for network traffic data. 
In Li et al. [ 17], the authors suggested deep transfer learning (DTL) to develop the IDS 
model on CIC-IDS2017 for achieving better performance. Okey et al. [ 18] suggested a 
framework for intrusion detection in cloud-based IoT systems. The proposal uses trans -
fer learning to support devices with limited resources. In most cases, tabular datasets are 
not suitable for modeling IDS-based CNNs as they do not consider spatial relationships 
between features. In Li et al. [ 19], the authors proposed a graphic conversion technique 
that converts attack data into image format for the NSL-KDD dataset. In a similar vein, 
Zhu et al. [20] proposed an optimized approach that maps dataset features to pixel posi -
tions in an output image by grouping similar features. To better adapt the base models 
to edge data center environments, the hyperparameters of the proposed IDS model are 
carefully tuned and optimized to further enhance performance.
Recently, hybrid blockchain-based IDS have emerged as a new decentralized approach 
that performs security analysis and access control close to the data source, thereby 
reducing latency and enhancing user privacy [ 21, 22]. Within this framework, machine 

Page 5 of 49
Marwan et al. Discover Artificial Intelligence           (2026) 6:255 
learning is employed to enhance intrusion detection performance, while blockchain 
technology provides secure, immutable, and decentralized data storage [ 23]. More -
over, the platform employs the InterPlanetary File System (IPFS) for off-chain data stor -
age and utilizes zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) mechanisms to ensure data integrity and 
secure data transmission [24]. On the one hand, the Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance 
(PBFT) consensus algorithm ensures system resilience and prevents single points of 
failure, enabling effective intrusion detection within heterogeneous IoT networks [ 25]. 
On the other hand, the proposed hybrid blockchain-based framework integrates elliptic 
curve cryptography (ECC) with federated learning to balance robust security guarantees 
against the limited computational and energy resources of IoT devices [26].
Nevertheless, despite significant advances in deep learning–based intrusion detection 
for edge data centers, numerous challenges remain to be addressed. This study intro -
duces an advanced hybrid model that integrates transfer learning (TL) and federated 
learning (FL) to enhance the accuracy and robustness of the IDS while addressing the 
complexity and challenges of resource-constrained edge networks. Overall, the com -
bination of TL and FL models promotes strong generalization capabilities, making the 
proposed framework suitable for deployment across diverse network environments and 
intrusion detection benchmarks beyond UNSW-NB15. In fact, the FL model further 
enhances generalization by exposing the global model to heterogeneous data distribu -
tions across multiple clients or data center nodes, while the TL model enables learning 
of generic, dataset-agnostic network traffic representations, such as flow-level statistics 
and temporal patterns On the other hand, explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) pro -
vides a principled way to guide the selection and configuration of hyperparameters dur -
ing the model design process. Wang et al. [ 27] employed SHAP and Local Interpretable 
Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME) to integrate both global and local explanations. In 
this context, XAI techniques are used to identify and interpret the relationships between 
different types of data center attacks and the corresponding dataset features.
3 Proposed framework
Recently, advanced deep learning (DL) techniques have been proposed for IDS in edge 
computing to overcome the limitations of traditional AI techniques due to the increased 
number of learning layers. To discover hidden trends and patterns in raw datasets, DL 
models perform better in the case of large datasets, as their performance is directly pro -
portional to the volume of training data. On the one hand, transfer learning (TL) is an 
effective approach to model and predict different types of cyberattacks in edge nodes 
with a scarcity of data and computing resources by using models that have been pre -
viously trained on cloud servers. This study examines the effectiveness of deep learn -
ing approaches in improving intrusion detection through the identification of unknown 
attacks in data centers. To validate the proposed IDS model, we assessed its effectiveness 
in correctly identifying various attack categories based on multiple performance metrics. 
Our research suggests a novel approach that leverages both transfer learning and convo-
lutional neural networks (CNNs) to build intelligent IDS in edge data centers. Because 
of its excellent performance in classification, CNN is not only a very strong approach 
for handling numeric tabular data, but it can also handle image data. In fact, CNNs 
make use of filters (also known as kernels) to perform operations on images, includ -
ing edge detection, blur, and sharpening. To achieve this, we first need to transform the 

Page 6 of 49
Marwan et al. Discover Artificial Intelligence           (2026) 6:255 
numerical tabular data into an image representation. The resulting image labels have to 
be utilized in accordance with the dataset's chunk attack patterns. Importantly, this pre-
trained model combines the concepts of transfer-learned architectures that utilize the 
strengths of various models such as OnlyCNN, VGG16, DenseNet121, ResNet-50, Effi -
cientNetB0, InceptionV3, and MobileNetV3Small to train the IDS model. On the other 
hand, FL model has been considered a viable option to address cyberattacks as it enables 
several edge nodes to train a common IDS model without actually exchanging local data 
with other nodes. This technique requires model updates to be sent to the cloud servers 
for the aggregation of local model parameters.
Unlike existing works, the proposed framework applies an end-to-end explainable AI 
(XAI) approach tailored to produce reasonable and intuitive explanations of CNN-based 
IDS models. On the basis of the above considerations, Fig.  2 presents an overview of the 
proposed IDS model with the XAI module.
The proposed framework consists of four key components: (1) the input dataset, which 
consists of both normal and malicious network traffic; (2) a distributed machine learn -
ing framework based on FL model; (3) a TL model that relies on pre-trained AI models 
to improve performance; and (4) XAI tools to interpret and explain IDS models. Over -
all, the modular design allows efficient fine-tuning using limited labeled data, enabling 
rapid adaptation without full retraining. As a result, the proposed framework is expected 
to preserve stable performance across different intrusion datasets despite domain shifts. 
Moreover, transfer and federated learning jointly encourage generalized representations 
and robustness to dataset-specific bias, thereby reducing the risk of overfitting to data -
set-specific patterns.

entails locating, rectifying, or eliminating dataset issues, including handling outliers, 
deleting duplicates, and dealing with inconsistent or missing data. In this instance, we 
converted categorical attributes to numerical values using one-hot encoding. Moreover, 
we use an unsupervised technique (called an isolation forest) that employs a group of 
decision trees to find outliers in a dataset. Given the limited availability of training data

This study introduces an innovative framework that integrates transfer learning (TL) 
and federated learning (FL) within resource-constrained edge computing environments, 
enabling the effective utilization of data from diverse sources while minimizing compu -

4.1 Transfer learning
The proposed TL model leverages pre-trained networks often convolutional neural net -
works (CNNs) originally trained on large-scale datasets. A typical CNN architecture 
comprises convolutional layers, pooling layers, and fully connected layers. By employing 
TL, IDS models can learn generic network-traffic representations that are largely data -
set-agnostic. These low- and mid-level features have been shown to transfer effectively 
across benchmark intrusion detection datasets, which share common protocol behav -
iors despite differences in attack distributions. Another important consideration when 
applying TL is that samples must be extracted and converted into grayscale images. Var-
ious methods exist for converting tabular data into images; however, all images must be 
resized to a fixed resolution before being fed into the CNN model. Moreover, instead of 
relying on a single classifier, we employ ensemble learning based on multiple classifiers 
to aggregate the outputs of CNN models for improved accuracy. In this work, we pro -
pose an effective ensemble-based framework that integrates several pre-trained CNN 
models with varying depths, including OnlyCNN, VGG16, DenseNet121, ResNet-50, 
EfficientNetB0, InceptionV3, and MobileNetV3Small. The top three best-performing 
CNN models are then selected to construct the final TL model.
Practically, the system examines incoming traffic and divides it into two categories: 
normal and abnormal. First, we use a random search strategy to obtain high-performing 
hyperparameters and optimize the CNN model under examination to detect the most 
well-known attacks. To select the best three pre-trained models, it is particularly use -
ful to compare each CNN model’s performance when using Adam optimizer, which 
enhances convergence speed and achieves better loss-function minima. On the other 
hand, the ensemble learning approach aggregates multiple base models to enhance over-
all predictive performance and robustness. Motivated by this principle, we integrate 
three distinct base models into a unified framework, enabling the ensemble to lever -
age the complementary strengths of each component. This combined model serves as a 
powerful tool for processing and interpreting the input image data, ultimately leading to 
more accurate and reliable outcomes.
Without delving into implementation details, a typical architectural representation of 
the TL-based IDS system consists of several interconnected modules, namely: a prepro -
cessing module, a data transformation module, a pre-trained CNN module, an ensemble 
CNN module, and an XAI module. Figure 3 conceptually illustrates the proposed frame-
work and highlights how these modules are seamlessly integrated to form the complete 
system.
More interestingly, we investigate explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) to improve 
the performance of intrusion detection systems. The framework is evaluated based on 
its ability to accurately predict attacks using well-known evaluation metrics.

4.2 Federated learning
Several edge nodes must collaborate to develop a unified learning model through local 
training, which necessitates the use of distributed learning techniques. Federated Learn -
ing (FL), unlike traditional centralized learning frameworks, is a decentralized approach 
that preserves data privacy and confidentiality by ensuring that each local model is 
trained exclusively on data residing at the corresponding edge node. In this paradigm, 
communication between the edge nodes and the cloud server is limited to the exchange 
of updated model parameters rather than raw data, thereby significantly reducing trans -
mission latency and communication overhead. By retaining the training data at the 
edge nodes and transmitting only the locally optimized parameters to a global aggrega -
tion server, federated learning (FL) enables K edge nodes to collaboratively construct 
a shared learning model, as illustrated in Fig.  5. In this framework, each edge node 
independently trains a local IDS model using its private dataset and periodically sends 
the updated parameters or gradients to the aggregation server localized at the cloud 
computing.

4.6 Hyperparameter setting
Hyperparameter tuning is the process of selecting the optimal set of values and algo -
rithms for a CNN model to enhance its performance. More importantly, hyperparam -
eter tuning helps optimize a deep learning model by controlling the bias–variance 
trade-off, improving its ability to generalize. Adjusting parameters slightly to achieve 
even greater performance is known as fine-tuning. Note that fine-tuning during transfer 
learning involves releasing and unfreezing some or all of the pre-trained model’s layers 
to allow it to better adapt to the current task. This approach is used to determine the 
optimal hyperparameters for a CNN model, such as batch size, number of hidden layers, 
number of epochs, activation function, optimizer, learning rate, and filter sizes. More -
over, Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) splits the data into good and bad groups 
and then uses the density ratio of these groups as an acquisition function (AF). After 
separating the data, the TPE algorithm uses this density ratio as the acquisition function 
to guide the sampling process. In this study, we use TPE because it is fast, simple, and 
non-parametric, with a runtime complexity of O (nlogn).
For transfer learning, all pre-trained CNN models are fine-tuned under identical 
experimental conditions to ensure a fair comparison. ImageNet pre-trained weights 
are adopted, and the models are trained for 10 epochs using the Adam optimizer with 
a learning rate of 0.0001 and a batch size of 32. Categorical cross-entropy is employed 
as the loss function, with ReLU activation in hidden layers and Softmax in the output 
layer. The input image size is set to 256 × 256, and dropout with a rate of 0.5 is applied to 
reduce overfitting.
T o ensure reproducibility, the federated learning framework is configured with 10 dis-
tributed clients, each holding a local partition of the UNSW-NB15 dataset. These clients 
update a shared global model locally several times to improve communication efficiency. 
For the FedAvg model, the number of epochs is set to 600 for each global model update 
during synchronization. Training is performed using FedAvg model, also known as 
Local SGD, over 5 communication rounds (R = 5), where all edge nodes participate in 
each round. During every round, each edge node conducts 2 local training epochs (E = 2) 
on its private data using a mini-batch size of 32 and the Adam optimizer with a learn -
ing rate of 0.0001. After local training, edge’s model updates are transmitted to a central 
cloud computing and aggregated using a data-size weighted average, following the stan -
dard FedAvg strategy. The resulting global model is then redistributed to all edge nodes 
for the next communication round until convergence is achieved.

kernel, a regularization parameter = 1.0, a kernel coefficient γ = scale, and balanced class

The aim of this study is to build a robust IDS system that leverages an advanced deep

5.1 Transfer learning
To build efficient IDS models for edge data centers, convolutional operations are per -
formed on RGB images. Since CNN classifiers are designed for image classification, we 
convert the dataset into image matrices using the OpenCV library to create RGB rep -
resentations. This representation facilitates the learning of higher-level abstractions 
and complex inter-feature relationships that are less effectively captured by traditional 
tabular models. Moreover, similar tabular-to-image encoding strategies have been suc -

strating improved performance over conventional deep learning approaches. After this 
transformation, we label the generated images based on the attack pattern of each data 
chunk. This process is repeated until all labels in the dataset are correctly transformed 
into images by mapping features to pixel positions. Finally, the UNSW-NB15 dataset is 
converted into RGB image blocks, which improves the learning capability of the pro -

5.3 Explainable deep learning
XAI methods aim to enhance the transparency of highly complex and opaque deep 
learning models by providing IDS models with reasoning that is both generalizable 
and understandable to humans. Among the most widely used techniques for improv -
ing interpretability in machine learning are SHAP and LIME. For deep learning models, 
SHAP offers a more comprehensive global interpretation, whereas LIME is particularly 
effective at providing local explanations. To achieve this, SHAP employs a mechanism 
known as the SHAP kernel to define coalition weights, ensuring that each coalition of 
features contributes appropriately to the estimation of Shapley values across the dataset. 
Essentially, SHAP unifies LIME and Shapley values by constraining explanations to an 
additive feature attribution model.

6 Conclusion
Cloud–edge data centers are expected to transform digital infrastructure by bring -
ing data processing and storage closer to end devices, thereby addressing the growing 
demands for high performance and low latency. However, edge environments generally 
exhibit weaker security due to their limited memory and processing capabilities com -
pared to hyperscale data centers. In next-generation security platforms, we explore how 
deep learning models can address intrusion detection challenges by identifying potential 
cyberattacks and analyzing malicious network traffic patterns, particularly for fine-tun -
ing security configurations in next-generation data centers (NGDCs).
Within the proposed IDS framework, we implement both centralized and distrib -
uted detection models to construct federated learning (FL) and transfer learning (TL) 
architectures using CNN-based models. This hybrid approach, which integrates pri -
vacy-preserving learning with knowledge transfer, enables collaborative training across

distributed servers. In addition, we employ a fusion strategy to integrate dataset features 
as well as the outputs of three pre-trained CNN models. Simulation results demonstrate 
that the proposed framework achieves superior performance in both binary and multi-
class classification tasks, thereby ensuring a more robust intrusion detection system for 
next-generation data centers (NGDCs).
To provide a more comprehensive and rigorous evaluation of the CNN-based mod -
els, particularly with respect to the features that most influence IDS performance, we 
incorporate SHAP and permutation feature importance (PFI) explainability techniques. 
SHAP is employed to interpret the contribution and relative significance of input fea -
tures within the proposed IDS. The results highlight the effectiveness of the proposed 
framework, demonstrating how it successfully integrates the complementary strengths 
of multiple deep learning models and feature selection methods to enhance both perfor -
mance and interpretability.
In future work, we plan to apply a Quantum Deep Autoencoder (QDL) to address 
anomaly detection in next-generation data centers (NGDCs). Owing to their reduced 
parameter requirements and fewer training iterations, QDLs offer the potential to design 
an IDS that enhances system robustness while accommodating the resource constraints 
of edge data centers. In parallel, we intend to integrate a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) 
for synthetic data generation to mitigate dataset imbalance. Additionally, we will investi-
gate the impact of various non-IID data distributions on federated learning, with partic -
ular emphasis on effective data partitioning strategies. Although PFI and SHAP improve 
model interpretability, they face robustness challenges in noisy environments. Therefore, 
future research will focus on evaluating explanation stability, cross-client consistency, 
and the level of agreement among different explainable AI (XAI) methods.
