ABSTRACT Intrusion detection system (IDS) is a combination of software application and hardware devices 
that monitors the network and filters activities for malicious or unauthorized access attempts. IDS are 
deployed for generating large volumes of stream data, which can be challenging to identify relevant features 
and reduce false positives. Feature selection is a candidate solution for preserving only relevant features and 
filtering out non -relevant features in IDS. The feature selection  is performed using multi -objective 
optimization based meta-heuristic searching algorithms (MOO-MHS), minimizing two objective functions: 
error rate and memory usage. Traditional FS has limited suitability due to the stream nature of IDS data and 
the occurrence of concept drift.  To solve this challenge, the MOO-MHS is promising to be updated to support 
dynamic feature selection (DFS) for serving IDS. Therefore, this paper proposes a novel framework for DFS 
in IDS using MOO-MHS. The proposed framework is an Enhanced Dynamic Filter-Based Feature Selection 
(E-DFBFS) that enables periodic call of non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm 2 (NSGA-II) that provides 
a novel solution selection algorithm using two selection types, namely, mean and median . The performance 
of the proposed E -DFBFS framework is compared with existing state -of-the-art benchmarking algorithms 
considering both synthetic and real-world data, providing superiority over DFBFS in terms of accuracy and 
F-measure and most classification metrics.

I. INTRODUCTION 
Intrusion Detection System (IDS) is a crucial component in 
modern network security infrastructure, designed to detect and 
prevent unauthorized access, intrusions, and malicious 
activities within a network or system. IDS comprises hardware 
devices and software applications that continuously monitor 
network activities and analyze the data for potential threats. As 
the volume and complexity of cyber threats continue to grow, 
the need for effective intrusion detection systems becomes 
increasingly important.  
An intrusion is generally considered as any unauthorized 
activity that takes place in a network or system. It is essentially 
a breach of the security perimeter of a system and can be either 
malicious or benign. An intrusion becomes a cyber-attack 
when there is an intent to cause harm, such as stealing data, 
corrupting files, or distributing malware. Hence, while all 
cyber-attacks are intrusions, not all intrusions necessarily 
culminate in cyber-attacks. 
Genetic Algorithm (GA) is a wide ly-used optimization 
technique inspired by the process of natural selection and 
evolution [1]. GA has been used to tackle optimisation issues 
in a number of different fields by examining the search space 
for probable answers . In  addition to GA, there are other 
optimization algorithms such as Particle Swarm Optimization 
(PSO) [2]. Ant Colony Optimization (ACO)  [3], and 
Simulated Annealing (SA)  [4], which have been used to 
address different types of optimization problems.

Feature selection (FS) is an integral aspect of the machine 
learning pipeline, as it significantly impacts the model 
performance and outcomes [5]. Many features can increase 
model complexity in high dimensional datasets , leading to 
overfitting and decrease d generalization performance. One 
can simplify the models and optimize their performance  by 
engaging in feature selection . The feature selection process 
involves identifying and eliminating redundant or irrelevant 
features and selecting the most relevant a nd informative 
features for a specific problem. This results in improved 
predictions, increased accuracy, and reduced computational 
costs. Furthermore, feature selection enables a deeper 
understanding of the data and the relationships between 
features, facilitating better interpretation of the models and 
their results.  
Dynamic feature selection (DFS) in stream data refers to 
selecting the most relevant and informative features in real-
time as the data is generated and arrives in a continuous stream 
[6]. This approach is beneficial for handling large-scale and 
rapidly evolving data streams, where the feature space may 
change over time. However, i mplementing dynamic feature 
selection in stream data comes with several challenges. Firstly, 
the feature space may change suddenly, making it difficult to 
determine the relevance of features. Secondly, the volume and 
velocity of the data can lead to comput ational and storage 
limitations, requiring efficient and scalable algorithms for 
feature selection. Thirdly,  the concept drift, where the 
underlying distribution of the data changes over time, can 
result in the selection of suboptimal features, leading to 
decreased performance [7]. Dynamic feature selection 
algorithms must adapt to the data stream's changing nature 
comprising the computational limitations, and detect and 
respond to concept drift. Additionally, the algorithms must be 
able to perform feature selection in real -time and maintain 
their performance over extended periods. 
Three main categories of feature selection techniques exist: 
wrapper, filter, and embedded. Wrapper methods train a 
machine learning model to evaluate feature subsets, resulting 
in higher computational costs but more accurate evaluations. 
Filter methods use statistical measures to evalua te feature 
relevance based on the data's intrinsic properties, offering 
faster and more efficient evaluations but not considering 
feature interactions. Embedded methods combine wrapper and 
filter approaches by integrating feature selection into the 
learning algorithm, providing more accurate evaluations than 
filter methods and more efficient evaluations than wrapper 
methods [8]. 
The choice of feature selection category plays a crucial role 
in determining the effici ency and performance of the 
algorithms in dynamic feature selection in stream data.  
Despite their accurate evaluation of the feature subset, 
Wrapper methods may not be well suited for dynamic feature 
selection due to their computational cost and high demand for 
computational resources. Furthermore, their reliance on 
training a model in real-time may result in slow and unreliable 
performance when dealing with high -volume and rapidly 
evolving data streams. Conversely, filter methods are 
computationally efficient and can be applied quickly to the 
data stream. However, their evaluation of the feature subset is 
based on intrinsic properties of the data and does not take into 
account the interactions between features or the effect of their 
combination on the performance of the model. This may result 
in suboptimal feature subsets, especially in cases where the 
data stream is evolving rapidly and the concept drift is present. 
Embedded methods offer a trade -off between accuracy and 
efficiency in dynamic feature select ion. By integrating the 
feature selection process into the learning algorithm, 
embedded methods can effectively handle the high -volume 
and rapidly evolving nature of stream data.  
Some approaches use genetic programming for feature 
selection and classifica tion [9]. Other approaches have 
provided single objective meta-heuristic for feature selection 
such as [10] that used whale optimization and S -shape 
function. These approaches demonstrate the versatility and 
adaptability of meta-heuristic search algorithms in addressing 
feature selection challenges. By utilizing different 
optimization techniques, researchers can develop innovative 
solutions for various domains and applications, ultimately 
enhancing the performance of classification models. 
The utilization of meta -heuristic algorithms for feature 
selection has gained popularity due to thei r capacity to 
produce near -optimal solutions to complex optimization 
problems [10]. Such algorithms, including genetic and particle 
swarm optimization, employ heuristics to explore the feature 
space and identify the most relevant features. This approach 
proves particularly beneficial in handling high -dimensional 
and large-scale datasets, where traditional feature selection 
methods may struggle to find an optimal solution. For 
example, a recent study applied a Non -Dominated Sorting 
Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) to address false data injection 
attacks in cyber-physical power systems, focusing on multi-
objective optimization that includes both minimization of 
contaminated measurements and maximization of attack 
impact while maintaining stealth  [11]. Another study 
employed genetic algorithms for the real -time detection of 
jamming attacks in CPPSs, further highlighting the versatility 
of meta-heuristic approaches in complex systems [12]. 
However, applying meta-heuristic algorithms to dynamic 
feature selection in stream data poses sever al challenges, 
including the concern of convergence, high computational 
cost, and the issue of using multi -objective meta-heuristics. 
These algorithms must converge quickly to changes in the 
feature space and adapt to the continuous influx of data in real-
time. Moreover, the high computational cost of meta -
heuristics can make it challenging to apply them to high -
volume data streams in real -time. Finally, multi -objective 
meta-heuristics can also present difficulties as they provide a 
set of non -dominated solutions rather than a single optimal 
solution, which may not be suitable for dynamic feature 
selection.

This article aims  to propose a novel multi -objective 
evolutionary algorithm to be used for feature selection by 
proposing a novel method for selecting one solution from the 
Pareto front. Our proposed algorithm is a modification of the 
existing one to make it more effective for producing an 
optimal set of features.  
The primary objective of this article is to improve dynamic 
feature selection in intrusion detection systems by developing 
a more efficient and adaptive approach. To achieve this goal, 
the authors present several significant contributions as 
follows: 
1- They propose a novel variant of an online multi -
objective meta-heuristic searching algorithm, whi ch 
leverages evolutionary-based searching techniques for 
dynamic feature selection. Contrasting with traditional 
multi-objective meta -heuristic searching algorithms 
that provide a set of Pareto front solutions, this new 
approach yields a single optimal sol ution for feature 
selection. 
2- The authors introduce a dual -mode approach for 
generating optimal dynamic feature selection 
solutions. The length of the optimal feature set is 
decided by either the mean or the median of the 
suggestions of solutions discovered in the Pareto front 
once the Pareto front has been constructed and non -
dominated solutions have been supplied . This 
flexibility allows for better adaptation to different  
situations in selecting the most appropriate feature set. 
3- The proposed Enhanced Dynamic Filter-Based Feature 
Selection (E -DFBFS) framework is rigorously 
evaluated using both synthetic and real-world data. The 
performance of E-DFBFS is benchmarked against the 
Dynamic Filter-Based Feature Selection (DFBFS) and 
the traditional Online Sequenti al Extreme Learning 
Machine (OSELM) without feature selection. The 
assessment employs various classification metrics as 
both time series and overall values, demonstrating that 
E-DFBFS outperforms its counterparts in terms of 
accuracy and effectiveness.

searching for feature selection has attracted numerous 
researchers. The use of metaheuristic searching algorithms for 
feature selection has attracted numerous researchers due to 
several compelling reasons. First, these algorithms are known 
for their global search capability, allowing them to efficiently 
explore vast search spaces and locate global optima rather than 
getting trapped in local optima. This capability is particularly 
important for feature selection, where the search space is often 
large and complex. Second, metaheuristic search algorithms 
are highly adaptable as they can be applied to a wide range of 
problems, making them suitable for different feature selection 
tasks across various domains and applications. Third, these 
algorithms can effectively handle non-linear relationships and 
complex interactions among fea tures, which are often 
encountered in real -world problems. Additionally, their 
robustness and ability to provide near-optimal solutions with 
relatively low computational overhead make them an 
attractive choice for researchers working on feature selection 
challenges. However, the majority of them have been applied 
for binary classification problems and with single optimization 
mode as stated in the work of [13]. This survey aims  to 
investigate the usage of meta -heuristic algorithms in multi -
objective optimization for feature selection.

A. Overview of Dynamic Filter-Based Feature 
Selection (DFBFS) Algorithm  
The ensuing section serves to elucidate the fundamental 
constructs underpinning the Dynamic Filter -Based Feature 
Selection (DFBFS) Algorithm. To facilitate a nuanced 
understanding, this overview is partitioned into two salient 
subsections: the fi rst delineates the General Framework of 
DFBFS, while the second delves into its specific 
implementation concerning Dynamic Feature Selection. 
1) GENERAL FRAMEWORK  
The architecture of the Dynamic Filter -Based Feature 
Selection (DFBFS) is visually encapsulated in Figure 1. The 
methodology commences with the initialization of both the 
population and the classifier modules. Subsequently, a buffer-
update procedure is invoked in a continuous manner, 
synchronized with the influx of new data samples into the 
stream. Upon each data sample's arrival, the buffer undergoes 
an update. One pivotal condition is then evaluated whether a 
data drift is indeed detected. In scenarios where a drift is not 
detected, the newly arrived data samples are directed towards 
classification by the extant classifier. It is imperative that the 
classification module be updated with the most recent feature 
set and classifier parameters. The latter are obtained via their 
respective update blocks. Conversely, if a drift is detected, the 
affected d ata samples are earmarked for reevaluation and 
employed to modify both the feature set and the classifier 
parameters. The feature updating procedure is actuated 
through the application of a dynamic feature selection 
algorithm, underpinned by genetic optimi zation techniques. 
This genetic algorithm is designed to iteratively refine its 
solutions, leveraging the population set from its preceding 
invocation. Furthermore, it should be noted that the algorithm 
is configured to yield a pre -determined, fixed number  of 
features, in accordance with a user-defined reduction ratio. 
2) LIMITATIONS OF DFBFS 
The performance of the Dynamic Filter -Based Feature 
Selection (DFBFS) is subject to certain limitations that 
warrant critical attention. Firstly, the algorithm's feature 
selection mechanism is activated exclusively upon the 
detection of a feature drift. This strategy is vulnerable to false 
negatives, leading to potential performance degradation due to 
undetected, yet significant, shifts in feature relevance. 
Secondly, the classifier's retraining is contingent solely upon 
the identification of a feature drift. This conditionality might 
yield outdated models, given the fact that not all types of 
concept drifts are accompanied by feature drifts. In essence, 
while feature drift  reflects temporal variations in feature 
relevance necessitating neural network re-calibration, concept 
drift pertains to shifts in the overall statistical distribution of 
the data that may or may not involve changes in feature 
relevance. Lastly, the criterion for feature selection in DFBFS 
is predicated on a user -defined reduction ratio. This static 
approach may not effectively adapt to the inherently dynamic 
nature of feature drift, especially when such drifts are 
accompanied by changes in the number of relevant features. 
3) DYNAMIC FEATURE SELECTION 
The algorithm governing dynamic feature selection is 
schematically represented in Figure 2. The procedure 
commences with an initialization phase, followed by iterative 
cycles involving crossover and mutation operations. Objective 
functions are then evaluated; specifically, feature relevancy 
and feature reduction serve as the guiding criteria for these 
evaluations. After sorting solutions based on their non -
dominance and calculating their crowding distances, the 
population is updated accordingly. 
Upon the completion of these iterative cycles, the algorithm 
proceeds to what can be termed as an "accumulator -based 
feature selection," conducted specifically using the first rank 
of the Pareto front. To elucidate, this implies that a histogram 
is generated based on the frequency or 'votes' each feature 
garners across the top-ranked solutions in the Pareto front. A 
thresholding mechanism is subsequently employed to 
discriminate between features that have amassed high and low 
accumulative votes, selecting only those that surpass a 
predetermined threshold for further processing. 
The final set of features, now curated, is then harnessed to 
train the classifier. Notably, the algorithm utilizes a filter -
based approach rather than a wrapper-based one, guided by the 
objective functions of feature relevancy and feature reduction 
ratio.

B. Enhanced Dynamic Filter-Based Feature Selection 
(EDFBFS) Algorithm 
This section elucidates the intricacies of our innovatively 
developed Enhanced Dynamic Filter-Based Feature Selection 
(EDFBFS) framework and its associated algorithm. The 
EDFBFS framework is architecturally agnostic, capable of 
integration with a broad spectrum of multi -objective meta-
heuristic algorithms. Specifically, within the context of our 
research, we instantiate the EDFBFS algorithm through the 
adoption of a Non -Dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II 
(NSGA-II) as the underlying optimization mechanism. 
Additionally, we introduce a novel feature in the EDFBFS 
algorithm: the implementation of dynamic and variable-length 
feature selection. This attribute allows the algorithm to adapt 
effectively to varying data landscapes by operating under two 
distinct modes—median and mean. These modes influence 
how the feature selection process dynamically adjusts its 
criteria for feature relevancy, thereby enabling a more tailored 
and robust approach to feature selection. 
1) EDFBFS FRAMEWORK  
As it is depicted in Figure 3, we have updated the original 
Dynamic Filter-Based Feature Selection (DFBFS) framework 
to create the Enhanced Dynamic Filter -Based Feature 
Selection (EDFBFS). When comparing EDFBFS wi th 
DFBFS, we identify three key differences that improve the 
newer version: 
1. Scheduled Feature Selection: In EDFBFS, feature 
selection occurs at regular intervals, regardless of whether a 
change in feature importance has been detected. This contrasts 
with DFBFS, where feature selection only happens when a 
specific check for feature drift is active and confirms that drift 
has occurred. This is observed by changing the conditional 
statement to “update period is reached?”.  
2. Regular Classifier Updates: EDF BFS updates its 
classifier at regular intervals, whether or not feature drift has 
been detected. In DFBFS, the classifier is only updated when 
feature drift is confirmed, which could lead to outdated 
models. 
3. Dynamic Feature Relevancy: EDFBFS does not rely on 
a preset reduction ratio to decide the number of relevant 
features. Instead, it automatically determines feature 
importance based on the data itself. In contrast, DFBFS uses a 
fixed reduction ratio to determine feature importance, making 
it less adaptable to changes. 
2) DYNAMIC AND VARIABLE LENGTH FEATURE 
SELECTION 
Contrary to the foundational algorithm that employs a 
predetermined threshold for ascertaining the inclusion or 
exclusion of features based on their respective accumulator 
values, our enhanced approach—termed Enhanced Dynamic 
and Variable Length Feature Selection (EDVLFS)—leverages 
either the mean or the median of these accumulator values as 
a dynamic threshold. This adaptive thresholding strategy 
offers the advantage of parameter -free feature selection and 
contributes to more stable algorithmic performance, 
irrespective of dataset-specific variations. 
In terms of solution identification, the algorithm generates 
a Pareto front, a collection of non-dominated solutions that are 
optimal across mult iple objectives. This contrasts with 
traditional approaches that yield a singular optimal solution. 
Given that multiple solutions exist in the Pareto front, a 
methodology to select a single, most relevant solution is 
imperative. This is accomplished throug h a specified 
algorithm, delineated in Algorithm 1. The algorithm takes two 
key inputs: the Pareto front, derived from the Non-dominated 
Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA -II), and the type of 
solutions to select from within that front. The output comprises 
the set of features considered to be most pertinent to the task 
at hand. 
In Algorithm 1, relevant features are selected based on a 
Pareto front generated through the Non -dominated Sorting 
Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II). Initially, the length of the 
Pareto front is calculated and stored in a variable, N, and an 
array X of size N is initialized to zero. The number of features 
for each solution in the Pareto front is counted and stored in 
the respective index of array X. Depending on a predefined 
selection type—either 'median' or 'mean' —the 'Number of 
Relevant Features' is computed using either the median or 
mean of array X. For each feature, an 'Accumulator' variable 
is populated with its frequency of occurrence within the Pareto 
front. Finally, features are  selected based on the maximum 
counts in the 'Accumulator', up to the calculated 'Number of 
Relevant Features

In addition to the synthetic data, we evaluate E-DFBFS on

captured over nine weeks, and contained both normal and 
attack traffic. The KDD-99 dataset has since become a widely 
used benchmark for evaluating intrusion detection  systems 
and remains an important milestone in developing network 
security technology. The KDD-99 dataset contains a total of 
4,898,431 records, which are divided into a training set of 
2,540,760 records and a testing set of 1,943,671 records. The 
dataset has 41 features or attributes for each record, including 
basic network traffic characteristics such as duration, protocol 
type, service, source and destination addresses , ports , and 
flags. The dataset has five classes of network traffic, including 
normal traffic and four types of attacks: Denial of Service 
(DoS), User to Root (U2R), Remote to Local (R2L), and 
Probing. The distribution of these classes in the training set is 
imbalanced, with the majority being normal traffic (67%) and 
the remaining classes accounting for only a small percentage 
of the data. NSL -KDD 99 is an improved version of the 
original KDD-99 dataset, which was created to address some 
of the limitations and issues with the original dataset. The 
NSL-KDD 99 dataset has the same basic structure and features 
as the KDD-99 dataset, but with some important differences. 
The NSL -KDD 99 dataset [27] has a total of 1,25,973 
records, which are divided into a training set of 73,053 records 
and a testing set of 52,920 records. This dataset has also 41 
features or attributes for each record, similar to the KDD-99 
dataset. 
However, the NSL-KDD 99 dataset has been preprocessed 
to address some of the issues with the KDD-99 dataset, such 
as the high degree of redundancy and the presence of irrelevant 
and duplicated records. The NSL-KDD 99 dataset also has a 
more balanced distribution of classes, with the majority being 
normal traffic (67.1%) and the remaining classes being 
attacks, including DoS, Probe, U2R, and R2L. 
The evaluation includes generating classification metrics as 
time series and as overall metrics.  The classification metrics 
includes accuracy, precision, recall, F1-metric, G-mean, and 
area under curve (AUC).

V. Conclusion and Future Works 
Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) are a cornerstone in 
modern cybersecurity infrastructure, serving as a vital line of 
defense for id entifying malicious or unauthorized activities 
within networked systems. Despite their critical role, the 
effective deployment of IDS is often fraught with challenges, 
primarily due to the high-volume and dynamic nature of the 
data they are tasked with ana lyzing. A key limitation lies in 
the application of Traditional Feature Selection (FS) 
techniques, which employ Multi -Objective Optimization -
based Meta -Heuristic Searching algorithms (MOO -MHS). 
These traditional methods are ill-suited for IDS because they 
lack the adaptability required for handling real -time data 
streams and concept drift, a limitation that our proposed 
Enhanced Dynamic Filter -Based Feature Selection (E -
DFBFS) framework addresses effectively. 
E-DFBFS is a specialized framework designed for 
Dynamic Feature Selection (DFS) in IDS environments. It 
successfully circumvents the principal challenges commonly 
encountered in dynamic feature selection, which include 
computational complexity, rapid convergence, model 
stability, and adaptability to concept drift. By employing an 
accumulator-based feature selection mechanism, which is 
constructed from Pareto front solutions, E -DFBFS 
substantially mitigates computational overhead. The 
integration of the Non-Dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm 
II (NSGA -II) into the framework ensures both rapid 
convergence and seamless adaptability to the continuously 
evolving nature of incoming data. The use of mean or median 
as adjustable thresholds within this mechanism imbues the 
model with a robust level of stability.  We conducted an 
exhaustive evaluation of E -DFBFS using both synthetic 
(BGFD1, BGFD2, and BGFD3) and real -world datasets 
(KDD 99 and NSL-KDD 99). The assessment confirmed the 
superior performance of E -DFBFS over conventional 
Dynamic Filter-Based Feature Selection (DFBFS) methods, 
across multiple classification metrics, including accuracy and 
F-measure. As for future research directions, we are 
considering the integration of E -DFBFS with ensemble 
learning approaches to tackle imbalances in data. We also 
intend to incorporate dynamic -aware algorithms like 
reinforcement learning, aimed at further enhancing the 
framework's adaptability to feature drift.
