1 Introduction
The global cyber threat landscape has undergone a paradigm
shift in scope and severity over the past decade. According to
the IBM Cost of a Data Breach Report, the average cost of a data
breach reached $4.88 million in 2024, with network intrusions
comprising the majority of incident vectors (IBMSecurity, 2024).
Organizations across ﬁnancial services, critical infrastructure,
and healthcare sectors face increasingly adaptive adversaries
deploying multi-vector attacks including Denial-of-Service (DoS),
Exploits, Reconnaissance, and Advanced Persistent Threats
(APTs) (Sarker, 2024 ). Traditional perimeter-based security
mechanisms—ﬁrewalls, signature detection, and rule-based
Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS)—are fundamentally ill-suited
to detect zero-day exploits and polymorphic malware, as they
rely on static pattern libraries that cannot adapt to novel attack
vectors(SharmaandGupta,2024 ).
Machine learning (ML) has emerged as a transformative
paradigm for anomaly-based network intrusion detection,
enabling models to learn discriminative representations from
high-dimensional network ﬂow data ( ChenandLi, 2025 ;
Al-HaijaandDroos, 2024 ). Supervised ensemble methods—
particularly Random Forest, XGBoost, and LightGBM—have
demonstrated strong classiﬁcation performance on benchmark
datasets, yet each individual learner exhibits characteristic
failure modes: tree-based methods suﬀer from high variance;
gradient boosting is computationally intensive; and neural
networks remain opaque black boxes oﬀering no interpretable
decision rationale for security analysts ( Capuanoetal., 2022 ;
Demšar, 2006). Stacking generalization —a meta-learning
strategy wherein a higher-level learner is trained on the out-
of-fold predictions of diverse base models—has been shown to
mitigate individual learner weaknesses while preserving their
collective discriminative strength (Wolpert, 1992; Rajagopaletal.,
2020).
Despite these advances, two critical gaps persist in the existing
literature. First, the majority of published IDS frameworks
optimize solely for classiﬁcation accuracy, neglecting the
organizational requirement for transparent and auditable AI
decisions. Regulatory frameworks such as the EU AI Act,
NIST AI RMF, and ISO/IEC 42001:2023 explicitly mandate
explainability for high-risk AI deployments in security-critical
environments ( Barredo-Arrietaetal., 2020 ; Paniguttietal.,
2023). Second, reported performance improvements are
rarely subjected to rigorous statistical signiﬁcance testing,
rendering claimed superiority claims potentially attributable
to random variation across experimental folds rather
than genuine model improvements ( Demšar, 2006). These
limitations collectively undermine the operational deployment
readiness and governance compliance of published IDS
models.
This paper addresses both gaps through the following
contributions:
1. CyberShield stacking ensemble: A two-level stacking
architecture with nine heterogeneous base learners and an
XGBoost meta-learner, achieving AUC-ROC = 0.9929 andF
1 =
0.9535ontheUNSW-NB15benchmarkdataset.
2. Domain-informed feature engineering: A 22-feature
expansion from 45 to 67 dimensions using interaction terms,
logarithmic transforms, and ratio features, all constructed
without data leakage by applying transformations exclusively to
training-partitionstatistics.
3. Leakage-free evaluation protocol: A rigorous pipeline-
encapsulated evaluation framework in which all preprocessing,
scaling, and meta-feature generation operations are
strictly contained within CV fold boundaries, preventing
distributionalleakage.
4. Tri-method XAI layer: A uniﬁed explainability
framework integrating SHAP ( LundbergandLee, 2017 ),
DeepLIFT ( Shrikumaretal., 2017 ), and Integrated
Gradients (Sundararajanetal., 2017 ), providing cross-method
attribution stability validation and actionable feature rankings
forSOCdeployment.
5. Rigorous statistical validation: Performance superiority is
veriﬁedviaWilcoxonSigned-RankTestandPaired t-Testacross
ﬁve cross-validation folds, conﬁrming signiﬁcance atα = 0.05
againstthreeoffourbaselines.
1.1 Novelty and positioning
Unlike prior stacking-based IDS studies, which typically
combine three to four homogeneous tree learners and report
only point-estimate accuracy on a single split, the distinguishing
contribution of CyberShield is thesimultaneousintegration of (i) a
nine-learner heterogeneous base layer spanning linear, tree-based,
gradient-boosted, neural, and instance-based model families; (ii)
a strictly pipeline-encapsulated, leakage-free evaluation protocol
applied uniformly to preprocessing, feature engineering, and out-
of-fold meta-feature generation; (iii) a tri-method XAI layer
whose cross-method consensus is treated as evidence of genuine
feature reliance; and (iv) formal statistical hypothesis testing of
the performance gain. To our knowledge, no prior UNSW-NB15
stackingstudycombinesallfourelements;existing works( Table1)
satisfy at most two. The contribution is therefore methodological
rigorandoperationalauditabilityratherthanarawaccuracyrecord.
The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. Section 2
reviews related work in ensemble-based IDS and XAI-augmented
cybersecurity. Section 3 details the proposed methodology.
Section 4 presents and analyses experimental results. Section 6
concludesthepaperandoutlinesfutureresearchdirections.

fundamentally cannot detect zero-day exploits or novel variants of
existing attack families. Anomaly-based IDS emerged to address
this limitation by establishing statistical baselines of normal

2.2 Machine learning approaches for IDS
The proliferation of benchmark datasets such as KDD Cup
1999, NSL-KDD, and UNSW-NB15 ( MoustafaandSlay, 2015 )
accelerated the adoption of supervised ML for IDS. Kasongo
and Sun (KasongoandSun, 2020 ) demonstrated that Random
Forest with wrapper-based feature selection achieved 93.62%
accuracy on UNSW-NB15. Deep learning approaches, particularly
Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Long Short-Term
Memory (LSTM) networks, have demonstrated superior feature
abstraction capabilities. However, these deep architectures
require signiﬁcant training data, computational resources,
and—critically—sacriﬁce interpretability for performance
gains(Capuanoetal.,2022 ;Marinoetal.,2018 ;Leeetal.,2022 ).
Multiclass classiﬁcation on UNSW-NB15 has been studied by
severalgroups. Rajagopaletal.(2020) demonstratedthatastacking
ensemble applied to heterogeneous datasets (UNSW-NB15
and UGR’16) achieved 94%–97% accuracy, outperforming
individual classiﬁers. More recently, Vishwakarmaetal.
(2025) reported that an explainable stacking ensemble
improved model transparency without sacriﬁcing classiﬁcation
accuracy.
2.3 Ensemble methods and stacking
generalization
Stacking generalization, introduced by Wolpert (1992)
and subsequently formalized by Breiman (1996) , addresses
the bias-variance tradeoﬀ by training a meta-learner on
the out-of-fold predictions of diverse base classiﬁers. The
present work systematically extends these contributions
by incorporating nine diverse base learners, enforcing
strict anti-leakage pipeline encapsulation within every
cross-validation fold, and integrating a tri-method XAI
layer for cross-validated explainability ( Zhangetal.,
2024).
2.4 Explainable AI in cybersecurity
The “black box” nature of high-performing ML models
presents a fundamental operational challenge for security
analysts who require not only accurate predictions but also
justiﬁable, auditable decision rationales (Senevirathnaetal., 2024 ;
Barredo-Arrietaetal., 2020 ). SHAP ( LundbergandLee, 2017 ),
grounded in Shapley values from cooperative game theory, has
emerged as the dominant post-hoc explanation method for tabular
ML models in cybersecurity contexts ( Lundbergetal., 2020 ;
Rajpurkaretal., 2024 ). Warneckeetal. (2020); Pandeetal. (2023)
systematicallyevaluatedSHAP,LIME,andgradient-basedmethods
for deep learning IDS, ﬁnding that SHAP provided the most stable
andsemanticallyconsistentexplanationsacrossattackcategories.
DeepLIFT ( Shrikumaretal., 2017 ) propagates activation
diﬀerences relative to a reference baseline through the network in
a single backward pass. Integrated Gradients (Sundararajanetal.,
2017) provides axiomatic guarantees—Sensitivity and
Completeness—by integrating gradients along a linear
interpolation from baseline to input. Capuanoetal. (2022)
evaluated these methods for deep learning-based IDS and found
that cross-method consensus provides stronger evidence of
genuinemodelreliancethananysinglemethodalone—avalidation
principledirectlyadoptedinthepresentwork.
2.5 Organizational cyber risk resilience
Beyond detection accuracy, contemporary cyber risk
governance frameworks. Jalalvandetal. (2024) survey criteria
and methods for alert prioritization in security operations centres
and report that interpretable risk metrics support more eﬀective
triage than opaque alternatives, directly informing the design
philosophyoftheCyberShieldframework.

3 Methodology
3.1 Dataset description
This study employs the UNSW-NB15 benchmark dataset,
generated by the Australian Center for Cyber Security (ACCS)
using the IXIA PerfectStorm tool in a controlled Cyber Range
Laboratory environment (MoustafaandSlay, 2015 ). The dataset
comprises 257,673 network ﬂow records with 45 raw features
spanning nine attack categories—Fuzzers, Analysis, Backdoors,
Denial-of-Service (DoS), Exploits, Generic, Reconnaissance,
Shellcode, and Worms—alongside a Normal traﬃc class.
Following stratiﬁed partitioning, 206,138 samples (80%) were
allocated for training and 51,535 samples (20%) for testing.
A preliminary data quality audit conﬁrmed zero missing
values, zero duplicate records, and a total memory footprint of
138.97MB. The class composition of the dataset is shown in
Figure1, and the class-conditional distributions of the principal
ﬂow features are shown in Figures2–5. Figure6 reports the
skewness and kurtosis of all 45 raw features, and Figure7
shows the distributions of the engineered features by class
Table2.
3.1.1 Classiﬁcation task
Although UNSW-NB15 provides ﬁne-grained multiclass
labels (nine attack families plus Normal), the present study
formulates and evaluates intrusion detection as abinary problem:
each ﬂow is classiﬁed as Attack (y = 1) or Normal (y = 0).
The attack-category labels are retained only for the exploratory
analysis of class prevalence in Section 4.1; all reported metrics,
confusion matrices, ROC curves, and statistical tests pertain
to the binary task. Accordingly, no claim is made regarding
discrimination among individual attack categories. Multiclass
attack-family classiﬁcation is identiﬁed as future work in
Section6.

3.3.1 Rationale for the 22 features
The engineered set was not chosen arbitrarily but derived
from four attack-motivated transformation families, each
targeting a known limitation of the raw features for intrusion
discrimination: (i) logarithmic transforms (x′ = log(x + 1))
applied to the heavily right-skewed volume features (skewness
> 25; seeTable3) to linearise their relationship with the decision
boundary; (ii)ratio features(e.g., byte_ratio, Port_Ratio,
Payload_per_Port) that capture source/destination
asymmetry characteristic of exﬁltration and scanning;
(iii) diﬀerence features(e.g., mean_diff) encoding directional
traﬃc imbalance; and (iv) interaction terms (e.g., Conf×
Payload) combining connection-state and volume signals.
Within each family, candidates were retained only if they (a) were
motivated by an established attack signature and (b) exhibited
non-redundant behavior, i.e. pairwise Pearson correlation
|ρ| < 0.95 with existing features on the training partition. This
procedure yielded 22 features. Their utility is conﬁrmed post
hoc in Section 4.6: three engineered features ( byte_ratio,
log_dbytes, mean_diff)rankwithinthetop-10globalSHAP
importances.

4.5.1 On the reliability of 5-fold testing
With n = 5 paired observations the Wilcoxon Signed-Rank
Test is severely power-limited: its smallest attainable two-sided
p-value is 0.0625, which lies aboveα = 0.05 and thereforecannot
reachsigniﬁcanceregardlessofhowconsistentlytheensemblewins.
This is a structural property of the test atn = 5, not evidence
of an absent eﬀect; indeed the ensemble outperformed XGBoost,
LightGBM, and Extra Trees on all ﬁve folds (W = 0). For
this reason the pairedt-test is treated as the primary inferential
instrument, and it conﬁrms signiﬁcance against three of the four
baselines.Weneverthelessregardtheﬁve-folddesignasalimitation
(Section 6); repeated stratiﬁed cross-validation (e.g., 5×10) or the
corrected resampledt-test would yield more reliable estimates and
isrecommendedforconﬁrmatorystudies.

5.1 Computational cost of the ensemble
Combining nine base learners with a meta-learner necessarily

Crucially, training is an oﬄine, periodic operation, whereas the
inference cost—approximately 64μs per ﬂow—is the deployment-
relevantﬁgure.
5.2 Real-time feasibility
A per-ﬂow latency of∼64 μs corresponds to a single-thread
throughput on the order of 1.5× 104 ﬂows per second, which
rises further with batched and parallel scoring. This comfortably
exceeds the ﬂow-arrival rate of most enterprise network segments
and indicates that near-real-time scoring is feasible. Operationally,
theensembleisthereforebestpositionedasa near-lineanalyticthat
scores aggregated ﬂow records (rather than per-packet) seconds
after ﬂow completion. Where strict line-rate inline blocking is
required, the calibrated single XGBoost learner can serve as a low-
latency ﬁrst stage, with the full ensemble and XAI layer invoked
asynchronously for high-value or ambiguous alerts. Resource

6.1 Limitations
Several limitations warrant acknowledgment. (i) Single
dataset. Evaluation is conﬁned to UNSW-NB15; although
it remains a widely used benchmark, validation on more
recent and operationally diverse datasets such as CIC-
IDS2017 (Sharafaldinetal., 2018 ) and the IoT-oriented ToN-IoT
corpus (Moustafa, 2021) is needed to establish generalization,
and is a priority for future work.(ii) Laboratory traﬃc.UNSW-
NB15 was generated in a controlled environment; evaluation
on live organizational network traﬃc would strengthen external
validity ( MoustafaandSlay, 2016 ). (iii) Binary scope. The
study addresses Attack-vs-Normal detection only and does
not discriminate among the nine attack families. (iv) Limited
folds. The ﬁve-fold design constrains the statistical power of
the Wilcoxon test, whose minimum attainablep-value (0.0625)
exceeds α; repeated or higher-fold cross-validation would yield
more reliable signiﬁcance estimates (Demšar, 2006). (v) XAI scope.
Explanations are computed on representative base learners rather
than on the meta-combination directly (Section 4.6).(vi) Cost.
The ensemble carries higher training and inference cost than a
single model (Section 5.1), which informs the tiered deployment
recommendationabove.
6.2 Future work
Promising directions include extension tomulticlass attack-
familyclassiﬁcation(distinguishingDoS,Exploits,Reconnaissance,
and other categories), cross-dataset validation on CIC-
IDS2017 ( Sharafaldinetal., 2018 ) and ToN-IoT ( Moustafa,
2021), federated learning integration (Sarker, 2024), adversarial
robustness evaluation, online/streaming adaptation with concept
drift detection, counterfactual explanations for SOC analysts,
and integration with knowledge graph-based threat intelligence
platformsalignedwithMITREATT&CK.
Insummary,CyberShieldAnalyticsdemonstratesthatrigorous,
leakage-free ensemble learning combined with multi-method
explainability and statistical signiﬁcance testing constitutes a
principled and deployable approach to organizational cyber risk
resilience.
