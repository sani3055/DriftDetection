Abstract. This paper proposes a solution to the challenges posed by the proliferation of IoT devices,
which has expanded cyberattack surfaces characterized by continuous changes over time (Concept
Drift). Traditional Network Intrusion Detection Systems (NIDS) often suffer from severe performance
degradation when facing novel attacks due to a lack of adaptability. This paper introduces CD-AHAL,
a hybrid security framework that integrates a CNN–BiGRU–Attention deep learning architecture
with an Uncertainty-aware Active Learning strategy, employing an automatic labeling mechanism
via a simulated Oracle. The system is evaluated through a two-stage process: (1) Offline training
to establish a foundational knowledge base; and (2) Online evaluation on a synthetic data stream
exhibiting concept drift. To demonstrate comprehensive effectiveness, we compare CD-AHAL against
three baseline architectural variants CNN-GRU, CNN-Attention, and GRU-Attention operating in a
static (non-updating) mode. Experimental results demonstrate that while static models suffer from
accuracy degradation in changing environments, CD-AHAL is capable of autonomously detecting drift
and rapidly recovering performance, thereby maintaining superior average accuracy and F1-score.
Keywords. Active learning, CD-AHAL, class imbalance, CNN–GRU–Attention, concept drift, IoT
security, network intrusion detection.
1. INTRODUCTION
In the era of the Internet of Things (IoT), the heterogeneity and complexity of inter-
connected devices have created highly dynamic and vulnerable network environments. Al-
though Network Intrusion Detection Systems (NIDS) serve as an essential line of defense,
traditional deep learning models often suffer from poor generalization under changing traffic
conditions [1, 2]. Two key challenges are severe class imbalance, which causes rare attacks
to be overlooked, and concept drift, where evolving attack patterns make previously learned
knowledge obsolete. Recent studies further show that AI-powered NIDS can be vulnerable

A static model, even if accurate in laboratory settings, can quickly degrade when deployed
on real-world streaming data. Recent defense-oriented studies have emphasized adaptive ro-
bustness and evasion mitigation for deep learning-based intrusion detection systems [4,5]. To
address these limitations, we propose CD-AHAL (concept drift-aware hybrid active learning),

Scientifically, this study bridges uncertainty estimation and continuous learning in cyber-
security. Instead of relying only on reactive error-based drift detection, CD-AHAL uses En-

annotation costs through simulated Oracle labeling, making it suitable for low-latency IoT

2. RELATED WORK
CNN-based models have been widely used in NIDS due to their efficient local feature
extraction and low computational cost [9]. However, they are limited in capturing long-
term temporal dependencies in complex attacks. Recurrent models such as LSTM and GRU
address this issue by modeling temporal patterns [10,11]. Attention-based and Transformer
models have also shown strong performance, but their computational overhead limits their
suitability for edge or near real-time deployment [1, 2]. Therefore, a lightweight hybrid
architecture that balances detection performance and operational cost remains necessary.
Class imbalance is another persistent challenge in NIDS, often biasing models toward
majority benign traffic. Although SMOTE [12] is effective in offline training, applying it
directly to streaming data may distort the real-world traffic distribution. In addition, NIDS
models frequently degrade under domain shifts and concept drift caused by emerging attack
variants [13]. Beyond natural drift, adversarial manipulation is also a growing threat, as
adversarial samples can alter traffic representations and mislead deep learning-based detec-
tors [3]. Recent studies have explored adaptive defense strategies and autoencoder-based
mitigation against evasion attacks [4, 5], but they do not fully address online adaptation
under evolving streaming traffic.
To address distribution shifts, several drift detection and online updating strategies have

been proposed, but they still have limitations:
1. Continuous Online Deep Learning (ODL) [14]: ODL enables rapid adaptation but
requires frequent updates, leading to high computational cost and possible catastrophic
forgetting.
2. Reactive Drift Detectors [15]: Methods such as ADWIN rely on error-rate changes,
meaning they react only after misclassifications occur.
3. Inefficient Active Learning [16]: Although Active Learning reduces labeling cost [6],
many NIDS studies still rely on basic uncertainty-based sampling, which may waste
labeling budgets during abrupt drift.
Based on these limitations, this study addresses the following research questions:
• RQ1: How can concept drift be detected proactively before significant classification
errors occur?
• RQ2: How can Active Learning update the model with minimal labeling cost while
maintaining detection accuracy?
Active Learning is effective for reducing labeling effort by selecting high-uncertainty
samples [6]. However, the integration of MC Dropout-based uncertainty estimation [8] with
a lightweight drift-aware NIDS remains underexplored. Although prior works emphasize
adversarial defense and evasion mitigation in AI-based NIDS [3–5], uncertainty-guided active
learning for online concept-drift adaptation is still insufficiently investigated. Therefore,
this study uses Entropy via MC Dropout as an early warning signal to trigger retraining,
balancing accuracy and computational cost [17].
3. METHODOLOGY
3.1. Overall architectural model
The CD-AHAL framework is designed as a continuous learning pipeline that seamlessly
connects static offline initialization with dynamic online adaptation. The overall architec-
ture is built upon a hybrid deep learning backbone, specifically a CNN-BiGRU-Attention
model. The rationale behind this integration is to balance robust feature extraction with low
inference latency. The CNN is highly efficient at capturing local spatial correlations from
standardized network flows, while the Attention mechanism dynamically assigns weights to
critical features, ensuring that subtle anomalies from rare attacks are not overshadowed by
dominant benign traffic.
The proposed system is divided into four tightly integrated functional blocks (see Fig-
ure 1):
• Block 1: Frozen Preprocessing Pipeline. Function: Standardizes raw network traffic
into a unified feature space. Contribution/Rationale: When transitioning from the
offline training phase to the online streaming phase, all normalization parameters are
strictly frozen. This prevents data leakage and accurately simulates real-world opera-
tional constraints where future data distributions are unknown.
• Block 2: CNN-BiGRU-Attention Feature Extractor & Classifier. Function: The core
detection engine. The 1D-CNN extracts local features, which are fed into a Bi-GRU
for temporal modeling, and then passed to the Adaptive Attention Mechanism. Con-
tribution/Rationale: The CNN provides lightweight processing suitable for high-speed
network traffic. The Attention Mechanism serves as a targeted focal point, address-
ing class imbalance by selectively prioritizing minority attack classes and filtering out
irrelevant background noise.

• Block 3: Uncertainty Estimation Module (MC Dropout). Function: Quantifies the
model’s predictive uncertainty (Entropy) for every incoming data sample utilizing MC
Dropout [8]. Contribution/Rationale: Instead of waiting for misclassifications to occur,
the framework uses MC Dropout to execute stochastic forward passes. A sudden spike
in the calculated Entropy serves as a proactive, early-warning signal that the data
distribution has shifted (Concept Drift). This is far more effective than traditional
error-rate monitors like ADWIN [15].
• Block 4: Active Learning & Oracle Labeling. Function: Manages the retraining budget
and updates the model dynamically based on Active Learning principles [6, 7]. Con-
tribution/Rationale: When a drift signal is triggered by Block 3, this module selects
only the samples with the highest uncertainty (Entropy) and queries a simulated Or-
acle for ground-truth labels. By strictly limiting the labeling budget (e.g., to 20%
of the batch), this block ensures rapid fine-tuning without exhausting computational
resources or human-annotation effort.
3.2. Investigated model architectures
1. The proposed CD-AHAL framework is the integration of a CNN + Bi-GRU + attention
architecture. This is the only model equipped with a drift-aware active learning mechanism
in Phase 2.
2. CNN-GRU (baseline 1): The attention module is removed. The model operates under
a static (non-adaptive) setting in Phase 2. The objective of this model is to isolate the
effect of attention and quantify performance degradation when no adaptation mechanism is
available.
3. CNN-Attention (baseline 2): The Bi-GRU module is removed. The model operates
in a static setting. The objective of this model is to assess the importance of temporal
dependency modeling (sequence learning).
4. GRU-Attention (baseline 3): The CNN module is removed. The model operates in a
static setting. The objective of this model is to examine the role of CNNs in denoising and
local feature extraction.

Justification of the entropy threshold and drift signal:
It is important to contextualize why an aggregated Hbatch value (e.g., 0.349 observed
during our streaming evaluation) is considered a strong drift signal, despite being seemingly
low compared to the theoretical maximum entropy bounds (e.g.,log 2 (C)). In NIDS envi-
ronments, benign traffic constitutes the vast majority of the data stream. A well-trained,
highly confident model typically yields an extremely low baseline entropy (approaching 0)

for normal operations. Therefore, any sudden, aggregated spike in Hbatch (such as 0.349)
represents a massive relative deviation from the stable baseline, indicating significant model
confusion.
Furthermore, utilizing the aggregated Hbatch rather than the individual H(x) allows
CD-AHAL to distinguish between genuine concept drift and transient noise. Isolated data
anomalies or transient noise may cause a high H(x) for a few individual samples, but their
mathematical effect is heavily diluted when averaged over a sufficiently large batch size N.
Consequently, a significant and sustained increase in Hbatch strictly correlates with a macro-
level domain shift or the emergence of novel attack variants (Concept Drift), warranting an
immediate active learning update.
Labeling budget strategy: In Phase 2, once drift is detected, the system enforces a
constrained labeling budget to optimize resource usage and maintain timely responsiveness:
Fixed budget (B): Instead of requiring labeling for all new data, we limit label queries
from Oracle to 20% of the number of samples in each batch of data detected as drifting.
Simulated Oracle mechanism: In this experimental framework, the role of Oracle
is simulated by retrieving actual labels (ground truth) available in the test datasets. This
design simulates the real-world process where the system automatically sends suspicious
samples to third-party services (such as VirusTotal, AbuseIPDB) or uses a stronger Teacher
Model for identification, rather than relying on manual operations by human experts.
Sampling process: This process includes two steps as follows.
Step 1 (Uncertainty sampling): The system calculates the Entropy index from MC
Dropout predictions. Samples with the highest Entropy values (indicating the model’s “con-
fusion”) are prioritized for sending queries to the Oracle to obtain the most accurate labels
for updating weights.
Step 2 (High-confidence Pseudo-labeling) : For the remaining samples that are
not selected for labeling, if the model has a very high prediction confidence (Confidence >
0.95), the system will automatically assign a “pseudo-label” based on the current prediction.
These samples are added to the supplementary training set to reinforce knowledge without
incurring labor costs.
Temporal dependency modeling (Bi-GRU):To capture the evolving nature of mod-
ern attacks (e.g., long-duration or slowly varying DDoS campaigns), the CNN-extracted fea-
tures are fed into a bidirectional GRU (Bi-GRU). Unlike purely feed-forward architectures,
Bi-GRU leverages both past and future context within the traffic sequence, which improves
temporal robustness and helps stabilize predictions under time-evolving concept drift.
Adaptive attention mechanism: This is the key component for addressing data im-
balance. The Attention Mechanism allows the model to automatically assign higher weights
to time points containing important information and ignore segments of noise. As a result,
weak or rare attack signals (minority classes) are not “drowned out” by the vast sea of benign
traffic.

3.6. Two-phase detection strategy
To balance real-time alerting requirements with the need for fine-grained analysis to
support digital forensics, CD-AHAL operates under a two-stage pipeline and reuses a shared
feature extractor to reduce computational overhead:
Phase 1: Rapid Screening (Binary Detection): The top priority of NIDS is early detec-
tion. Therefore, the initial model is configured with the Binary Cross-Entropy loss function
to perform the binary classification task: Normal (Benign) versus Attack. This stage helps
to quickly remove most of the clean traffic to reduce the load on the system and provide
immediate alerts when there are signs of abnormality.
Phase 2: Fine-grained Identification (multiclass classification): Once a flow is flagged
as malicious, the system switches to multiclass classification using categorical cross-entropy.
At this stage, the model assigns the traffic instance to a specific attack category (e.g., DDoS,
PortScan, Botnet, WebAttack, etc.) according to the predefined label space.
This hierarchical design improves overall accuracy by allowing each stage to specialize in
a distinct objective, while also maintaining deployment feasibility on resource-constrained
edge devices.
3.7. Handling concept drift via uncertainty-based active learning
To ensure the CD-AHAL model can operate sustainably in a real-world environment
where new attack variants continuously emerge we established an online adaptation process
based on two principles:
1. Preserving data integrity: When transitioning from offline training to streaming de-
ployment, the entire preprocessing pipeline is frozen. Normalization parameters estimated in
the offline phase are reused for all incoming samples, and no synthetic data generation tech-
niques (e.g., SMOTE) are applied to the online stream. This design prevents data leakage
and more faithfully reflects operational constraints.
2. Uncertainty-guided monitoring and updates: Instead of labeling all incoming data
(which is costly and impractical), CD-AHAL leverages MC Dropout to estimate predictive
uncertainty for each instance, quantified by the mean entropy computed over T = 10–50
stochastic forward passes.
Drift detection: A sudden increase in entropy serves as an early-warning signal indi-
cating potential concept drift or domain shift (e.g., the emergence of new attack variants
or abrupt changes in traffic behavior). Upon detecting this signal, the system triggers the
active labeling procedure.
Labeling strategy: The system activates the Active Learning loop, only querying the
Oracle for labels of the most uncertain samples. Using the Oracle instead of humans allows
the system to operate fully automatically. In a real-world deployment environment, this
Oracle would be APIs (VirusTotal, AbuseIPDB) or heavyweight Teacher Models. In this
study, we use the original labels of the data to simulate the absolute accuracy of the Oracle’
s response.
Fine-tuning: The model performs rapid incremental fine-tuning on the newly labeled,
small-scale dataset, allowing performance recovery without retraining from scratch.

All models are trained under consistent settings to ensure fair comparability between
the baselines and the proposed CD-AHAL architecture. We adopt binary cross-entropy for
Phase 1 and categorical cross-entropy for Phase 2, together with class weighting to emphasize
minority attack classes.

again (visible as red markers around Batch 30). As a result, the model achieved a significant
leap in performance, immediately boosting accuracy back to the 90–92% range. This demon-
strates that CD-AHAL’s entropy-based sampling strategy not only helps the model recover
but also enables it to learn new knowledge to achieve significantly higher performance than
its previous fluctuating state.

Toward the end of the experimental cycle, the model encounters a dense series of con-

5. CONCLUSIONS
This paper proposed and conducted a comprehensive evaluation of CD-AHAL, a hy-
brid network intrusion detection framework optimized for dynamic IoT environments. By
combining the feature extraction capability of a CNN–BiGRU–Attention backbone with an
uncertainty-guided active learning strategy, the proposed approach effectively addresses the
dual challenges of concept drift and class imbalance.
Extensive experiments demonstrate that CD-AHAL provides a balanced and practical
solution for IoT settings [24]. Compared with resource-intensive SOTA approaches such as
ARF and ODL, the proposed framework substantially reduces computational overhead (97
updates versus 249) while maintaining stable and efficient latency ( ∼769 ms per adaptation
cycle). Moreover, the entropy-driven drift signal is shown to be more effective than classi-
cal techniques such as ADWIN, suggesting a promising direction for low-resource adaptive
security systems. Results on complex composite streaming scenarios further indicate that
CD-AHAL outperforms static models and representative baselines in terms of rapid accu-
racy recovery and sustained F1-score (achieving 90.77% in binary and 77.33% in multiclass
scenarios), enabled by an oracle-query mechanism under drift.
Future work will focus on two directions to strengthen real-world applicability: (1)
operationalizing the oracle by directly integrating real-time threat-analysis services (e.g.,
VirusTotal, AbuseIPDB) or developing specialized teacher models for automated labeling,

rather than relying on simulated ground truth; and (2) further reducing computational
cost via knowledge distillation to enable even more efficient deployment on highly resource-
constrained edge devices.
