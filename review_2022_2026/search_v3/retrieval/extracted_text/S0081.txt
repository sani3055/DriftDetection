Abstract 
As more IoT-powered Smart farming systems push automation to the next level, many farms are facing security 
risks, including spoofing, data breaches, and denial -of-service (DoS) attacks. Most current intrusion detection 
methods do not perform well due to limited scalability and the decision system's li mited interpretability, as well 
as the dynamic nature of agricultural settings. On the one hand, traditional machine learning models fail to capture 
more complex temporal-spatial patterns in network traffic; on the other hand, many existing deep learning -based 
systems lack satisfactory explainability and are not feasible for edge deployment. To overcome these limitations, 
this paper offers AgroCyberShield, a new cybersecurity framework for IoT-enabled smart agriculture. At the core 
of this framework is the proposed FarmSecureNet algorithm  a hybrid deep learning model that integrates 
Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Bidirectional Long Short -Term Memory (BiLSTM), and attention for 
effective and explainable intrusion detection. These two datasets come from different sources on which the model 
is trained to enable generalisation. Extensive experiments demonstrate that FarmSecureNet achieves 96.4% 
classification accuracy, with F1-score, precision, and recall outperforming baseline models. We present further 
validation of the framework with SHAP -based explainability, confusion matrix analysis , and ablation studies. 
Moreover, the low latency and minimal memory footprint, as demonstrated in edge-deployment tests on Raspberry 
Pi and Jetson Nano, further support real-time performance. The developed AgroCyberShield framework enhances 
the cybersecurity of smart farms while improving decision transparency, contributing to the sustainable and safe 
digitisation of agriculture. 
Keywords - Smart Farming Security, Intrusion Detection, Deep Learning, Edge Deployment, Explainable AI

1. INTRODUCTION 
IoT is rapidly transforming smart farming, turning traditional practices into data-enabled, automated systems. On 
modern farms, connected IoT devices, such as wireless sensor networks, automated irrigation controllers, soil and 
weather sensors, and drone -based monitoring tools, are commonplace, helping improve production, save 
resources, and support real-time decision-making. Meanwhile, the growing reliance on connected natural-physical 
systems in agriculture makes smart farms increasingly vulnerable to cyberattacks. Such threats would be through 
data breaches, spoofing, DoS, malware propagation, and attacks on critical infrastructure that would be damaging 
to crops and cause supply chain failures and economic destruction within the region [1], [2] 
However, studies consider general, shallow, and deep learning models to identify threats in IoT -based smart 
environments, but this approach is  quirky. Some traditional machine learning models, SVMs, RFs, and KNN, 
have been commonly applied as baseline approaches for network  anomaly detection [3], [4], and also, deep 
learning approaches, CNN [5] and RNN [6], have shown better detection performance in time-series-based cyber 
threat detection. However, most current approaches lack robustness in dynamic environments, fail to generalise 
across datasets, and do not provide interpretable outcomes. Furthermore, most existing works do not consider the 
feasibility of deployment on edge devices or leverage heterogeneous data sources to enrich training. This implies 
a significant research gap for a holistic, interpretable, deployable, and specific framework for  intelligent farming 
networks. 
To overcome these drawbacks, this paper presents a novel cybersecurity framework, AgroCyberShield, that aims 
to make intelligent farming networks resi lient against cyberattacks by enabling effective, interpretable 
cybersecurity through deep learning and data fusion . The main goal of this study is to design, develop, and 
implement a low -cost, accurate, and resource -minimal solution. It effectively design s a knowledge-explainable 
IDPS that leverages dual -dataset fusion for DNN feature representation, adopts hybrid deep learning for 
classification, and operates on resource-constrained edge devices. Specifically, the novel FarmSecureNet model 
includes convolutional layers to learn spatial features and bi-directional long short-term memory (BiLSTM) layers 
to learn temporal dependencies, with attention to the most salient intrusion features. This kind of integration will 
reduce the problem of classifying advanc ed attack types with high accuracy while keeping computational  
overhead to a minimum [13]. 
However, beyond the typical stack of CNN, BiLSTM and attention blocks, the proposed AgroCyberShield 
framework introduces several innovations. First, it proposes a dual -source learning strategy to reconcile the 
domain-oriented telemetry from the device TON_ IoT with the network-flow behavioural information of IoT -23, 
thus enhancing generalisation capability across smart farming domains with heterogeneous IoT attack surfaces. 
Second, it puts together a single processing and threat -alignment pipeline by harmoni sing the representation of 
elements in the feature space, semantically mapping the labels, and chronologically constructing the temporal 
sequence of elements . Third, the FarmSecureNet model contains three layers composed of a CNN, a BiLSTM, 
and an attentio n mechanism, each assigned a respective function to capture short -range interactions among 
engineered telemetry and traffic features, temporal attack progression, and the identification of the most influential 
time steps for threat classification. Fourth, explanations and attention visualisation based on SHAP are combined, 
thereby enabling the link between the model's results  and operationally meaningful indicators of the device and 
the network. Fifth, deployment tests on the Raspberry Pi and the Jetson Nan o have been conducted, proving the 
suitability for running inference in a resource-constrained environment with low latency. Finally, the quantitative 
contribution and robustness of each framework's individual components are assessed through ablation analy sis, 
statistical validation, and cross-dataset analysis. 
An integrated and explainable cybersecurity framework , particularly tailored for the smart farming environment 
provided by IoT, is the main contribution of this study. AgroCyberShield brings together heterogeneous telemetry 
and network -layer evidence into a common threat -learning space, unlike generic intrusion -detection systems, 
which are limited to a single traffic domain. It is built on a core detection engine, FarmSecureNet, that combines 
a convolutional component, a bidirectional temporal component, and an attention-based component to incorporate 
local interactions between features, sequential attack behaviours, and attention to relevant parts of the context. 
The framework also provides mechanisms  for explainable AI, enabling clear interpretation of threats and 
facilitating human-in-the-loop security decisions. The effectiveness and reliability of the proposed approach are 
demonstrated through extensive comparisons with baseline models, confusion -matrix analysis, ablation studies, 
and statistical significance testing, and its practical deployability is validated through  experiments on edge 
devices. These contributions point AgroCyberShield towards a domain-specific, understandable and deployable 
security solution for heterogeneous smart farming networks.

2. RELATED WORK 
Traditional agriculture has become a smart farming environment with the widespread deployment of Internet of 
Things (IoT) devices, wireless sensor networks, cloud computing, uncrewed aerial vehicles (UAVs), ar tificial 
intelligence (AI), and automated control. However, technologies such as these enable continuous surveillance, 
precision irrigation, automated decision-making, and efficient use of resources, but also create new opportunities 
for agricultural infra structure to be vulnerable to cyberattacks, privacy disclosure, unauthorised manipulation, 
service disruption, and physical consequences. Previous studies have thus been conducted from architectural, 
cryptographic, intrusion -detection, blockchain, intellig ent analytics and communication viewpoints. The main 
research directions are summarised below: 
2.1 Security Requirements, Threat Models, and Vulnerabilities in Smart Agriculture  
Initial research on IoT -supported agriculture revealed that heterogeneous sens ing devices, gateways, 
communication networks, cloud services and farm management apps pose security requirements throughout 
various layers of architecture [1]. To address the need to account for evolving threats and operational conditions 
within an agricultural setting, adaptive security and scenario -oriented risk analysis were subsequently proposed 
[2]. Strategic assessments of the security issues revealed attacks on the security aspects of confidentiality, 
integrity, availability, authentication, authorisation, and privacy, and showed that smart farming is still vulnerable 
to sophisticated attacks such as advanced persistent attacks [3]. Many vulnerabilities have been identified as 
potential threats, such as device vulnerability, weak authentication, outdated firmware, insufficient data exchange 
security, DDoS attacks, man-in-the-middle attacks, and physical tampering [4]. 
Security evaluations also show that agriculture networks exhibit several characteristics that distinguish them from 
typical industrial networks, such as the placement of devices in outdoor environments, limited power, and physical 
access to devices by adversaries [20]. In recent years, the combination of technological faults, operational 
dependability, security threats, roles of stakehold ers, and countermeasures is related in recent cybersecurity 
taxonomies [17], and threats to precision -agriculture components, data flows, and interacting entities have been 
identified systematically with STRIDE-based threat modelling [14]. At the same time, during practical tests of the 
deployed agricultural platforms, vulnerabilities are identified due to obsolete platforms, insecure management, 
protocol shortcomings, lack of software maintenance, and lack of penetration testing [13]. Other factors such as  
cost, rural connectivity, technical complexity and cybersecurity awareness may also limit the application of proper 
security controls [23]. The agricultural operational characteristics, physical exposure, stakeholders, and domain -
specific risks should the refore also be taken into account in a comprehensive cybersecurity strategy, including 
network attacks. 
2.2 Authentication, Privacy Preservation, and Secure Data Communication  
One of the most significant research paths is authentication and secure communic ation, as malicious agricultural 
devices can tamper with the environmental data or control automated farming processes. To ensure security 
against replay, DoS and resource-exhaustion attacks, lightweight and tamper-resistant authentication mechanisms 
have been developed based on resource -efficient protocols for constrained IoT devices [5]. In addition, 
cryptographic authentication and key exchange protocols using elliptic curve cryptography, hashing, biometric 
features and formal security proof show the pos sibility of maintaining privacy at acceptable computation and 
communication costs [6]. 
Secure feature encoding and attention -based deep learning together are effective in protecting farm data for 
anomaly detection while preserving privacy [7]. Agricultural  data-sharing research also highlights the need for 
encryption, role -based access control, machine -learning algorithms and intrusion detection, and secure data 
sharing [19]. More recent privacy -oriented models secure communications between sensing layers, central

and replay resistance [42]. An important concern is intellectual property protection, as cultivation strategies, plant

2.3 Blockchain, Edge/Fog Computing, and Trustworthy Agricultural Infrastructure 
The application of blockchain has been examined from various perspectives to achieve decentralised trust in 
agriculture, secure storage, provenance, and secure transactions. Blockchain and smart contracts have the potential 
to enable agricultu ral data to be unchangeable and trusted, and interactions between agricultural producers, 
consumers, sensors, and supply -chain participants [9]. Monitoring, irrigation, warehousing, and protection of 
agricultural infrastructure are also possible applicatio ns for IoT –blockchain integration [10]. Blockchain -based 
assurance mechanisms enable transparency and traceability, authenticity and certification for sustainable 
agricultural products, while IoT sensors provide real-time monitoring of environmental and crop conditions [12]. 
At the same time, fog, edge, SDN, and blockchain technologies have been combined to achieve distributed 
protection against DDoS attacks, among others, and to minimise reliance on centralised cloud processing in 
layered security architec tures [21]. Agricultural communities can use cloud -based security monitoring and 
blockchain to capture and share information on unusual activity and attacks across farm nodes [39]. Smart-contract 
authentication also enables secure device-to-device and device-to-gateway communication while keeping devices 
anonymous and communication traceable [40]. Communication between agricultural drones and ground sensors 
has also been explored using blockchain [41], and blockchain and edge computing are used to provide s ecure 
climate monitoring and automatic irrigation decisions [44]. However, in the face of computational, storage, 
latency, consensus, and scalability concerns, especially in limited sensing environments, blockchain poses 
challenges. Thus, blockchain should  be used in tandem with edge intelligence and not as the only cybersecurity 
solution. 
2.4 Machine Learning and Deep Learning for Intrusion Detection 
Machine learning has emerged as a key component of agricultural cybersecurity because signature-based methods 
are not effective in detecting new, or "evolving", attacks. Dimensionality reduction and classifiers like support 
vector machines (SVM) and random forests have been used to classify malicious network activities in smart -
irrigation intrusion-detection frameworks [25]. LSTM, CNN –LSTM, CNN, DNN, and RNN models have been 
used to enhance DDoS detection in recent years using deep learning [26, 28]. To date, IDS surveys have been 
organised by technology enabling the solutions [29], [55], [59], and ensemble clas sifiers have shown that their 
performance is more robust than that of a single machine-learning solution [30]. 
Beyond just specific applications of IDS, there is also general IoT research that can provide useful foundations 
for the design of anomaly-based deep IDS [31], intrusion detection and prevention with ML/DL [35], and multi -
stage classification with clustering, oversampling, and neural classification to resolve data imbalance [33]. 
Techniques proposed for the Industrial IoT system have also shown tha t feature reduction and balancing using 
SMOTE are advantageous for binary and multiclass intrusion classification [36]. To make deep -learning 
predictions more trustworthy, Explainable intrusion detection has added new features such as SHAP, LIME, and 
rule-based explanations [34]. Likewise, generic smart -city IDS solutions demonstrate the applicability of 
lightweight supervised classifiers to IoT networks [56]. 
New smart-agriculture IDSs are increasingly based on hybrid and optimised architectures. BiGRU–LSTM models 
are edge-oriented models that detect attacks with low latency in severe operating conditions for agriculture [49]. 
Transfer-learning-based IDSs with metaheuristic hyperparameter optimisation and ensembles based on 
confidence scores enhance detection capabilities over a variety of datasets for IoT systems [50]. Federated learning 
and blockchain have also been combined to allow intrusions to be detected collectively while maintaining the 
privacy of local data on farms [51]. Hybrid CNN–BiGRU architectures with attention and optimisation techniques 
have also been shown to be an effective DDoS identification method in fog -enabled farms [54]. While there are 
significant advances, challenges with dataset dependency, class imbalance, unknown attacks, explai nability, 
model complexity and cross-farm generalisation remain unsolved. 
2.5 UAV, Edge, Communication, and Emerging Smart-Farm Attack Surfaces 
A modern agricultural network is not only composed of fixed sensors, but also makes use of UAVs, edge nodes, 
LoRa gateways, autonomous machines and distributed communication systems. Precision -agriculture

introduces extra security dependencies [16]. An IoT –UAV smart farming review emphasises connectivity issues

LoRa and LoRaWAN are two available technologies that deliver long -range communication with  energy

not network-level cybersecurity. Trust, resilience, e thical AI, and risk management are other key considerations 
in high-risk rural environments for AI-powered drone-security systems [22]. These studies demonstrate that future 
agricultural cybersecurity mechanisms should not only safeguard the use of IoT sen sors, but the whole cyber -
physical system. 
2.6 Toward Integrated and Adaptive Cybersecurity for Smart Farming  
Studies on smart agriculture invariably use IoT, AI, cloud computing, wireless sensing, automation, and data 
analytics as tools to boost agricultural productivity while also increasing cyber risk [8] [48]. Operational examples 
show the value of connected sensing and UAV imagery, weather monitoring, and automated decision support and 
applications [43], while monitoring systems using cameras and physi cal intrusion sensing show the convergence 
of cyber and physical protection requirements [11]. AI is seen to be a tool for adaptive monitoring, predictive 
threat analysis, automated security management, and incident response [18]. Conventional detection is  further 
enhanced by the ability to collect attack data and proactively manipulate adversarial traffic with cyber -threat 
intelligence and deception-based platforms [27]. Combining early detection, machine-learning classification with 
automated response is a more valuable approach than just attack identification, as seen in the case of SDN -based 
multilayer DDoS detection and mitigation [32]. Other IoT -critical domains, with related softwarized IDS 
architectures, also indicate the potential to improve scalabi lity and responsiveness by implementing security 
services dynamically, accelerating deep learning, and using virtualisation [60]. The convergence of operational 
intelligence and anomaly detection is also in evidence in smart-agriculture monitoring, with hybrid AI/ML models 
now being used to monitor crop health and predict potential issues. The integration of operational intelligence and 
anomaly detection is further reflected in smart -agriculture monitoring, where hybrid AI/ML approaches are now 
employed to track crop health and identify potential problems. Moreover, recent security studies have identified 
attack scenarios, including false -data injection, jamming, DDoS, malware, and unauthorised control. They are 
considering federated learning, blockchain, an d the principle of zero trust as promising research fronts [15]. 
Corroborating survey data indicates that although threat taxonomies and countermeasures are well established, 
comprehensive operational frameworks remain relatively scarce [37]. 
Benchmark intrusion-detection datasets and cutting-edge deep-learning approaches have significantly shaped the 
development of intelligent security systems in IoT. The CICIDS2017 dataset was introduced to include realistic 
benign and attack traffic data with various int rusion scenarios and has later been used as a valuable benchmark 
for assessing new IDS techniques [61]. Likewise, N -BaIoT showed that IoT botnet detection is possible using 
deep autoencoder -based behavioural modelling, emphasising the importance of learnin g individual device 
anomalies to detect compromised IoT nodes [62]. More recent work has focused on interpretable detection, which 
helps enhance intrusion classification and transparency in IoT security decisions using a deep -learning-based 
feature selection mechanism that is interpretable [63]. Moreover, Privacy-preserving Distributed Learning (PDL) 
has emerged as a key consideration, where Federated IDS architectures, combining supervised and unsupervised 
deep-learning models, learn attack patterns while avoiding the concentration of sensitive IoT data at one central 
point [64]. Related federated -learning strategies also show how privacy -preserving collaborative intrusion 
detection can enhance security among heterogeneous IoT participants while exposing le ss local data. 
More recent research also reveals trends toward hybrid, optimised, and trusted cybersecurity solutions. Domain -
oriented IDS architectures are particularly relevant in Agriculture 4.0, where high -performance DDoS detection 
is a specially developed hybrid de ep-learning model [66]. Advanced IoT intrusion -detection models also use 
deeper representation learning to boost discriminative ability between benign and malicious targets in more 
complex attack scenarios [67]. Attention -guided transformer architectures, originally developed for fake -profile 
detection, show the potential of encoder-only attention mechanisms for accurate and explainable classification of 
complex cyber-related patterns [68]. Blockchain-based cybersecurity has also been combined with elliptic -curve

In conclusion, the literature suggests significant advancement in various aspects of authentication, blockchain, 
intrusion detection, edge security, privacy protection, UAV protection, and threat modeling. These capabilities 
are typically explored individually, though. Current solutions often focus on either authentication/autho rization, 
intrusion classification, blockchain-based trust, or individual network-security solutions without integrating them 
together and considering the diversity of agricultural devices, evolving attack patterns, privacy concerns, real -
time detection, e xplainability, distributed intelligence, and adaptable mitigation. Furthermore, many IDS 
techniques are evaluated on generic datasets of IoT traffic, not on agricultural traffic, and a high detection accuracy 
does not guarantee that the IDS is robust to un seen attacks, device variability, changing environments or 
limitations. The following limitations drive the need for AgroCyberShield, which should view cybersecurity in an 
integrated, adaptive and resource -aware manner and be capable of integrating multi -layer threat monitoring, 
intelligent intrusion detection, trustworthy communication, privacy protection, and proactive response for resilient 
IoT-driven smart-farming networks. 
 
3. PRELIMINARIES 
The following section describes the foundational concepts needed to understand the cybersecurity framework we 
propose for intelligent farming networks. Initially, it examines how IoT technologies are integrated into precision 
agriculture, followed by a detailed analysis of emerging cyber threats and defence solutions. This background lays 
the groundwork for the justification and range of AgroCyberShield. 
3.1 Foundations of IoT-Driven Smart Farming 
The Internet of Things (IoT) in agriculture can be charac terised as smart farming or precision agriculture, which 
has transformed traditional agriculture into a data -oriented, automated, and dynamic system. IoT -based smart 
farming is a system of interconnected soil moisture sensors, temperature sensors,  weather stations, smart irrigation 
controllers, and even uncrewed aerial vehicles (UAVs) that gather and transmit data in real time. Drones are used 
in farm fields to monitor crop health & resourceutilization of crops and the environment, which aids decision -
making, minimising waste and maximising yield.

3.2 Cybersecurity Threats and Defense Mechanisms 
The fast rollout of IoT-enabled devices in smart farming, widespread use of automated machines leaves farmers 
vulnerable at all levels of farming systems. These systems are usually set up in plowed fields and consist of 
multiple interconnected environments where sensors, gateways and cloud servers communicate with mobile 
endpoints in the immediate vicinity with very limited computational power and energy. Consequently, various 
types of cybersecurity threats against weak authentication, insecure protocols and lack of intrusion detection will 
be leveraged against intelligent farming networks. 
Denial-of-Service (DoS) and Distributed Denial -of-Service (DDoS) attacks are among the most serious threats, 
as they flood network elements with malicious traffic. The edge gateways or MQTT brokers can be disabled by 
these attacks, interfering with real-time data capture and control. Data injection is another common attack vector, 
where adversaries modify sensor data, for example , engaging in unnecessary irrigation and applying high or low 
fertilizer doses. 
Spoofing attacks refer to the a ct of impersonating legitimate nodes to gain unauthorized access to a system and 
manipulate data. In the case of replay attacks, an attacker records the valid data packets and sends them back to 
the system to create discrepancies with respect to the system state and actuator behavior. In addition, malware 
attacks (such as Mirai, Gafgyt) take advantage of default credentials and open ports to turn Internet of Things 
(IoT) devices into botnets that launch clustered assaults. T able 2 summarizes essential attacks about IoT-based

compromised IoT devices. This usage can serve as a foundation for detecting device-level anomalies and attacks 
against smart farming communication layers.

4.2 Data Preprocessing 
To ensure consistent inputs into FarmSecureNet, a structured and reproducible prepro cessing pipeline was 
developed to preprocess the heterogeneous TON_IoT and IoT-23 records. Due to differences in data types, feature 
structures, and attack annotations, initial data cleaning and extraction were performed independently  prior to 
feature alignment and joint training. 
At TON_IoT, the appropriate telemetry, operating system logs, and network traffic  files were located and 
collected. Records were sorted by time based on the time listed in the original records. There were no duplicate 
entries or corrupted records. All samples were also checked for valid class labels , and those without a valid label 
were excluded. The median estimated from the training partition was used to impute missing numerical values, 
and the most frequent training -category val ue was used to impute  missing categorical values. Source-specific 
attributes, such as device identifiers, record identifiers, and IP addresses, that could lead to memorisation of the 
datasets were not included in the model inputs. To handle categorical fea tures such as device_type, protocol, 
service, state, and so forth, one-hot encoding was used. 
In IoT-23, streamed PCAP files containing raw IoT traces were converted to structured flow-level CSV data using 
CICFlowMeter. The features extracted were as follows: flow duration, number of packets sent from source to 
destination (or vice versa), volume of bytes sent, inter-arrival time statistics, connection states, protocol indicators, 
and TCP flag features. Each capture scenario was sorted by connection start t ime. Only attributes that had zero 
variance were removed, and any numbers that fell above the trustworthy numbers that were assigned to the 99th 
percentile in the training partition alone were clipped . Directional traffic indicators were changed to the typical 
S2D and D2S configurations.

4.2.1 Dual-Dataset Alignment and Fusion Strategy 
Originally, TON_IoT and IoT -23 were not merged directly as they provide different sets of features and attack 
taxonomies, and the data modalities are also different. In its place a feature level domain alignment and late -
concatenation approach was adopted. Each dataset was then separately cleane d and processed. TON_IoT 
telemetry and log data was converted to statistical and temporal descriptors, while IoT -23 packet captures were 
converted to flowlevel features using CICFlowMeter. Source specific bias was avoided by de-identifying the data 
sets wi th the removal of the dataset -specific identification, IP addresses, ports and attributes that cannot be 
transferred. 
Both datasets were subsequently modeled through a common schema for behavioural features of connection, 
which included connection duration, packet statistics, byte statistics, traffic rate, inter-arrival behaviour, protocol 
state, directionality of flow and temporal variation. No attempt was made to transfer the features for which data 
is available in one data set to another, and semanticall y similar behavioural descriptor have been retained. 
Continuous features have been normalised with parameters obtained only from the training partition of each of 
the datasets. Both representations were then fitted with PCA and components corresponding to at least 95% of the 
variance were retained, thus generating a common latent feature space of dimensions equal for the two 
representations. 
The original attack labels were then harmonised to the uniform classes normal, DoS, injection, botnet and malware 
based on the label-harmonisation procedure reported in Table X. The training partitions of TON_IoT and IoT -23 
were then sampled-sequentially concatenated after feature and label alignment to yield the joint training set. No 
preprocessing fitting or oversampling was performed on the validation and test partitions, and they do not contain 
the samples from these partitions. SMOTE was only applied to the entire training set.  
Three assessment approaches were used. GCN was first trained and tested on stratified partitions of samples from 
both datasets; a second round of evaluation was performed where the test segment contained cross -dataset data. 
Second, training and testing were performed on separate models with both datasets also to evaluate the 
performance within each domain. Third, they used cross -dataset evaluation which involved the training of the 
model on one of the datasets, and then testing it in the other dataset after running the same alignment pipeline, 
thus evaluating model transferability under domain s hift. This approach guarantees that the described fusion is 
not simply the concatenation of raw incompatible features but supervised learning on top of a harmonised 
behaviour representation.

4.5 Model Design: FarmSecureNet 
The AgroCyberShield framework is based on the FarmSecureNet model, which is the main model of the intrusion 
detection system. It is created to analyse the temporally ordered feature sequences which are generated after the pre -
processing, feature alignment, label harmonisation and sequence construction of the TON_IoT and IoT -23 datasets. 
The model consists of a sequence of  one dimensional (1D) convolutional layers, a Bidirectional Long Short -Term 
Memory (LSTM) network, attention mechanism, dense classification layers, and an explanation module based on 
SHAPs as illustrated in Figure 2. The fixed architecture allows the mode l to learn local traffic patterns, bidirectional 
temporal dependency and the relative importance of each element of the sequence for explainable multi class intrusion 
detection.

4.6 Pipeline Workflow 
AgroCyberShield is a modular end -to-end pipeline that deals with heterogeneous data from IoT systems and 
produces interpretable and accurate multiclass threat predictions. The workflow starts with the acquisition of the 
TON_IoT and IoT -23 datasets, which contain complementary data relevant to IoT -enabled smart -farming 
environments comprising of telemetry, system-log and network-flow data (see Figure 3). The data is then passed 
through a standardized preparation phase before it is provided to the FarmSecureNet model proposed in the 
system. 
Pre-processing involves handling incomplete and duplicated records, encoding categorical variables, normalising 
numerical variables and dealing with inconsistent representation of features. Label harmonisation maps the attack 
categories of the two data sets to the same consistent multiclass taxonomy and feature alignment is then used to 
build a common input space between the two data sets. Finally the processed records are sorted into fixed -length 
temporal sequences to be learned effectively both local traffic characteristics and time -dependent attack 
behaviour. 
The extracted sequences are then utilized for training and evaluating FarmSecureNet, a hybrid of convolutional 
feature extraction, bidirectional temporal learning, and attention -based feature refinement. The accuracy, 
precision, recall, F1-score, confusion matrices and statistical significance analysis are used to evaluate the model 
predictions. To generate explanations using SHAP, the input features that make a prediction are identified. The 
end-to-end workflow evaluates the robustness of the system in the presence of noise and adversarial inputs and 
the efficiency of deployment on Raspberry Pi 4 and NVIDIA Jetson Nano platforms for use in real -world smart-
farming security systems with limited resources.

5.1 Experimental Setup 
To validate the proposed AgroCyberShield framework and its core detection model, FarmSecureNet, extensive 
experiments were conducted on the TON_IoT and IoT -23 datasets. The experimental evaluation is performed 
based on these three aspects: (i) intrusion detection performance, (ii) interpretability of the underlying model and 
(iii) practical feasibility for deployment of such a model into an IoT-based smart farming ecosystem. 
The experiments and large-scale evaluations were conducted on a workstation with the  following configuration: 
Intel Core i7–12700H CPU, 32 GB of RAM, and an NVIDIA RTX 3060 GPU (6 GB of VRAM). The framework

All datasets were split into 80:10:10 for training, validation, and test sets, respectively,  with stratification to 
maintain class distributions. Random shuffling was avoided to preserve certain temporal dependencies important 
for sequence modelling. In the case of the IoT-23 dataset, raw PCAP files were converted to flow-level CSV files

FarmSecureNet was trained for 100 epochs with a batch size of 64, a learning rate of 0.001, the Adam optimiser,

resource-constrained agricultural gateways running the Jetson Nano by ASIC.  
A total of 100 randomly selected instances from the test dataset were used to conduct explainability analyses with 
SHAP and LIME to evaluate the consistency of feature contributions and the relevance of decisions. All latency,

In conclusion, this experimental framework provides a diverse set of common intrusion scenarios applicable to

In this regard, multiple assessment metrics were utilised to assess the overall performance of the designed

classification,  resistance to class imbalance, readiness for online applications, and deployment ease, an important

5.6 Statistical Significance Analysis of Baseline Comparisons

improvements observed across different intrusion detection models are statistically significant . To further ensure 
that the performance improvement of the proposed FarmSecureNet model over baseline methods is real and not

FarmSecureNet with each baseline model using a paired two -tailed t-test at the 95% confidence level (α = 0.05).

Raspberry Pi 4 remains a feasible lower -cost deployment platform for smart -farming gateways with less 
demanding latency requirements. 
 
6. DISCUSSION 
New agricultural practices have continually incorporated IoT technologies, ushering in a smart  farming era in 
which real-time monitoring and parameter adjustment, as well as data processing and scientific data selection,  
will become feasible. This advancement has been tremendous for technological progress but has also increased 
the cyber-attack surface for cyber adversaries in the blockchain, especially in a decentralised, resource-constrained 
environment. Existing implementations of cybersecurity techniques in modern IoT networks typically offer 
limited scalability, real-time detection, explainability, and generalizability across different setups, including types 
of smart farming. 
We have conducted a systematic literature review that identified the key deficiencies of the current state -of-the-
art systems. Traditional intrusion detection methods r ely on static feature sets  or rules and lack adaptability to 
evolving threats. Likewise, today’s state -of-the-art models utilising deep learning techniques are still very 
restrictive, limited to addressing only one dataset or modality, and most often neith er support real-time operation 
nor are interpretable, which is necessary to guide actionability in agricultural defence  applications. Besides, the

ARTICLE IN PRESSARTICLE IN PRESS

existing literature lacks a comprehensive treatment of lightweight deployment issues and still targets high 
detection accuracy for mild-touch attacks. 
To overcome these shortcomings, this project presents the AgroCyberShield cybersecurity framework, providing 
a holistic solution for IoT-enabled intelligent farming networks. Our proposed hybrid deep learning model , 
FarmSecureNet, is the core of this system, combining a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), a bidirectional 
Long Short -Term Memory (BiLSTM), and context -aware attention to model spatio -temporal context from 
network traffic data. Moreover, a two -dataset fu sion strategy is utilised to enhance the model's generalisation  
capability. These decisions  reflect major methodological innovations aimed at filling  gaps in the existing 
literature. 
In an experimental evaluation of FarmSecureNet, the classification accura cy reached 96.4% using the Random 
Forest, SVM, CNN -only, and BiLSTM -only baseline models . The attention mechanism  and the dual -dataset 
design are also crucial to performance, as ablation studies confirmed. Deep insights into model behaviour can be 
achieved through explainability analyses, such as SHAP visualisations and attention weight maps, which provide 
transparency and confidence in real-world deployments. Edge deployment tests were also conducted on both the 
Raspberry Pi and the Jetson Nano, and the in tegrated system proved theoretically sound, achieving a balance 
between accuracy and efficiency. 
AgroCyberShield thus pushes the frontiers of cybersecurity into smart agriculture by addressing three critical gaps 
in detection accuracy, explainability, and deployment readiness. Our approach can lead to proactive, interpretable 
defence mechanisms, with the potential for integration into online  IoT settings. Section 6.1 discusses the study's 
limitations and future directions. 
6.1 Limitations of the Study 
The limitations of the present study need to be taken into account , even though  it showed good performance. 
Starting with widely used benchmark datasets to train and assess the proposed AgroCyberShield framework, while 
necessarily acknowledging limitations due to data-type adaptation, agri-technology noise, and adversarial factors 
not present in these benchmarks, particularly given the  focus of the existing literature on smart farming. 
Specifically, the system has not been evaluated in adversarial learning settings, meaning scenarios in which the 
training data itself is manipulated by malicious actors seeking to poison the dataset or create adversarial samples 
that help evade intrusion detection systems. 
Second, although we show the feasibility of edge deploymen t on representative resource - constrained devices, 
i.e., Raspberry Pi and Jetson Nano, the scalability of the framework on heterogeneous IoT hardware platforms 
with diverse computational, energy and networking constraints is not evaluated; the results from ultra-low-power 
sensors or extremely large-scale multi-gateway agricultural infrastructure may differ. 
Third, even though the dual -dataset fusion strategy enhanced generalisation and robustness on the interdomain 
detection task, it would not work for the unseen domain and would require further calibration/feature alignment, 
or possibly retraining on the target domain,  to resolve domain shift and mitigate model degradation. In addition, 
you need to be careful because the gradual shift is exposed to the mode l, and traffic changes over time, which 
could lead to gradual data contamination and make the model unreliable in the long term. Such constraints urge 
prudent deployment of these systems; further, they indicate the necessity of robustness analysis for deep learning–
based IDS in a dynamic, possibly adversarial IoT ecosystem. 
 
7. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE SCOPE 
The study proposed AgroCyberShield, an integrated deep learning –based cyber defence framework capable of 
securing IoT-enabled intelligent farming networks  against a variety of existing and forthcoming cyber threats. 
The proposed system used a dual -dataset fusion strategy while employing advanced engineering features within 
a proposed hybrid  FarmSecureNet architecture (that combines CNN, BiLSTM, and attentio n mechanisms) to 
effectively capture the spatial and temporal intrusion patterns in heterogeneous IoT environments. Through robust 
experiments, we show that AgroCyberShield achieves high classification performance, achieving an overall 
accuracy of 96.4%, w hile outperforming traditional machine -learning and standalone deep -learning baselines in 
all cases. Beyond predictive accuracy, this framework emphasises interpretability and practical deployment,

ARTICLE IN PRESSARTICLE IN PRESS

which are often overlooked in current innovative agricultu ral security solutions. SHAP-based explainability 
provided transparent, feature -level insights into model decisions, instilling  trust and usability for real -world 
operators. In addition, an empirical deployment assessment on resource-limited edge devices (namely, Raspberry 
Pi and Jetson Nano) reaffirmed the practical feasibility of low -latency inference and the framework's relatively 
low memory consumption, underscoring its suitability for edge-assisted agricultural settings. Finally, the ablation 
analysis confirmed the architectural design selection, demonstrating the critical role of attention mechanisms in 
suppressing noise and in data fusion across different sources to enhance  detection robustness and class 
discrimination. The other way around, AgroCyber Shield addresses some of the most noticeable limitations of 
current IoT intrusion detection systems, improving the aforementioned aspects of accuracy, explainability, and 
deployability in a  single framework. The results of this study highlight the viabilit y of hybrid deep learning 
methods for protecting intelligent farming infrastructure and provide a practical , interpretable,  and robust 
cybersecurity solution for today's innovative agrarian ecosystems. 
Although the AgroCyberShield framework has been evalua ted using established public datasets, structured data 
collection from real-world databases and implementation in operational, innovative farming environments will be 
the focus of future work. In the first phase, IoT sensing nodes, such as soil moisture an d temperature–humidity 
sensors, irrigation controllers,  and edge gateways, will be deployed in collaboration with pilot farms and 
agricultural research stations. We will set these devices to continuously log multimodal telemetry data and 
network traffic with the consent of the farmers and farming operators, in compliance with ethical and data privacy 
norms. 
Phase two is a controlled attack -simulation approach conducted in a sandboxed farm -network environment. 
Labelled intrusion data will be generated in a s afe, isolated environment by emulating common IoT -specific 
threats, including spoofing, data injection, denial -of-service (DoS), and botnet -based scanning. This allows for 
accurate threat modelling without affecting real agricultural processes. The telemet ry and traffic traces collected 
will be time -stamped, anonymised, and synchronised with the schema used in the data for the current study to 
comply with the FarmSecureNet training pipeline. 
Phase three will continue to be built on incremental and continuous learning. Periodic retraining and online fine-
tuning mechanisms will be used to integrate  real-world data streams and adapt the intrusion detection model to 
changing attack patterns and seasonal variations in farming operatio ns. Key aspects of this work will include 
investigating domain adaptation strategies to  address distributional shifts when moving from public benchmark 
datasets to field -collected data . Moreover, we will also be co -evaluating the viadomain experts’ explain ability 
outputs we generated using SHAP and attention mechanisms, focusing on  interpretability and trust in decision -
making in operational settings. 
Last but not least, we will conduct large -scale deployment studies to evaluate the system's long -term stability, 
scalability, and robustness under realistic network loads . Such experiments will include energy consumption 
analysis, edge-device resource exploitation, and resilience against adversarial behaviour, such as data  poisoning. 
The phased roadmap outlined  here establishes a comprehensive transition pathway from benchmark-oriented 
validation to practical deployment, reinforcing AgroCyberShield's applicability as a relevant cybersecurity 
solution within innovative IoT-based farming ecosystems.
