An optimized isolation forest based intrusion detection system 
for heterogeneous and streaming data in the industrial Internet 
of Things (IIoT) networks
Shaimaa Ahmed Elsaid1,2 · Adel Binbusayyis3,4
Received: 2 May 2024 / Accepted: 21 August 2024
© The Author(s) 2024  OPEN
Abstract
While conventional Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) are essential for defending against intruders in the Industrial 
Internet of Things (IIoT), handling data from heterogeneous and streaming data sources should receive more attention. 
This work introduces a novel Optimized IForest-based Intrusion Detection System (OIFIDS) which is designed to handle 
both heterogeneous and streaming data efficiently. The suggested approach employs a collection of optimized binary 
trees, each of which is trained on a distinctive subset of data, and in which the location of empty leaves determines 
the anomaly score assigned to a certain data point. Optimizing isolation Forest (iForest) utilizing a modified version of 
the Harris Hawks Optimization algorithm, which exploits both Exploration factor and Random walk strategies (ERHHO) 
decreases the dataset’s dimension, decreases its learning time, and enhances the detection precision, accuracy, F1-score, 
FPR, and recall. To demonstrate how effective the proposed approach is, it is evaluated using three datasets: CICIDS-2018, 
NSL-KDD, and UNSW-NB15. The experimental results prove the ability of the suggested approach in handling both het-
erogeneous and streaming data efficiently and delivering results that were comparable to the cutting-edge baseline 
techniques. Moreover, it performs effectively when there are no anomalies in the training sample and when dealing 
with challenging scenarios with several irrelevant features and high dimensions. Based on the comparison with various 
state-of-the-art IDSs, the suggested approach is able to detect intrusion with greater accuracies of 95.6%, 94.8%, and 
99% than the other approaches on the NSL-KDD, UNSW-NB15, and CICIDS-2018 datasets, respectively. Experiments on 
heterogenous data reveal that Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC) of OIFIDS beats the baseline approach for UNSW-NB15 
dataset and is higher than the second-best method by 8% and 2.4% for NSL-KDD and CICIDS-2018 respectively. Evaluat-
ing the proposed system on streaming data illustrates that it can address the concept drift problem well with high AUC 
value of 0.948, 0.97, and 0.922 on the NSL-KDD, CICIDS-2018, and UNSW-NB15 datasets, respectively.
Highlights
Multiple benchmarking datasets and extensive evaluations show that the proposed OIFIDS.
– Converges rapidly with a small number of trees; great detection accuracy and high efficiency can be attained with a 
small subsampling size.

1 Introduction
Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) is one of the most popular uses of the Internet of Things. which refers to a network of 
linked sensors, instruments, and other devices associated with industrial computer applications, such as energy man-
agement and manufacturing. Because of its interconnection, there is potential for increased productivity and efficiency 
as well as other financial gains through the gathering, sharing, and analysis of data [1 ]. By leveraging cloud computing 
to enhance and improve the process controls, the IIoT is an advancement of the distributed control system (DCS) that 
enables a greater level of automation. While the industrial sector has benefited greatly from the incorporation of IoT 
technologies, this connected critical environment lack adequate security and privacy protections. IIoT is found by cyber-
criminals to be a very attractive target. Its security market is expected to increase at a compound annual growth rate 
(CAGR) of 26.36% to reach $13.36 billion by 2028 from its estimated $3.35 billion in 2022 [1]. It is expected to be involved 
in over 25% of all cyberattacks against enterprises. By 2027, endpoint security is anticipated to be valued over $29 bil-
lion [2]. Researchers recommend employing Machine Learning (ML) and Deep Learning (DL) as an Intrusion Detection 
System (IDS) to detect intrusions in IIoT networks [3 ]. Nevertheless, there are unique difficulties associated with using 
ML and DL in IDS for this type of networks. These challenges are summarized in handling heterogenous and streaming 
data, and the capability of generalizing the IDS to deal efficiently cross different heterogeneous networks. The following 
paragraph highlights the effect of these challenges on detecting intrusions in IIoT networks.
First, Managing the varied data gathered by various devices presents a challenge. There may be variations in the dis-
tribution of data, quantity of samples, and duration of collecting for every edge device. In addition, the feature sizes and 
attack types of data gathered from various devices/clients may also differ as a result of system updates or different device 
activities. Designing ML/DL based IDS models for IIoT security detection are made more difficult by this heterogeneity 
[4]. Second, the classification of the learning process can also be based on either the detection method uses a flowing 
dataset or a static dataset for learning. Traditionally, learning from static datasets has been the method used for machine 
learning algorithms and anomaly detection approaches [5 ]. Before performing actual detections, it is expected that all 
relevant information may be gathered from the static training dataset. It implies that the dataset needs to be large and 
easily available. If the entire training dataset is not currently available, then the performance of this approach degrades.

In actuality, there are numerous use cases such as those in the medical, IT security, and agricultural fields where there’s 
initially no or little data, but that extra data gradually accumulates over time (streaming data). When fresh data becomes 
available, the detection algorithms should be able to update their understanding and make detections as soon as pos-
sible with the lowest amount of initial information possible. Online or progressive learning are two names for this tech-
nique. The majority of these streaming data have the following features: high speed, nonstationary data distribution or 
Concept Drift (CD), and infinite duration. Numerous research work has demonstrated that using ML/DL techniques may 
result to encouraging outcomes in the IDS sector. However, by relying on static data sources, many of these studies have 
failed to take into account the dynamic nature of the technology. Third, the capability for cross-contextual generalization 
of the designed IDS. Stated differently, when applied to different networks, IDSs designed and trained using particular 
network data typically yield subpar results.
Despite the fact that there are several techniques that can detect anomalies in network traffic, Isolation Forest (iFor -
est) [6–8] is clearly superior to other algorithms specially for handling heterogenous and streaming due to its low time 
complexity and quantitative depiction of abnormalities and its capability of detecting both clustered and distributed 
anomalies without "correcting" the fundamental measure and identifying anomalies encircled by regular points in addi-
tion to outlying scattered points. Following are the characteristics of isolation forest:
• It uses subsampling to a degree that is not possible with other techniques.
• It does not detect problems by measuring density or distance. A considerable processing cost related with distance 
calculation has been removed.
• It can handle high dimensional challenges involving many irrelevant attributes and extremely large datasets.
• It has constant training time as well as low memory requirement.
Nevertheless, iForest struggles to identify local anomalous spots, which reduces algorithm accuracy. As a result, an 
optimized version of iForest is proposed in this work based on ERHHO optimization algorithm to overcome its shortage 
by reducing the features set and hence decreasing the training and testing time and making it possible to identify the 
best split points that correlate to the attributes that are most easily separable. The following subsections highlight the 
motivations and contribution of this work.
1.1  Motivation
Several of the existing IDSs are limited to static and relatively low dimensional datasets due to the inherent properties 
of their original techniques. They frequently underperform as a result, leading to either too few or too many false alarms 
(in which normal traffic is mistakenly detected as attack). The nature of heterogeneous networks and streaming data 
affects the detection process as follows:
– Real-world datasets contain heterogeneous data including several types of variables, categorical and continuous 
attributes. It is extremely challenging to compare different data instances because of data heterogeneity. Throughout 
categorization tasks, the suggested system continuously updates its backend data to adjust to varying data behaviors.
– In streaming environments, data behaviors may evolve over time and this may cause degradation in the IDS perfor -
mance. The proposed system employs concept drift technique to overcome drifting of streaming data.
– Receiving too many data each day for classification. The suggested technique utilizes ERHHO to decrease the data 
dimensionality and hence reduces the training and testing time

2  Related work
Network threats have increased as a result of the rapid growth of IIoT systems, which may negatively affect how well 
they function. IDSs are a reliable way to safeguard IIoT devices, and a lot of research has been done on them. In recent 
years, heterogenous and streaming data classification has emerged as a crucial area of study. High speed, nonstationary 
data distribution, and unlimited duration are the features of these kinds of data. Furthermore, scholars are now forced 
to focus on addressing heterogenous and streaming data categorization, including its challenges [11], due to the quick 
growth of current technologies like the Internet of Things (IoT) [9 ] and Artificial Intelligence (AI) [10]. The majority of 
research on anomaly identification in streaming environments has been limited to numerical data because it has been 
done in the field of signal processing [12–14]. In an attempt to address the issues of concept drift and memory demand, 
Tan et al. presented Streaming Half-Space Trees [15], although, like previous conventional approaches, the study only 
focused on numerical data. Replicator Neural Networks (RNNs), which are theoretically similar to autoencoders, were 
proposed by Hawkins et al. for one-class anomaly detection [16]. The RNNs attempt to replicate and learn input patterns. 
An anomaly score is calculated as the reconstruction error, which is obtained by deducting the recovered output from 
an input instance. Utilizing deep architectures, some recent publications [17– 19] demonstrate that deep architectures 
can be applied to unsupervised anomaly detection.
The effectiveness of the DL-based model has been evaluated in [20] using two datasets: NSLKDD and UNSW-NB15. 
The results for the DL-based technique are as follows: for the NSL-KDD dataset, the results are 99.0% accuracy rate, 
99.0% detection rate, and 1.0% false positive rate (FPR); in contrast, the UNSW-NB15 dataset shows 98.9% accuracy rate, 
99.9% detection rate, and 1.1% FPR. However, by employing mixed rule-based characteristic gathering, the suggested 
method achieves higher consistency by using only significant features for class classification in the data sets. Further -
more, hybrid neural networks (HDRaNN) have been used to identify attacks in the IIoT network [21]. Two prominent IIOT 
security datasets, UNSW-NB15 and DS2OS, are used to assess the suggested technique. Nevertheless, there are a lot of 
security and privacy issues when combining physical and digital systems. The proposed model exhibits improved con-
sistency due to the hybrid rule-based feature collecting technique that utilizes a restricted amount of suitable features 
for class categorization inside the data sets. This model does not provide equal support for every feature. The HDRaNN 
is a hybrid model that uses dropout regularization to combine the advantages of a multilayer perceptron and a deep 
random neural network.
In [22], a model for the adaptive light gradient boosting machine (light GBM) is presented for the analysis of Internet of 
Things data. This model can adapt to idea drift and identify attacks with high accuracy. It is evident that locating concept 
drift and updating the present model in accordance with the drift’s data stream is an efficient way to address the issue 
of concept drift in IoT security challenges. By comparing the current Domain Generation Algorithm (DGA) standards 
with the IoT intrusion detection system, the authors of [23] proposed leveraging deep learning and Deep Belief Net -
work (DBN) to improve the system. In [24], a DL-based model utilizing sparse evolutionary training (SET) was created 
for the analysis and identification of prevalent threat types. The mean accuracy of the SET-based prediction model is 
99%, and its average testing time is 2.29 ms. It has been found that the expected model will improve accuracy by 6.25% 
on average. While the suggested method in this study shows good overall detection performance and efficiency, it is 
crucial to recognize some limits. First and foremost, it is crucial to remember that during training, the innovative sparse 
evolutionary training (SET) based prediction model might not fully restore the old model’s ideal detection state. It also

A novel intrusion detection system called the Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory based Explainable Artificial Intel-
ligence framework (BiLSTMXAI) was created in [30] to find intrusions in heterogeneous IIoT networks. To improve the data

Krill herd optimization (KHO) technique is then used to choose the noteworthy features from the databases. The authors

of category hyperparameters and the hyperparameter value for improved detection performance. In order to identify 
intrusions in streaming data, the authors of [33] introduced an accuracy boosting model (ABM) that combines random 
forest machine learning models and severe gradient boosting. To precisely identify IoT devices including lights, smoke 
detectors, thermostats, motion sensors, baby monitors, outlets, TVs, security cameras, and watches, cross-validation and 
engineering approaches are utilized. Extreme gradient boosting (XGB) and random forest (RF) are used as base learners 
with adaptive boosting in the suggested ensemble model. The exam results show improved 91% accuracy, 93% precision, 
93% recall, and 93% F1 score. A summary of the contributions made by the current approaches and their performance

3  Methodology
Devices in IIoT networks have a high vulnerability to network intrusions because of the open nature of the deployment 
environment of these types of networks and the poor security control protocol operation ability brought on by con-
strained computing capabilities. This section describes the methodology used in this study to assess how well the propose 
OIFIDS approach generalizes across diverse networks and handles the heterogenous and streaming data. It starts with a 
brief description of three datasets that depict various networks: known as heterogeneous networks in our context. Next, 
a detailed elaboration of how the suggested approach handles the heterogenous and streaming data and enhances the 
generalization among those heterogeneous networks by an experimental method.
3.1  Datasets
Below is a quick explanation of the datasets NSL-KDD, CICIDS-2018, and UNSW-NB15 that were utilized in this work to 
assess OIFIDS performance. Table 2 compares the three datasets.
3.1.1  NSL‑KDD dataset
KDD99, a popular dataset for cyber security research, was created in 1999 [34]. The KDD99 method has weaknesses 
that need be addressed, such as overt repetition and unnecessarily high record counts in the test and train datasets, 
which make them difficult to manage. To overcome the previously identified shortcomings, NSL-KDD [35] presented a 
more modern adaptation. Since 2009, the NSL-KDD dataset has been recognized as the new gold standard for cyber 
security research. 22,544 entries from KDDTest + are in the testing dataset, while 125,973 records from KDDTrain + are 
in the training dataset. The 41 features in each record are categorized into four main feature groups: content features, 
connection-based traffic features, basic features, and time-based traffic features.
3.1.2  UNSW‑NB15 dataset
A dataset that accurately represents intrusions that happen in actual terrestrial network environments is represented 
by the UNSW-NB15 dataset [36]. The two main csv files in this dataset are the training set and testing set files. With 45 
features, it has labels for normal (56,000 records) and nine different types of attacks: worms (130 records), backdoors 
(1,746), exploits (33,393 records), fuzzers (18,184 records), generic (40,000 records), reconnaissance (10,491 records), DoS 
(12,264 records), shellcode (1,133 records), and analysis (2000 records).

3.1.3  CICIDS 2018 dataset
With an emphasis on network-based anomaly detectors, the Canadian team developed a systematic way for gathering 
data in order to assess, test, and analyze intrusion detection systems. The CICIDS 2018 dataset [37] has one category 
of normal traffic labeled as 0 and seven categories of anomalous attack traffic labeled as 1: brute-force, internal net -
work intrusion, heartbleed, botnet, DoS, DDoS, and web attacks, are all examples of cyber-attacks. The data includes 
a target machine with 420 computers and 30 servers, an attack machine with 50 hosts, and 5 target machine sectors. 
For each machine that was acquired, the dataset includes system logs and network traffic, together with 79 attributes 
that were extracted from the traffic. About 16,000,000 instances were gathered for ten days. There is a class imbal-
ance in this multi-class dataset, with attack (anomalous) traffic making up about 17% of the occurrences. A subset 
of 3,836,730 of 16,000,000 records were utilized in Experiment 1.
3.2  Proposed system
Intrusion Detection System (IDS) [40– 46] is a sophisticated tool that may assist enterprises in identifying illegal net -
work access. It can detect suspicious activity and inform the system administrator by analyzing the network traffic. 
Researchers use machine learning techniques to propose various IDSs to detect intrusions in IIoT networks. Nonethe -
less, IDSs continue to face difficulties due to the vast volume of IIoT data that contains duplicate and irrelevant infor -
mation which increases the algorithm’s computational and space complexity as well as limiting the system’s ability to 
detect abnormal network traffic besides the challenges of dealing with heterogenous and streaming data. In order to 
choose the pertinent features and attain improved attack detection performance, this study suggests an efficient IDS 
based on both iForest, which detects anomalies using the idea of isolation rather than distance or density metrics, and 
the ERHHO optimization algorithm which deleting irrelevant features and reduces the data dimensionality and hence 
overcomes the shortage of iForest. The proposed approach uses subsampling as a technique to effectively handle 
swamping and masking effects in addition to achieving low linear time complexity and little memory consumption 
which makes it a proper technique for dealing with large scale datasets and handling heterogenous and streaming 
data. When dealing with streaming data in IIoT systems, the feature ranges and class distributions are all dynamic 
factors that could alter dramatically over time. In online data stream analytics, data pre-processing should therefore 
be dynamic processes that require constant updating upon drift detection, as opposed to the static process found 
in typical ML based IDSs. So, the proposed OIFIDS system utilizes a concept drift detector to effectively detect and 
adapt drift. The characteristics of the proposed optimized iForest IDS (OIFIDS) are:
1. The new model can quickly find the optimal split point for a given property by exploiting the ERHHO heuristic search-
ing method.
2. The well-balanced iTree produced by the recommended method differs greatly from the extremely imbalanced 
isolation tree produced by the conventional iForest.
3. Compared to iForest, the new model builds the forest with fewer isolation trees.

3.2.1  Preprocessing step
- Eliminate records that contain "Infinity, " "NaN, " or missing data and convert time stamps to Unix epoch numeric values 
( seconds from January 1, 1970).
- Remove any column from the dataset that contains more than 50 percent of the dataset’s missing values.
- Normalize the dataset to improve convergence accuracy and speed while reducing the effect of the data dimension.
- Since target encoding [47] performs well with isolation forest, it is chosen to handle categorical features. While reduc-
ing the feature space, it may effectively extract the useful information from categorical data. Target encoding replaces 
each categorical value with the target variable’s mean.
- Split the considered dataset into two random subsets with the 80/20 rule; training subset and testing subset.

3.3  Classifying the optimal dataset using iForest (IF)
As shown in Fig.  3, after processing the dataset with ERHHO to reduce its dimensionality by selecting the most 
relevant attributes, the resulted dataset is sampled and used to build multiple iTrees (Algorithm 3) which form the 
iForest to find abnormalities. The normal vector and the intercept point are the only two random pieces of data that 
must be determined for the IF algorithm at the branching nodes. Preparing the sampled dataset, creating iTrees, and 
identifying anomalies by figuring out each node’s path length are the next steps.
1. Define the max. no. of trees (t ), choose Ψ  (subsampling size) data points at random from the training dataset, 
and add them to the sampled training subset X* ( Algorithm 2).
2. From the training dataset X* that was sampled, construct an isolation tree (Algorithm 3) as follows:
 i. Choose an attribute at random from the list of available attributes.
 ii. At random, choose the attribute’s split point (the attribute’s value).
 iii.  Split X* into the two subsets Xleft and Xright by comparing the related features of the data points with the split point 
(p).
 iv. The recursive technique ends when the set can no longer be split or when the height of the recursion tree reaches 
 log2(Ψ ): average predicted tree height. This restriction is justified by the isolation tree’s disinterest in data points 
with heights above the average.
 v. Follow the same procedure for X left and X right. The choice of the split point is now limited by the position in the 
recursion tree.
 vi. Use this method again, sample X for t iterations, to obtain t isolation trees.

3.3.1  Handling concept drift
In the suggested OIFIDS system, concept drift is detected by the combined use of ADWIN and EDDM [48]. Concept drift was 
detected by EDDM by tracking changes in the model’s performance, while ADWIN found drift by comparing the means of 
the two successive data frames. In the event of concept drift, the learning models are updated based on the new concept 
samples within a time window by the dynamic ERHHO features selection algorithm; the value of ERHHO step length r2 and 
r3 will be modified and hence other set of features are selected to overcome the concept drift.

5.2  Experiment 2: evaluation on heterogeneous data
Different IoT devices typically collect heterogeneous data, which makes training IDS models more difficult. To address the 
heterogeneity issues among different clients, OIFIDS is suggested in this study and evaluated on three different datasets 
from heterogenous networks. Area Under the Precision Recall (AUC-PR) score is used to test the overall performance of 
the proposed approach. Table 6 displays the experiment’s results of AUC values; the detection performance of the model 
is improved when the AUC value increases. When categorical features are included in the dataset, the classic iForest’s 
performance declines, but the proposed OIFIDS performs better as expected. It consistently beats the baseline approach 
on the UNSW-NB15 dataset. However, the OIFIDS performance on the dataset with only numerical features performs 
somewhat higher than with all features. The experiment’s results support our prediction that it can handle heterogeneous 
data. Furthermore, the experimental results show that the proposed OIFIDS consistently outperforms other IDSs with 
the highest average AUC. OIFIDS is higher than the second-best method by 8% and 2.4% for NSL-KDD and CICIDS-2018 
respectively. Table 6 demonstrates OIFIDS as the most trustworthy technique, with the best performance across most 
datasets. The average AUC-ROC score for OIFIDS is the highest of all the examined IDSs.
5.3  Experiment 3: Evaluation on streaming data
The numerical attributes in the training data were standardized as a part of the preparation of the data. The mean and 
standard deviation of the training data were used to normalize a streaming data incoming instance. One-hot encoding, 
which turns each category into a vector corresponding to its categorical value, is used to handle categorical attributes. 
The changing data behaviors were simulated in order to assess how well the proposed system can address the concept 
drift problem. It was first trained on data just using the SMTP protocol, and then tested on data using SMTP protocol 
followed by HTTP protocol. Concept drift to happen as the dataset’s network protocol changes. The experiment results 
are displayed in Table 7 with AUC values presented as the assessment metric. Surprisingly, the proposed OIFIDS works 
admirably without upgrading on the dataset for the NSL-KDD. The NSL-KDD dataset’s data distributions were unaffected 
by the protocol change. When compared to the other IDSs, the proposed one performs the best. It performs incredibly 
well on the UNSW-NB15 dataset compared to other IDSs. The results demonstrate that the OIFIDS could adjust to chang-
ing data distributions while the other models, which were initially created to detect abnormalities in static datasets, are 
unable to deal with idea drift. iForest performs badly across the majority of datasets and has the lowest average AUC 
score. The AUC values demonstrate the robustness with which the proposed OIFIDS outperforms the competition.

5.4  Calculating computational complexity 
5.4.1  Time complexity of iForest:
To build an iTree with a depth of  log2(N), if the training dataset consists of k features and N instances (samples), the neces-
sary computations are kN ×  (log2(N) +  log2(N/2) + ⋅ ⋅ ⋅ + 1). The time complexity is therefore O(kN[log2(N)]2).
5.4.2  Time complexity of ERHHO:
The total computing complexity of the ERHHO algorithm is O(2  × k × N × T). It consists of three essential components: 
Initialization, position updating, and fitness evaluation.
• The initialization phase’s computational complexity is O(k × N), where k stands for the number of populations and N 
for the issue dimensions.
• It takes O(k) to perform fitness evaluation.
• The computing complexity of a random walk method to avoid entering local optimum regions is O(2 × k × N × T).
5.5  The OIFIDS’s generalizability
One of the challenges facing most ML/DL-based IDSs is their inability to generalize across multiple settings. Put differently, 
systems which are trained using particular network data typically don’t work well on other networks. The consequence 
of the suggested OIFIDS is a workable strategy for generalization over heterogenous networks. To prove its superiority, 
it was evaluated using the NSL-KDD, UNSW-NB15, and CICIDS-2018 datasets. These datasets provide a large variety of 
factors that mimic both real-world daily routines and simulated attack behaviors. The generalizability of the suggested 
OIFIDS method was clearly demonstrated by looking over and analyzing each dataset separately. It is easier to evaluate 
the generalizability of the detection model when there are 14 additional attack types in the NSL-KDD test set compared 
to the training set’s 24; the test set has 38 attack types. Moreover, the proposed system was able to handle many datasets 
efficiently. This demonstrates its ability of handling heterogeneous data.
6  Conclusion and future work
Threats posing a risk to Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) systems could compromise their security, disrupt their regular 
operations, or damage critical infrastructure. Dealing with heterogeneous and flowing data in IIoT networks is necessary 
for developing effective cybersecurity strategies. When it comes to detecting anomalies in heterogeneous and stream-
ing data, iForest outperforms other techniques. Isolation forest is based mostly on randomization, it is unclear how to 
select the best feature and where it’s possible to find the optimal split point on an attribute especially while develop -
ing the isolation tree. This may degrade its detection performance. Therefore, optimizing iForest makes it possible to 
identify the best split points that correlate to the attributes that are most easily separable and hence enhances the 
detection precision, accuracy, F1-score, FPR, and recall. In order to lessen the detrimental effects of data heterogeneity 
on various devices, such as poor model prediction ability and sluggish model convergence speed, this study presents 
an efficient Optimized iForest based Intrusion Detection System (OIFIDS) to overcome the effect of iForest randomiza-
tion and enhances the detection performance in heterogenous and streaming data. Although neural networks are 
more resistant to the impacts of redundant (non-informative) features than is iForest, the performance gap between 
them can be closed by employing the ERHHO optimization algorithm. According to the experimental results, OIFIDS’s 
detection accuracy converges quickly with a small number of trees; great detection performance and high efficiency can 
be attained with just a tiny subsampling size. The proposed method regularly outperforms state-of-the-art detection 
approaches, including deep learning-based ones, as demonstrated by rigorous evaluations and many benchmarking 
datasets. Furthermore, the OIFIDS finds odd patterns in the data better than deep learning models. It can also adjust 
to changing data behaviors (concept drift). The proposed system is able to handle both heterogeneous and streaming 
data on the considered datasets and delivers results that were comparable to the cutting-edge baseline technique in

7  The key features of the proposed OIFIDS are listed below in brief:
– detects anomalies without the need to compute density or distance. In all distance-based and density-based tech-
niques, this significantly reduces the processing overhead.
– requires little memory and has a constant linear time complexity.
– Has the ability to handle exceptionally big datasets and high dimensional problems with irrelevant features.
– Continuously learns without retaining all previous data points and detections.
– Automatically operates without the requirement for manual parameter adjustment.
– Incorporates earlier data point information with the present detection.
– Lowers the overall number of false positives and false negatives.
– Reduces the time required to do the detection.
Although IForest algorithm is clearly superior to other algorithms due to its low time complexity, optimizing it with 
ERHHO algorithm may slightly increase the computational time. In addition to analyzing the proposed method in a cross-
device context and considering the combination of zero-trust architecture and distributed intrusion detection system 
implementation, our future study will concentrate on how to circumvent the issue of high search computation time.
