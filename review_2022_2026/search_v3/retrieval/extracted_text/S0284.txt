ABSTRACT 
The rapid expansion of Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS)  and Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) 
environments has increased exposure to sophisticate d cyber-attacks, necessitating resilient intrusion 
detection systems. Existing ML and DL approaches achieve high accuracy but lack adaptability, resilience, 
and continuous operation under dynamic conditions s uch as concept drift and network disruptions. To 
address this gap, this work proposes Resilient and Continuous Adaptive Intrusion Detection (RC-ADAPT), 
a robust IDS framework designed for accurate detect ion and uninterrupted operation. The model 
integrates a CNN–LSTM-based feature extractor to ca pture spatial–temporal patterns and an Adaptive 
Gradient Boosting Machine (AGBM) classifier for han dling imbalance and evolving attacks. A resilience 
evaluation mechanism is introduced to monitor syste m performance using accuracy, stability, and 
confidence, enabling adaptive responses such as fal lback activation and model updates. Evaluated on th e 
Edge-IIoTSet dataset, RC-ADAPT achieves 99.9992% accuracy for binary classification and 99.999% for 
multi-class classification. The proposed framework ensures high accuracy, adaptability, and reliability for 
CPS security. 
Keywords-cyber-physical systems; IoT security; deep learning; CNN–BiLSTM; XGBoost; drift detection 
I.  INTRODUCTION  
The rapid evolution of CPS and the proliferation of  IIoT 
technologies have significantly transformed modern 
infrastructures, enabling intelligent applications in smart 
manufacturing, healthcare, transportation, and crit ical 
infrastructure management [1]. However, the increas ing 
interconnectivity among heterogeneous devices, networks, and 
control systems has also expanded the attack surface, exposing 
CPS environments to a wide range of sophisticated c yber 
threats [2, 3]. Traditional signature-based IDS [4]  are 
inadequate in such dynamic environments due to their inability 
to detect unknown or evolving attacks, thereby moti vating the 
adoption of AI, ML, and DL techniques for securing CPS. 
Recent advances in AI-driven IDS have demonstrated 
promising results, where ML-based approaches such a s RF, 
SVM, and GB have been widely used for network intru sion 
detection [5-7]. In parallel, DL models including C NN, RNN, 
LSTM, and AE have shown superior capability in lear ning 
complex feature representations from high-dimension al IoT 
traffic data [8-10].  
Despite these improvements, several critical challe nges 
persist. Existing IDS models often struggle with sc alability in 
large-scale CPS environments, exhibit limited adapt ability to 
concept drift caused by evolving attack patterns, and are prone 
to performance degradation due to imbalanced data 
distributions. Moreover, most approaches primarily focus on

II.  METHODOLOGY 
A.  Architecture 
The RC-ADAPT architecture, illustrated in Figure 1,  was 
designed as a resilient and adaptive intrusion dete ction 
framework that integrates deep feature learning, ad aptive 
classification, and dynamic resilience management f or CPS 
environments. The process begins with the Edge-IIoT Set 
dataset, which contains diverse IIoT traffic patter ns 
representing both normal and malicious activities. The dataset 
is first partitioned into training and testing subs ets to ensure 
proper model generalization and unbiased performanc e 
evaluation. Following data preparation, the trainin g data is 
processed through a CNN–LSTM-based feature extracti on 
module, which effectively captures both spatial and  temporal 
characteristics of CPS data. The convolutional laye rs extract 
local feature correlations, while the LSTM componen t models 
sequential dependencies inherent in IIoT traffic fl ows. This 
hybrid representation enables the system to learn c omplex 
behavioral patterns associated with cyber-attacks. The 
extracted feature representations are subsequently passed to the 
AGBM classifier, which performs robust classificati on of 
normal and attack instances. AGBM enhances detectio n 
capability by handling non-linear relationships and  addressing 
class imbalance through adaptive learning strategies.

E.  Resilient CPS Architecture 
Unlike conventional IDS models, RC-ADAPT introduces a 
resilience-aware CPS architecture that ensures cont inuous 
operation under adverse conditions [16]. The proposed resilient 
CPS architecture is organized into three collaborat ive layers, 
each designed to ensure continuous operation, adaptability, and 
fault tolerance under dynamic and adverse condition s. At the 
edge layer, local resilience is achieved by enablin g real-time 
intrusion detection directly at IoT or IIoT devices . This layer 
performs lightweight inference using the trained mo del, 
ensuring low-latency decision-making without relian ce on 
centralised infrastructure. To handle unexpected fa ilures or 
degraded model performance, the edge nodes maintain  locally 
stored fallback models, which can be immediately ac tivated to 
ensure uninterrupted operation. This decentralized capability 
allows the system to sustain detection functionality even during 
network disruptions or cloud unavailability. The ad aptive 
control layer acts as an intelligent supervisory mo dule that 
continuously monitors system health, prediction con fidence, 
and model performance metrics. It evaluates variati ons in 
detection accuracy, confidence scores, and data dis tribution to 
identify potential anomalies or performance degradation. When 
such deviations exceed predefined thresholds, the layer triggers 
adaptive mechanisms such as partial retraining, par ameter 
recalibration, or switching to fallback models. Thi s dynamic 
control ensures that the system remains robust agai nst concept 
drift, evolving attack patterns, and environmental uncertainties.  
The cloud coordination layer provides global intell igence 
and long-term optimization. It aggregates data and learning 
updates from distributed edge nodes to perform comprehensive 
model retraining and refinement. This layer maintains multiple 
redundant and backup models to enhance system reliability and 
fault tolerance. Additionally, it distributes updated models back 
to edge devices, ensuring synchronization and conti nuous 
improvement across the entire CPS ecosystem. By int egrating 
distributed learning with centralized coordination,  this layer 
strengthens the scalability, adaptability, and resi lience of the 
overall system.  
Together, these three layers form a cohesive archit ecture 
that balances real-time responsiveness, adaptive in telligence, 
and global optimization, thereby ensuring reliable and 
continuous CPS operation even in the presence of cyber threats 
and adverse network conditions.

Initially, the proposed framework begins by initializing the 
core components of the system, namely the CPS-CNN–L STM 
feature extraction module and the AGBM classifier. These 
components together form the backbone of the IDS. In the next 
step, the feature extraction model is trained using the available 
training dataset. This stage enables the system to learn 
meaningful spatial and temporal patterns from the i nput data, 
transforming raw observations into informative feat ure 
representations. Following this, the classifier is trained using a 
weighted loss strategy, which helps in handling class imbalance 
and improves detection performance across different  attack 
categories. Once training is completed, the system enters the 
operational phase, where it processes incoming data samples in 
real time. For each new input sample, the trained f eature 
extractor generates a compact feature representatio n, which is 
then passed to the classifier to obtain a predictio n probability 
indicating the likelihood of an attack. After obtai ning the 
prediction, the system evaluates a resilience score  that reflects 
the current reliability of the model. This score is  computed by 
combining multiple factors such as prediction accuracy trends, 
system stability, and confidence levels. The resili ence score 
serves as an indicator of how well the model is per forming 
under current conditions. The system then compares this 
resilience score with a predefined threshold. If th e score falls 
below the threshold, it indicates potential degrada tion in 
performance or the presence of concept drift. In such cases, the 
system activates a fallback model to ensure uninter rupted 
detection and simultaneously updates or adapts the primary 
model using recent data. If the resilience score re mains above 
the threshold, the system continues normal operatio n without

B.  Binary Classification 
RC-ADAPT demonstrated exceptional performance in 
binary-class intrusion detection on the Edge-IIoTSe t dataset. 
As shown in Figure 2, RC-ADAPT achieved an overall 
accuracy of 99.9992%, indicating that nearly all in stances of

balanced performance of RC-ADAPT in handling both FPs and 
FNs.

E.  Discussion 
The proposed RC-ADAPT framework demonstrates several 
important advantages for intrusion detection in CPS . By 
combining CNN–LSTM-based feature learning with AGBM  
and resilience-aware monitoring, the framework achi eves 
highly accurate detection in both binary and multi- class attack 
scenarios. The resilience scoring mechanism continu ously 
evaluates system performance and helps maintain sta ble 
operation during failures, network fluctuations, or  adversarial 
conditions. In addition, the inclusion of fallback edge models 
ensures uninterrupted intrusion detection even when  the 
primary model experiences degradation. The adaptive  
retraining strategy enables the framework to respon d 
effectively to evolving attack behaviors while reducing the cost 
of complete retraining. RC-ADAPT also improves mino rity 
attack detection through class-sensitive weighting,  thereby 
lowering false negatives. Its lightweight edge infe rence 
supports real-time CPS applications with low latency, while the 
multi-layer architecture enhances scalability across distributed 
environments. Despite these strengths, the framewor k 
introduces higher computational complexity because of the 
integration of DL, adaptive boosting, and resilience evaluation 
modules. This increases processing time and resourc e 
consumption, particularly in constrained CPS device s. 
Therefore, future work will focus on reducing compu tational 
overhead and improving real-time efficiency without  
compromising detection reliability. 
IV.  CONCLUSION 
This work presented RC-ADAPT, a resilient and adapt ive 
intrusion detection approach designed for CPS and I IoT 
environments. RC-ADAPT integrates a CNN–LSTM-based 
feature extraction module with an AGBM classifier t o 
effectively capture both spatial and temporal patte rns in 
network traffic and accurately detect cyber-attacks. To address 
key challenges such as concept drift, class imbalan ce, and 
dynamic attack behavior, RC-ADAPT incorporates adap tive 
learning strategies and class-sensitive weighting. A major 
contribution of this work is the introduction of a resilience-
driven CPS architecture that continuously monitors system 
performance through a composite resilience score an d ensures 
uninterrupted operation by triggering fallback mechanisms and 
model updates when needed.  
RC-ADAPT was evaluated on the Edge-IIoTSet dataset [1], 
demonstrating superior performance in both binary a nd multi-
class classification. Overall, RC-ADAPT provides a robust, 
scalable, and reliable IDS solution capable of maintaining high 
detection accuracy and continuous operation in dyna mic and 
adversarial CPS environments. The experimental eval uation 
demonstrated that RC-ADAPT achieved 99.9992% binary -
class accuracy and 99.999% multi-class accuracy on the Edge-
IIoTSet dataset, outperforming several recent ML- a nd DL-
based intrusion detection approaches. The proposed resilience-
aware architecture further enables continuous opera tion 
through adaptive retraining and fallback mechanisms  under 
dynamic network conditions.  
Future work will focus on reducing computational overhead 
and validating the framework in real-world distribu ted CPS 
deployments. In addition, the proposed framework wi ll be 
extended to support federated and edge-intelligent learning for 
privacy-preserving intrusion detection across geogr aphically 
distributed CPS environments. Further research will  also 
investigate lightweight model compression and explainable AI 
techniques to improve deployment efficiency, transp arency, 
and trustworthiness in resource-constrained IIoT devices.
