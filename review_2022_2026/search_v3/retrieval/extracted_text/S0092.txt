ABSTRACT Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) serve as critical components in safeguarding 
network security by detecting malicious activities. Although IDS has recently been treated primarily 
through the lens of machine learning, challenges persist, particularly with high-dimensional data and 
feature drift. Feature drift pertains to the dynamic nature of feature significance, which can fluctuate 
over time, complicating the task of stable and effective intrusion detection. The existing  Genetic 
Programming (GP)-combiner based ensemble classifier framework demonstrates notable efficiency 
in online intrusion detection, especially in accommodating concept drift. However, it does not 
adequately address the specific type of concept drift known as feature drift. To rectify this gap, this 
article proposes a refined version of GP -combiner, named Dynamic Feature Aware GP Ensemble 
(DFA-GPE). This advanced framework incorporates an improved variant of Variable Length Multi-
Objective Particle Swarm Optimization (VLMO -PSO) to dynamically manage feature drift. The 
proposed VLMO -PSO employs a  smart population initialization strategy based on Bernoulli 
distribution and symmetric uncertainty. It also utilizes a unique set of transfer functions that map the 
mobility equation outcomes to the decision space. To further optimize the process, the framework 
introduces a novel exemplar selection method, striking a balance between exploration and 
exploitation. DFA-GPE's final feature selection decisions are informed by statistical analyses of 
feature weights, effectively addressing the challenge of dynamic feature selection as a multi -
objective optimization problem that simultaneously enhances accuracy and conserves memory. 
Comprehensive evaluation of DFA -GPE on two benchmark datasets, namely HIKARI 2021 and 
TON_IoT 2020, reveals its robust performance  across all metrics . From experiment results, our 
framework attains 99.09% and 92.64% accuracy on both datasets, respectively, while simultaneously 
reducing memory consumption. Hence, DFA-GPE emerges as a comprehensive framework adept at 
tackling the most pertinent issues related to stream data classification within IDS, notably 
outperforming existing methodologies.

networks[1]. This rise has been accompanied by a surge in the 
number of devices integrated into society, advancing intelligent 
and telemetry management. Consequently, this has accelerated 
the trend of digitalization by allowing physical objects to

detection of adversarial threats. It can monitor suspicious 
activities and protect sensitive information by monitoring data 
flow, alerting administrators to any warnings or threats against 
the system [5]. In the literature, IDS is often approached as a 
machine/deep learning problem where a classification model is 
trained to identify attacks based on the current state of the 
network, as captured by a predefined feature space  [6].  
However, online models are favored for managing IDS due to 
their ability to continuously update knowledge, adapting to the 
dynamic nature of data streams.  
ML-based IDS, when viewed as a data -streaming analysis 
tool, confronts many challenges. The extensive diversity of 
connected devices leads to a substantial increase in generating 
data, characterized as 'high dimensionality' . This surge 
manifests as non-stationary data traffic, refuting the traditional 
assumption of stationary and equally distributed data [7]. 
Furthermore, when deployed in real -world scenarios, IDS 
contends with the added complexity of data heterogeneity . 
Therefore, Feature Selection (FS) has become a crucial pre -
processing technique in ML -based IDS. It addresses the 
challenge of high-dimensional data by selecting the most 
discriminative features. FS technique is not exclusive to IDS 
but is employed across various fields [8]. FS is conducted prior 
to the construction of a classification phase to improve input 
data quality by selecting the most discriminative features while   
eliminating irrelevant or redundant features from the data 
stream that do not impact model performance  [9]. Employing 
FS can significantly reduce memory consumption and 
overfitting, while enhancing both the model's performance and 
interpretability [10]. However, in addition to addressing high 
dimensionality, FS should also crucially tackle feature drift 
challenge [11]. High dimensionality arises from vast 
probabilities for FS, causing many ML methods to confront 
NP-hardness challenges. Consequently, the FS process can 
become computationally expensive, often necessitating 
exponential time, and leading to slow convergence towards an 
optimal subset of features . Therefore, performing FS on high -
dimensional data necessitates both efficient memory and the 
availability of substantial computational resources . On the 
other hand, feature drift occurs when the significance of subset 
features changes over time, transitioning from relevant to 
irrelevant or redundant, and vice versa. This phenomenon 
highlights the non -applicability of static methods for FS. We 
mean by static methods, approaches that assume a single 
decision of optimal features for the entire data ( a single  
attribute representing the relevancy in a whole dataset)  [12]. 
Unfortunately, this assumption is often invalid due to the 
dynamic nature encountered in many applications , IDS is one 
among them. Hence, many IDSs in the literature that are trained 
for static environments may perform poorly when applied to 
non-stationary data streams. Over time these systems may 
struggle to adapt to changes in data distribution and the 
relationships between features  and target concepts, leading to 
increased false negatives, false alarms, and even a decline in 
accuracy [13]. As data streams have indefinite lengths and 
infeasible to  store on available memory for later analysis, 
classifiers should adopt a single -pass incremental learning 
approach. This involves processing new instances sequentially 
as they arrive and adapting to the changes, rather than handling 
the entire training set, as is  typical with stationary data  [14]. 
Considering, the significance of features in the data stream 
might not be equally throughout the experiment as data arrived 
sequentially [15]. 
FS is considered a combinatorial optimization problem with 
multiple objectives. On the top of these objectives are accuracy 
and memory reduction , which may have some conflicting 
natures. Hence, Meta-Heuristic (MH) Multi-Objective 
Optimisation (MOO) algorithms are one of the candidate 
algorithms for handling it [16]. However, direct application of 
MH-MOO in high -dimensional spaces presents inefficiencies 
due to two main challenges. The first is the significant memory 
and computational demands required to allocate the high -
dimensional solution space . The second one is the concern of 
non-convergence due to high number of candidate solutions. 
The first challenge can be mitigated by proposing variable 
length searching. It is meant by variable length searching 
enabling a different solution length according to its effective  
component and avoiding the addition of dummy variables to 
handle the length change between solutions. The second 
challenge is handled by balance between exploration and 
exploitation. It is meant by exploration taking a decision 
without any prior consideration of previous gained knowledge 
through the searching while it is meant by exploitation enabling 
previous gained knowledge for making current decisions. A 
balance between them is to be enabled in order to prevent 
lacking of convergence due exploration and sub-optimality due 
to exploitation [17].  
Furthermore, many ML algorithms are designed based the 
assumption that the training samples across classes are roughly 
balanced. When these algorithms are applied to imbalanced 
datasets, the introduced trained classifier is m ainly derived 
from the majority class. Moreover, we might miss or ignore 
essential patterns from the minority class . This results in 
abysmal prediction performance for the minority class due to 
its inadequate training samples . Users are typically more 
interested in minority classes since many attacks are derived 
from rare patterns . Thus, addressing the imbalanced data  
problem is critical to enhancing classification performance 
[18]. Many researchers have proposed ensemble learning  as a 
method to address the data imbalance problem [19]. This is 
because it isolates the classifier 's decisions from directly 
affecting the overall predictions. Instead, it weights them 
according to their validation performance. The basic concept of

Meta-heuristic search has emerged as the preferred technique 
due to its proven efficiency, leveraging randomized and 
heuristic methods to eliminate the need for exhaustive 
exploration of all combination possibilities to identify optimal 
or near-optimal solutions. Given the multi -objective nature of 
FS, many researchers have turned to  multi-objective meta -
heuristic search algorithms. One of these applications for FS is 
in IDS. In this context, such techniques are essential for 
reducing computational time and complexity while balancing 
the conflicting objectives of enhancing model performance and 
minimizing memory usage[30], [31]. 
In t he work  of [21], the authors discuss us ing a MOO 
algorithm to address the high -dimensionality challenge. Such 
variable-length algorithms prove effective by enabling particles 
in the PSO algorithm to have different lengths and length -
changing mechanisms, thereby defining a smaller search space. 
An alternative suggestion using a variable -size PSO algorithm 
for FS was proposed in [32], the algorithm employed a divide-
and-conquer strategy. On the other hand, a method for handling 
high dimensionality is discussed in  [33], the article suggests a 
strategy that combines filter and wrapper approaches in a single 
evolutionary process to achieve smaller feature subsets while 
maintaining classification performance in a reduced timeframe. 
A MOO-based evolutionary algorithm proposed to address the 
feature drift occurrence and enhance effectiveness in data 
streaming was introduced in [12], the algorithm determines the 
time of feature drift occurrence in the stream by using a one -
time random initialization mechanism to select a small subset 
of relevant features from the original feature space.  
Furthermore, in terms of balancing the trade -off between 
exploration and exploitation and avoid getting trapped in local 
optima, the study of [34] developed a MOO for FS called 
Binary Differential Evolution with self-learning. The algorithm 
addresses the limitations of Differential Evolution (DE) in 
terms of exploration by incorporating three embedded methods: 
a binary mutation for improved searching , a one -bit purifying 
technique to identify optimal individuals, and a non-dominated 
sorting operator with crowding distance to reduce 
computational complexity .  In research of  [35], the authors 
proposed a hybrid algorithm for FS that combines the strengths 
of both binary Grey Wolf Optimization (GWO) and PSO. This 
approach aims to balance the trade-off between exploration and 
exploitation, leading to enhanced solutions and search space s. 
More studies in the literature focusing on MOO FS methods 
address the challenge of balancing the trade -off between 
exploration and exploitation while avoiding local optima traps, 
as referenced in  [36], [36], [37], [38], [37], [39] and [40].  
The study presented in [41] proposes an algorithm that 
addresses both classification performance and feature cost as 
objectives. By incorporating both convergence -guiding and 
diversity-guiding search operators, it utilizes leader and 
external archives to enhance its search capabilities . Similarly, 
the study in [42] proposes an algorithm based on multi -
objective PSO for FS, addressing two conflicting objectives 
classification performance and the number of features.  
Furthermore, two binary versions of MOO algorithms FS to 
solve the two contradictory objectives by maximizing the 
accuracy of the algorithms and reduc ing the representation of 
the selected features as referenced in [43] and [44]. In addition, 
a fuzzy multi-objective FS method based on PSO was proposed 
by [45] to address FS problems with fuzzy cost. The method 
utilized a fuzzy dominance relationship to compare candidate 
particles and a fuzzy crowding distance measure to prune the 
solutions. The study in [46] proposes a multi-objective PSO FS 
method that ranks features based on their frequencies in the 
archive set to enhance the effectiveness of particle movement 
in PSO . The frequent challenge in the literature is balancing 
exploration and exploitation , m any authors have proposed 
multi-objective algorithms to address the aforementioned 
challenge. Some approaches tackle the high dimensionality 
challenge in FS,  while not many focus on  the feature drift 
occurrence in a data stream . The literature illustrates a 
noticeable neglect regarding DFS methods and their ability to 
manage the expected changes in feature relevancy over time.

H. Pareto front 
The Pareto Front in MOO denotes a set of non -dominated 
solutions for a problem with multiple objectives. Non -
dominated solutions are those in which it’s  not possible to 
improve one objective without simultaneously worsening 
another. The Pareto Front represents the trade -off between 
these conflicting objectives and can be used to identify optimal 
solutions for a given optimization problem. The non-dominated 
solutions within the solution space are referred to as Pareto sets 
[52].

C. Imbalance Data Handling 
In data imbalance handling, whe n a significant discrepancy 
exists between the quantities of data points in different classes. 
We utilized the Synthetic Minority Over -sampling Technique 
(SMOTE), an effective method for addressing imbalanced data 
[54]. SMOTE is designed to oversample the data points in the 
minority class by creating synthetic examples from the 
minority class sample 𝑥𝑖 in the feature space. In comparison to 
random oversampling, which duplicates minority class 
instances, we prefer using SMOTE. One reason is that random 
oversampling can introduce overfitting, especially in high -
dimensional datasets. This overfitting concern is effectively 
addressed by SMOTE  [55].  In our implementation, splitting 
the data into training and testing sets. the test set remains 
untouched during preprocessing and model training, preserving 
its original distribution for unbiased evaluation.  
SMOTE method procedure: 
1) Randomly select a data point 𝑥𝑖 from the minority 
class. 
2) Select the k nearest neighbors from the minority 
class sample 𝑥𝑖, where k=5.  
3) Compute the distance between the selected sample 𝑥𝑖 
and its chosen neighbors. 
4) Randomly select one of its closet’s neighbors points 
let’s assume 𝑧𝑖. 
5) Synthetically generating samples 𝑥𝑛𝑒𝑤 in the line 
segment between 𝑥𝑖 and 𝑧𝑖. 
The process iterates until a balance is achieved by blending the 
genuine minority class data with the synthetically created 
samples. The balanced dataset thus obtained serves as the 
foundation to train the model. 
D. GP-combiner  
The framework of the ensemble classifier based on the GP -
combiner consists of several components as presented in Fig. 
2. First, the stream data is fed into the framework and is 
assumed to be partitioned into a sequence of chunks. Each 
chunk is to be partia lly labelled, which means feeding its 
labelled part to the training sub -block and into another sub -
block that is intended for detecting the concept drift. 
Afterwards, there is the core of the GP -combiner which is the 
ensemble update process which consists of three sub -
components, namely, base classifier replacement, classifier 
induction, and combiner generation and graphs for prediction. 
The ensemble update process feeds the graph to the ensemble 
that performs the prediction and keeps the basic classifier p ool 
for the next round. Furthermore, in our approach, we utilize the 
DDM method for concept drift detection. DDM is a window 
technique that detects  the significant increases in the 
classification error rate and updates the classifier to adapt to the 
new concept. This mechanism ensures timely adaptation to 
concept drift and accurately maintains the framework's 
performance. 
E. Dynamic Feature Aware Genetic Programming 
Ensemble (DFA-GPE) 
DFA-GPE presents our novel variant of the GP ensemble 
classifier. It is distinguished by the addition of the DFS block 
based on improved multi-objective PSO, as illustrated in Fig. 
2. This is regarded as an early pre-processing stage that is used 
for enabling the process of G P combiner to be carried on the 
relevant features from each group of instances  instead of 
carrying it on the entire feature space through dual evaluation 
stages. Consequently, two added values are obtained, namely, 
better prediction performance and more memory efficiency.

4. Solutions selections 
The objective of the solution selection process is to choose a 
single solution from the Pareto front to serve as the FS solution. 
Since our optimi sation algorithm focuses on two objectives - 
accuracy and reduction ratio of features - the obtained solutions 
display varying degrees of quality concerning these objectives. 
Consequently, choosing one solution will necessitate a trade -
off between the two objectives, leading to the prioritisation of 
one over the other. To address this issue, we propose an 
algorithm that leverages the entire set of solutions for FS, 
employing a voting -based approach. The pseudocode  is 
outlined in Algorithm (5).

The voting -based FS algorithm using the Pareto front 
presents numerous benefits. Firstly, it ensures a balanced trade-
off between two objectives the accuracy and reduction ratio of 
features - by taking into account the entire set of solutions, thus 
preventing the over -prioritisation of one objective over the 
other. Additionally, the algorithm employs an ensemble 
approach by leveraging the collective wisdom of multiple 
solutions, leading to a more robust and stable FS compared to 
relying on a single solution.  Furthermore, t he adaptive 
threshold, calculated based on the maximum and minimum 
frequencies in the histogram, allows for a flexible selection 
criterion, which is particularly useful when dealing with diverse 
datasets or optimisation problems. This adaptability, combined 
with the algorithm's applicability to various optimi sation 
problems and datasets, results in a versatile and scalable 
approach. One of the key advantages of considering multiple 
solutions from the Pareto front is the potential to reduce 
overfitting, as the algorithm is less likely to select a solution 
that performs well only on a specific training dataset. 
Consequently, this can contribute to improved generali sation 
performance, enhancing the overall effectiveness of the 
approach to unseen data. Moreover, the algorithm generates a 
histogram representing the frequency of selected features 
across all solutions. This provides an easily interpretable 
insight into the importance of each feature, making it  simpler 
to understand the context and relevance of the selected features 
in relation to the problem at hand.  Finally, a general flowchart

C. Evaluation Metrics 
This section presents the evaluation  metrics from the 
classification perspective based on a confusion matrix 
containing two dimensions: the predicted and actual class. The 
classification performance was evaluated using four metrics: 
accuracy, precision, recall  and F1-score, as illustrated in 
Equations ( 9), (10), (11) and (12)  [18]. Considering that the 
True Positive (TP) is the number of actual attacks correctly 
classified as attacks. True Negative (TN) is the number of 
actual benign instances correctly classified as benign. False 
Negative (FN) is the number of actual attacks incorrectly 
classified as benign. False Positive (FP) is the number of actual 
benign instances incorrectly classified as attacks. 
The evaluation metrics are: 
Accuracy (Acc): accuracy d efined as  ratio of true positive 
instances and true negative instances to the total number of 
predicted instances. Can be measured by the Equation: 
Acc = TP + TN
TP + TN + FP + FN (9) 
Precision (Pr): precision calculated by dividing the number of 
true positive instances by the sum of true positive instances and 
false positive instances. 
Pr = TP
TP + FP (10) 
F1 Score: F1 score or the harmonic mean of precision and 
Recall.  
F1 Score = 2 ∗ Precision ∗ Recall
Precision + Recall (11) 
Recall (Rc): recall also called the sensitivity measurement, can 
be calculated by dividing the number of true positive instances 
by the sum of true positive instances and false negative 
instances. 
Rc = TP
TP + FN (12)

VII. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORKS  
In this article, we proposed an IDS for data stream detection 
with feature drift -aware. The IDS based GP -combiner 
ensemble classifier equipped with DFS named DFA-GPE. Our 
framework presents a novel DFS using a multi -objective PSO 
variant that optimized simultaneously accuracy and memory 
reduction and tackled the feature drift challenge in a high 
dimensionality data stream. It featured by an intelligent 
population initialization algorithm using Bernoulli distribution 
and symmetric uncertainty, for faster convergence by 
favouring certain regions in the solution space. The algorithm 
also incorporated a hybrid transfer function set, allowing 
versatile searching behaviour and several mechanisms were 
introduced to balance exploration and exploitation, including a 
novel exemplar selection method. Furthermore, the integration 
of the developed multi -objective DFS to the ensemble -based 
GP-combiner resulted in a unified framework addressing both 
concept and feature drifts in data stream scenarios. The 
proposed method uniquely used the entire Pareto front for FS, 
providing a more efficient way to capture changes in decision-
making across consecutive iterations. The article compared our 
method with four benchmarks: RF, LR, RFE and evolutionary 
algorithm NSGA -II on two datasets , namely,  HIKARI 2021 
and TON_IoT 2020. The results show the DFA -GPE method 
stands out distinctly from other approaches by consistently 
delivering superior results on both datasets. Notably, it 
achieves 99.09% accuracy on HIKARI dataset and 92.64% 
accuracy on TON_IoT dataset . Hence, o ur approach 
significantly outperforms all other benchmarks . This evident 
across all classification metrics, while demonstrating excellent 
memory efficiency, reduce memory usage to 93.49% and 
62.93% in both datasets, respectively. 
This superiority is attributed to its ability to perform 
variable-length searching  while simultaneously optimize 
multiple objectives, such as accuracy and memory , making it 
more flexible and adaptive than other methods, which leads to 
better classification performance. Additionally, the superiority 
of our IDS in both fields indicates to stability and scalability of 
our method. Future work can focus on assessing the 
algorithm's scalability on larger and more diverse datasets . 
Furthermore, optimizing computational efficiency  is another 
future work.  In addition, we  will explore alternative meta -
heuristic algorithms or variants in our method.
