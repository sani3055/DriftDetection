Abstract
Cybersecurity has become a serious concern in this digital age, with ever-increasing threats aimed at individuals, enter-
prises, and countries across the globe. AI has been rapidly emerging as an influential technology that possess enormous 
abilities to enhance data protection measures by automating threat recognition, vulnerability assessment, and incident 
response. This study focuses on comprehensively examining current literature works dedicated to AI along with its 
applications in cybersecurity and presents an innovative architecture to deal with challenges that pose enormous risks 
to worlds community. Explore advanced techniques, including Machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL), and their 
implementation in threat intelligence, intrusion recognition, malware analysis, phishing detection, and user authentica-
tion. Furthermore, the propounded review deliberates on various limitations such as data scarcity, model interpretabil-
ity, and vulnerability to adversarial attacks can hinder the effectiveness of existing models in real-world cybersecurity 
scenarios, leading to reduced accuracy, slower response times, and increased susceptibility to evasion techniques. The 
future advances, including the evolution of explainable AI, predictive analytics, quantum computing, and real-time 
management capabilities, are also emphasized. This review paper is intended to offer a systematic understanding of 
modern AI techniques and prospective solutions in enriching the cybersecurity domain, offering valuable insights for 
researcher scholars and professionals in diverse fields.
Article Highlights
• Covering comprehensive analysis of AI in cybersecurity, offensive/defensive strategies, evolving threats, and cyber -
security practices.
• The paper explains attack types, rising trends, cybersecurity policies, cryptography, ML/DL in IDS, and key studies on 
anomaly detection and malware.
• Described cybersecurity datasets, challenges, limitations, and future trends, and proposed the Q-PERAL methodology.

1 Introduction
1.1  Rise of AI in cybersecurity
Contemporary societies are witnessing the widespread impact of digital technologies, leading to a massive increase in 
big data and information flow [1]. This technological transformation offers abundant advantages and, in the meantime 
has also unveiled the confidential segment of citizens, companies, and nation-states to a wide range of information 
security risks. Ransomware attacks have increased by 150% in the past year alone, with businesses losing an average 
of $4.5 million per incident, while phishing attacks account for over 80% of all reported security breaches, targeting 
both individuals and organizations globally. [2 , 3] Biham (1994) introduced cryptanalytic methods that explored how 
attackers can take advantage of cryptographic systems using different but related keys to reveal underlying patterns 
in the encryption process. His research highlights vulnerabilities in block cipher algorithms, offering crucial insights 
that have informed the development of more secure encryption methods [4 ]. Digital threats like data poisoning, 
ransomware perils, phishing attacks, and advanced persistent threats (APTs), have become progressively complex and 
recurrent. Typical cybersecurity strategies rely on established regulations and conventional mechanisms which may 
not be sufficient to keep up with transforming threat landscapes and evolving ploys of malicious adversaries. This 
dynamic network security scenario requires more resilient and smart defense mechanisms along with cryptography. 
Modern innovations in AI have had a substantial influence on the cyber risk management landscape, with the rise of 
both offensive AI and defensive AI maneuvers. Utilization of AI technologies by attackers to streamline and augment 
various aspects of cyberattacks represent offensive AI whereas defensive AI revolves around capitalizing on AI to per -
ceive, minimize, and safeguard against security threats [5 ]. Understanding the relationship between these strategies 
is pivotal for developing real cybersecurity measures in a progressively multi-faceted and dynamic threat landscape.
Offensive AI techniques: Incorporates a wide plethora of methodologies adopted by perpetrators to take advan-
tage of network vulnerabilities, elude recognition, and exploit the effect of cyberattacks. These practices include 
automatic hacking infrastructures, adversarial ML, and AI-powered malicious applications [6 ]. For instance, self-
operating hacking frameworks harness AI algorithms to recognize and pursue software susceptibilities at scale, while 
adversarial ML techniques are employed to produce corrupt data that can betray AI-based network security systems. 
Moreover, AI-driven malware integrates ML/DL processes to dodge conventional detection setup and acclimate to 
self-protective measures employed by the intended agencies [7 ]. Certain AI-driven hacking frameworks include Dee -
pLocker, which uses deep learning to hide malicious payloads, AutoSploit, an automated tool that identifies and 
exploits vulnerabilities autonomously. DeepPhish creates highly personalized and convincing attacks. These frame -
works demonstrate how AI can also enhance cybercriminal capabilities by automating and refining exploit methods.
Defensive AI strategies: These strategies adapt to increasingly sophisticated offensive AI techniques by continuously 
evolving through techniques like adversarial training, anomaly detection, and real-time threat analysis. These strategies 
leverage machine learning to identify patterns in attack behavior, predict new threats and, enhance decision-making 
processes. Additionally, defensive AI systems improve their robustness against adversarial manipulation by learning 
from past attacks and adapting models to respond to emerging AI-driven threats more effectively [8 ]. Besides, security 
monitoring interfaces enable companies to vigilantly identify and alleviate potential threats by analyzing humongous 
amounts of data from diverse sources, such as security feeds, threat reports, and dark web forums [9 ].
1.2  Dynamic evolution of cyber threats and the need for advanced defensive techniques
The evolution of AI in cybersecurity intersects with regulatory and ethical challenges by raising concerns over privacy, 
data protection, and accountability. As AI-driven systems become more autonomous, they must comply with regula-
tions to ensure ethical considerations in data usage, transparency, and decision-making processes. Furthermore, the 
potential for AI to inadvertently reinforce biases or be exploited by malicious actors highlights the need for robust 
frameworks to govern AI’s deployment in cybersecurity while ensuring fairness and responsibility.
Cyber threats are continuously developing in varied aspects in terms of complexity, volume, and intricacy, present -
ing substantial hurdles to organizations worldwide. Frequency and diversity of these attacks are more than ever before, 
especially Advanced Persistent Threats (APT)s are complicated and can cause long-term damages since they are employed 
by extremely skilled adversaries with precise goals, such as reconnaissance, disruption, or monetary gain [10].

Shortcomings of traditional cybersecurity methods
Conventional network security systems are effective within specific context, exhibit various issues when con-
fronted with complex modern-day threats since they rely mostly on receptive measures. The emergence of Big 
Data, with its complexity and speed, challenges security teams in analysing insights and detecting threats in real-
time network environments. With the emergence of BigData, ubiquitous volume of data is being generated with 
each passing second with varied velocity and heterogeneity which can overwhelm traditional security teams to 
analyse and extract relevant insights. Moreover, detecting intricate details and indicators of compromise amongst 
legitimate traffic could pose severe challenges in real-time dynamic network scenario. The need for contextual 
awareness regarding business functioning, user behaviour as well as human fatigue can hinder their capacity to 
synchronize with the dimension and density of modern cyber-crimes [13]. IoT and 5 G networks introduce unique 
vulnerabilities due to their vast scale, interconnected devices, and reliance on real-time data. The increased number 
of devices and new communication protocols expand attack surfaces, while reliance on cloud and edge computing 
adds complexity to securing these networks.
Increasing demand for modern defensive strategies
Traditional models are inadequate for addressing modern threats, creating a rising need for advanced techniques 
like AI to enhance existing defenses. DL techniques possess the ability to process and analyse large volumes of 
data in real-time to look for compromising patterns and can detect anomalies accurately. AI-driven cybersecurity 
systems can adapt and evolve in response to transforming threat scenario. They can learn from fresh inputs and 
update themselves dynamically thus enhancing their resilience.ML and DL methodologies can improve their solu-
tion’s preciseness and effectiveness by making use of sophisticated predictive modelling analysis and prioritization 
abilities. These technologies can assist security agencies to qickly retort and respond to alerts, investigate risk inci-
dents, and distribute resources in a more efficient manner in comparison to state -of-the art methodologies [14].
Objectives and scope of the review
This literature review is aimed at offering an elaborate investigation of current scenario in cybersecurity by sys-
tematically analysing existing research studies and highlighting principal breakthroughs. It also identifies major 
challenges associated with these technologies to provide noteworthy solutions in the field of cybersecurity. To 
accomplish this, the review contributes towards following areas:
• Detailed analysis of AI techniques applied to cybersecurity areas such as IDS, anomaly detection, threat intel-
ligence and user behaviour analytics, coupled with a comprehensive review of current methodologies, datasets, 
and progress in the field.
• Identification of primary challenges and limitations in implementing AI techniques paving way for forthcoming 
research.
By methodically reviewing the literature, this study has been organised into following structure with Section 1 pre -
senting a detailed introduction on AI and the need for modern techniques in cybersecurity has been emphasized 
followed by objectives and contributions. Related Works in section 2 reviews existing research methodologies in this 
field by offering visual representation in terms of charts and tables along with identification of research gaps that 
the proposed system is focused on resolving. Proposed Methodology has been presented in detail in section 3 to 
highlight its effectiveness in dealing with shortcomings of existing cybersecurity solutions. Ultimately, Conclusion 
in section 4 summarizes crucial outcomes, deliberates on prospective avenues for future research, thus bestowing 
valued observations for researcher scholars and professionals in information technology security realm.

Cyber security policies and cryptography
In the context of cybersecurity, several approaches and principles play noteworthy roles in defending systems against 
attacks [28]. Key principles include confidentiality, integrity and availability collectively known as CIA triad. The term 
cyber-security policy denotes rules and regulations governing information distribution, data conservation and technol-
ogy control whose objective is to implement the major CIA principles. According to Palmieri et al. (2021), these are core 
policies that form the foundation of system security. Confidentiality ensures that only authorized individuals can access 
sensitive information whereas integrity guarantees authorized changes are only made to information and availability 
makes sure systems and data are legally accessed [29].
Le Nguyen et al. (2021) emphasized that while these principles are essential, they may not fully address the intricacies 
of modern cyber threats which require more comprehensive cybersecurity measures [30].
Alzubaidi et al. (2021) highlights the mounting complexities and constraints encountered in cybersecurity, especially 
owing to expanding interactions between virtual and physical data. This complexity demands meticulously designed 
comprehensive strategies that addressed these evolving challenges [31].
Additionally, Chandra et al. (2020) noted the fact that as cybersecurity becomes more and more sophisticated, organi-
zations need to adopt highly effective security perspective and invest in advanced systems and services to stay ahead 
of hackers. Companies like McAfee, Cisco, and Trend Micro are the major players whose contributions are noteworthy 
in this arena [32].

In terms of specific attack methods, the most commonly used methods by cybercriminals are Denial of Service (DoS) 
attacks [33] which involve consuming all server resources thus making access to service impossible for genuine users 
[Alghamdie et al., 2021). Man-in-the-Middle attacks [34] occur when a hacker intercepts and potentially alters data pack-
ets between a victim device and a router (Huang et al., 2020). Malware [35] such as worms or viruses infects devices and 
systems causing harm and disruption (Edgar et al. 2017). Phishing [36] involves sending seemingly legitimate emails to 
trick users into disclosing confidential information (Saxena et al. 2021). These attack methods illustrate the diverse threats 
that cybersecurity strategies must address. In the realm of cybersecurity, while cyber technologies have significantly 
enhanced community productivity and information dissemination, they also pose challenges to system security. Instant 
and fast data transfers often compromise overall security as security policies designed to protect systems can conflict 
with productivity goals of the organization.
According to Katrakazas et al. (2020), the motivation for increased efficiency frequently clashes with security require-
ments as preventive measures can restrict user access and consume critical system resources [37]. However, Tam et al. 
(2021) noted that national cyber-security policies generally cover a broad spectrum, where all citizens and foreign entities 
are involved, while corporate policies are more narrowly focused on employees and contractual obligations [38]. Cheng 
et al. (2020) emphasized that the scope and application of these policies differ significantly as the implementation of 
these policies also varies between government and corporate settings. Government policies are incorporated into law 
through formal processes, whereas corporate policies are typically managed by centralized security units responsible 
for enforcing standards and regulations [39]. Quigley et al. (2015) explained that while government policies become law 
corporate policies are often utilized to guide internal regulations and compliance [40].
Sakhnini et al. (2021) highlighted that cyber-security policies are crucial for guiding laws and regulations but they may 
not be as effective and authoritative as constitutional laws. Enforcement of cyber-security policies can be done through 
specific orders and regulations even in the absence of a formal policy. In organizational settings, multiple departments 
are expected to adhere to policies to avoid sanctions, with middle managers playing a key role in implementing and 
monitoring compliance standards [41]. Baig et al. (2017) drew attention to the fact that while these managers could be 
integral to policy enforcement, problems arise when policies are left to individuals who may prioritize cost savings over 
strict adherence to security measures [42]. Arend et al. (2020) further discusses the intricacy of managing data security, 
noting that it often involves balancing cost and security, as maintaining rigorous regulations may be impossible in the 
face of budgetary limitations [43].
Cryptography is essential to cybersecurity, as it primarily ensures major objectives of CIA triad by encrypting data to 
keep it accessible only to authorized users through techniques like hashing and digital signatures, which confirm that 
data has not been altered. In addition, cryptography supports certain other principles like authentication by verifying 
identities with digital certificates and multi-factor authentication to ensure non-repudiation so that transactions cannot 
be denied. It also enables secure communication via SSL protocol and involves effective key management measures 
for secure encryption. Additionally, cryptography protects data privacy by preventing unauthorized access to sensitive 
information. Some of the traditional cryptographic algorithms are provided in Table  1.
While these techniques provide essential security by encrypting data and managing keys, they often fall short in 
addressing the latest cybersecurity challenges. AI enhances cybersecurity by offering adaptive threat detection, real-
time analysis, and behavioral insights. Unlike static algorithms that protect data based on established methods, AI can 
analyze patterns and relationships to identify anomalies in real time, adapting to dynamic evolving threats. This enables 
proactive defense mechanisms, addressing specific vulnerabilities that conventional algorithms may miss and thus has 
the potential to improve overall system resilience.

2.1  Applications of ML and DL in cyber security
Intrusion detection systems (IDS) 
This is a critical component of cybersecurity infrastructure which is responsible for detection and respond to malevo-
lent operations within cyber networks. They operate by extensively analysing real-time network traffic and event logs to 
recognise possible indicators of suspicious behaviour that may indicate a risk of cyber-attack. Panda et al. [77] employed 
a hybrid approach combining Decision Trees, Principal Component Analysis and SPegasos which is a stochastic variant of 
sub-gradient solver in SVM. This method accomplished a high detection rate of 99.5% and a low false alarm rate of 0.1%. 
The combination of multiple techniques enabled effictive optimization thereby creating a robust system that accurately 
distinguishes between normal and intrusive behaviour.
Bo Lang et al. [78] provides a comprehensive overview of ML and DL techniques are applied to IDS. As cyber threats 
evolve in complexity, conventional rule-based approaches have proven inadequate in detecting well planned attacks. 
This survey examines the shift toward using advanced AI methods for more adaptive, accurate and robust detection.
Gaddam et al. [79] utilized K-Means Clustering and the ID3 Decision Tree for intrusion detection, achieving a recogni-
tion rate of 96.24% and a minimal false alarm rate of 0.03%. Incorporation of K-Means algorithm ensures accumulation 
of similar data points, aiding in identification of prospective intrusions, while ID3 Decision Tree classifies these clusters 
resulting in a method with high detection accuracy is extremely effective at minimizing false positives.
Wang et al. [80] proposed a hybrid technique using Fuzzy C-Means and Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs). Their method 
achieved a detection rate of 96.71% Fuzzy C-Means allows for soft clustering of data points enhancing flexibility in 
handling uncertainties and overlaps between normal and attack traffic, while ANNs are utilized for their strong pattern 
recognition capabilities.
Salama et al. [81] integrated Support Vector Machines (SVMs) with Deep Belief Networks (DBNs), resulting in a detec-
tion rate of 92.84. Through SVMs, high accuracy is attained while DBNs a type of deep learning model provides effective 
feature learning from complex data. Syarif et al. [82] combined multiple algorithms-Naive Bayes, J48 Decision Tree, JRip 
(rule induction algorithm) and iBK (nearest neighbour method) to achieve very higher detection rate of 99.80%. This 
hybrid approach leverages the strengths of different ML techniques to cover various aspects of data classification, lead-
ing to an extremely high accuracy level, likely due to the complementary nature of the models. AI techniques play a

2.2  Anomaly detection
Multiple studies have proposed solutions to identify outliers in cyber-security network, most of them are based on 
Semi-supervised, DL and algorithms based on graphs Table 4. Ahmed et al. (2016) presented a detailed survey on various 
anomaly detection networks and concluded that semi-supervised One-Class-SVM is the preferred approach due to its 
flexibility in dealing with multi-dimensional data and has minimal impact to noise in input [94– 96]. Yousefi et al. (2017) 
emphasized on the accuracy of OCSVM based models which are on higher side owing to its seamless methodology 
of handling datasets with noise. To tackle the challenge in handling multi-modal data, Auto Encoders (AE) have been 
proposed to rebuild different network data movement prototypes. These patterns are processed by more than one hid-
den neural layer followed by dimension reduction to match regular data movement pattern. Anomalies are highlighted 
when rebuilt message doesn’t match the normal pattern [ 97]. Common belief from various studies suggest OCSVM 
based approaches performs far better than AE based approaches. Implementation complexity and efforts required for

Network traffic analysis and malware detection
Multiple AI architectures like deep neural networks (DNNs) and autoencoders have been quite instrumental in 
strengthening network security by effectively scrutinizing network traffic data for the presence of any irregularities, 
interruptions, and compromising activities [115] These systems operate by integrating tremendous amounts of network 
traffic data and discriminating patterns indicative of unusual behaviour. By capitalizing on this understanding, they 
can then isolate deviations from pre-defined norms, thus potentially recognizing hostile activities [116]. By training 
DL networks on labelled datasets comprising both regular and abnormal network traffic instances [117], AI model can 
differentiate subtle patterns that may evade conventional rule-based IDS. As a result, they can effectively flag doubtful 
activities such as DoS attacks, phishing [118], port scanning, or uncommon data transfers.
Autoencoders, can also utilised to compress input data into latent representation and then reconstructing it to accu-
rately generate normal network traffic patterns. As a result, when exposed to abnormal data during inference, they 
exhibit sophisticated ability in identifying discrepancies, enabling them to effectively detect inconsistencies such as 
network intrusions or malware transmissions [119]. DL-based IDS thus possesses the capabilities to enhance accuracy 
and efficiency of threat detection in modern complex network environments. By continuously learning and adapting to 
evolving threats, these systems play a major role in defending network infrastructures against intricate attacks

2.4  Potential future directions and emerging trends
Exploration of modern technologies unveils several advancements that can effectively challenge evolving threat land-
scapes. Quantum cryptography leverages the concept of mechanics to develop ultra-secure communication protocols 
which can offer tough resistant to quantum attacks. Due to ever increasing proliferation of IoT devices, defensive strate-
gies will have to focus more on securely protecting associated devices and cyber ecosystems, requiring advanced meth-
odologies to govern weaknesses and safeguard data integrity. The integration of AI and ML into security infrastructures 
exhibits diverse prospects for enhancing threat detection, prediction, and mitigation practices, strengthening systems 
to adapt and progress against varied attacks self-sufficiently. Moreover, the upsurge of decentralized technologies like 
blockchain instigates novel archetypes for securing online transactions and individual identities, radicalizing cybersecu-
rity architectures towards decentralized and unalterable solutions [157]. The integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and 
Machine Learning (ML) into cryptographic systems has significantly advanced key management, threat detection and 
quantum-resistant algorithm development. Google’s AI-driven key management system uses ML to dynamically adjust 
encryption policies and optimize key lifecycles, enhancing protection against attacks on encrypted data. Darktrace’s 
AI-powered Intrusion Detection System (IDS) analyzes encrypted network traffic to identify subtle and complex attack 
patterns, enabling proactive threat mitigation. Meanwhile, IBM and Google are leveraging AI to develop quantum-safe 
cryptographic algorithms, such as IBM’s Qiskit Cryptography project, which simulates and optimizes quantum-resistant 
techniques. Additionally, Microsoft Research’s “CryptoNets” project employs deep neural networks to analyze and uncover 
vulnerabilities in traditional cryptographic systems like RSA and elliptic curve cryptography, demonstrating AI’s potential 
to strengthen cryptographic security. Building on these advancements, our future study proposes a novel framework 
that combines quantum-resistant cryptography with explainable AI to detect and mitigate anomalies in encrypted sys-
tems with the aim to enhance privacy and security by leveraging quantum-safe algorithms while providing transparent 
and interpretable AI-driven insights into anomaly detection, ensuring robust and future-proof cybersecurity solutions.

3.2  Proposed methodology
Title: “Q-PEARL: Quantum Privacy-Enhanced Anomaly Recognition Leveraging Explainable AI”
While current research studies have made substantial developments in anomaly detection, there remains unexplored 
potentialities for innovation. Proposing an enhanced solution offers plentiful benefits for cybersecurity experts and enter-
prises. The proposed solution, Q-PEARL, tackles scalability by leveraging quantum computing’s ability to process large 
volumes of data more efficiently than classical systems. By utilizing quantum-enhanced algorithms, high-dimensional 
anomaly recognition tasks can be handled effectively which are typically resource-intensive for traditional AI models. Our 
proposed architecture also makes use of Explainable AI (XAI) especially LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Expla-
nations) and SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations), which provide transparent and interpretable insights into how the 
quantum-enhanced anomaly detection model makes its decisions. These techniques allow cybersecurity professionals 
to better understand the underlying reasoning behind the model’s predictions, which is critical in high-stakes environ-
ments where interpretability is essential for trust and accountability [158]. At its heart, the arrangement begins with 
data collection and pre-processing cybersecurity data from heterogeneous sources. Quantum feature extraction utilises 
algorithms to derive complex feature representations from pre-processed data, unravelling the parallelism inherent in 
quantum computing [159]. These features are served as inputs to DL model for anomaly detection, where conventional 
and quantum constituents work together to identify deviant patterns. Synchronously, XAI techniques are integrated to 
present comprehensible elucidations for model’s decisions, permitting cybersecurity professionals to understand and 
evaluate detection outcomes efficiently. The design framework incorporates scalable quantum computing structure, 
augmented with resilient measures to protect confidential data. Incessant supervision ensures real-time assessment 
and fine-tuning of model’s performance, facilitated by frequent inputs from domain experts. Unified integration into 
existing cybersecurity frameworks are scheduled followed by intensive training and adherence to compliance and regu-
latory standards. Through this hybrid implementation, our model aims to advance anomaly detection methodology in 
cybersecurity by incorporating cutting-edge technologies and action-oriented observations for security practitioners.

recognition accuracy of security threats but also presents notable insights into decision-making process of AI models, 
thereby empowering security analysts to trust system’s performance. By providing a systematic understanding of these 
modern techniques and their prospective solutions, this review aims to offer valuable insights for researchers, scholars,
