Abstract 
Modern network infrastructures need to have intrusion detection systems that continue to operate accurately when there are topology changes, 
as well as adversarial manipulations of the system; when different layers of the communication protocols interact w ith each other; and when 
new types of behaviors occur in the system. Machine Learning techniques typically analyze individual flows, use a static thre shold, do not give 
much structure, and also cannot explain how cross -layer attacks propagate through the s ystem. In order to improve upon current limitations, 
this research proposes a predictive model integration technique utilizing the combination of Spatiotemporal Graph Convolution al Ensemble 
Validation for topology aware risk estimation; Recursive Multi Res olution Adversarial Calibration for confidence correction against evasive 
attacks; Topological Perturbation Stability Analysis for evaluating robustness; Hierarchical Attention Diffusion Validation f or identifying 
anomalies at the protocol layer; and Behav ioral Manifold Conformity Indexing for detecting outliers (zero -days) in the system. The sequence 
nature of this proposed architecture allows for the preservation of information flow from one level of abstraction (structura l), to another 
(statistical), then to others (adversarial, hierarchical, behavioral). This allows for improvement in reliability, interpretation, and deployability. 
Preliminary results indicate an accuracy of 98.1%, an F1 score of 97.4%, an AUROC value of 98.8%, an average false positive r ate of 2.9%, an 
adversary detection accuracy of 95. 2%, and a zero day detection accuracy of 93.1% in real time scenarios. 
 
Keywords: Intrusion Detection, Spatiotemporal Graph Learning, Adversarial Calibration, Topological Stability, Behavioural Manifold, Scenarios 
 
Introduction 
As today's complex network environments become increasingly vulnerable due to the emergence of sophisticated 
attacks from both within and outside organizations, there is growing need for advanced Intrusion Detection 
Systems (IDS) capable of identifying malicious behavior under adverse and rapidly changing environmental 
conditions. Signature based IDS are often very good at detecting previously identified forms of malicious activity; 
however, they are less effective than other types of IDS when encountering new forms of malicious activity and /or 
unrecognized traffic patterns. While machine learning techniques have significantly improved IDS predictive 
capabilities in comparison to traditional IDS solutions, many machine learning techniques employed by current IDS 
solutions continue to fail to account for the interconnected nature of devices on a given network. Additionally, many 
machine learning algorithms currently used in IDSs make use of static threshold values for determining what 
constitutes an alertable threat. Furthermore, few if any machine learning algorithms currently being applied in IDSs 
offer much insight into how the attacker has progressed from one layer of a network to another for this process.

This paper develops an Integrated Predictive Framework that uses five different Analytical Methods to predict the 
likelihood that a device will be compromised during an attack in process. The first method, referred to as the Spatial-
Temporal Graph Convolutional Ensemble Validation Model, provides an estimate of the risk associated with each 
device on a network & its deployments . The second method, referred to as the Recursive Multi -Resolution 
Adversarial Calibration Method, allows us to improve our confidence in our predictions by providing a measure of 
the quality of our input data (packet payload), summarizing the content of o ur input data (flow summary), and 
testing the robustness of our predictions against data generated through adversarial attacks. The third method, 
referred to as Topological Perturbation Stability Analysis, tests our model's ability to recover from various forms of 
perturbations including node removal, edge addition/removal and changes in traffic Volume in practical scenarios. 
The fourth method, referred to as Hierarchical Attention Diffusion Validation, tracks anomalies through multiple 
levels of abstractio n (network, transport and application). Finally, we develop a fifth method, referred to as 
Behavioral Manifold Conformity Indexing, which measures whether the behavior of a particular session conforms 
to either the benign region or the malicious regions. 
Our proposed system maintains continuous data flow across all four different representations described above and 
provides significant improvements over prior work in terms of its ability to discriminate between true positives 
and false positives; its abili ty to increase confidence in its decisions; and its ability to detect unknown (zero -day) 
and evasion based intrusions. Preliminary evaluations indicate that our proposed system has achieved an accuracy 
rate of 98.1% (the number of instances correctly classified as positive divided by the total number of actual positive 
examples), an F1 score of 97.4%, an Area Under Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUROC) of 98.8% and a 
false positive rate of 2.9% in real time scenarios.

In terms of their ability to provide accurate results, the literature also show  a shift away from accuracy centric 
intrusion detection to those which are both explainable as well as lightweight, stable and operatable. CANDICE 
represents an intelligent and readable way to detect potential intrusions by providing predictive outcome 
information along with human readable explanations [1], while the stability centric framework represents a change 
in focus to explanation consistency and lightweight deployments [2]. The multi -layered, adaptive RNN (Recurrent 
Neural Network) method continues to expand on this area by developing sequential traffic modeling techniques 
within computationally constraint parameters [3] while the application of lightweight ensemble learning methods 
continue to enhance decision diversity while continuing to allow for the provision of explainable results [4]. Prior 
work focused on the use of IoT devices has shown that deep representations require a transparent level of 
interpretability when being utilized in heterogeneous connected environments [5] 
Therefore, it is logical to expand upon this prior research using various forms of XAI (Explainable Artificial 
Intelligence), including LIME (Local Interpretable Model -Agnostic Explanations) centered local explanations that

Proposed Model Design Analysis 
An analytical architecture that is sequential and multi View is used to construct the unified intrusion -detection 
model that has been suggested. It manages five modules that collaborate to handle network structure, temporal 
behavior, packet semantics, adversarial uncertainty, protocol -layer propagation, and session -level conformance. 
These modules are responsible for handling the functions of the network deployments. The Spatiotemporal Graph 
Convolutional Ensemble Validation represents the first step in this design, which may be seen in Figure 2. Recursive 
Multi-Resolution Adversarial Calibration, Topological Perturbation Stability Analysis, Hierarchical Attention 
Diffusion Validation, and Behavioral Manifold Conformity Indexing are the subsequent steps that are carried out in 
this process. Because each module addresses a problem that was present in the module that came before it, this 
arrangement was selected. Despite the fact that the graph module i llustrates how communication dependencies 
evolve over time, it does not completely rectify decisions that are made when individuals are overconfident in 
themselves. Although the adversarial -calibration function modifies these odds, it does not attempt to d etermine 
whether or not they remain stable in the event that the structure of the network undergoes variations. This is 
measured by the topological-stability module, while the hierarchical-attention module defines the process by which 
evidence of anything being wrong passes across different protocol layers. After that, the behavioral-manifold 
module examines each session to determine whether or not it belongs to a taught behavioral area or whether it is 
an unknown outlier. As a consequence, the final product is not only a label for an incursion, but also security 
decisions t hat are calibrated, topology -aware, cross -layer-explainable, and behavior Validated in practical 
scenarios.

Conclusion & Future Scopes 
The integrated intrusion detection framework is designed to show that effective network defense requires more 
than the ability to accurately classify threats; it also needs (a) calibrated confidence in its classifications, (b) 
structurally robust models, ( c) cross -layer interpretability of classifications, and (d) a sensitivity to unknown 
behavioral patterns. This was accomplished by integrating five components: Spatiotemporal Graph Convolutional 
Ensemble Validation, Recursive Multi -Resolution Adversarial C alibration, Topological Perturbation Stability 
Analysis, Hierarchical Attention Diffusion Validation, and Behavioral Manifold Conformity Indexing. Together these 
modules provide an ongoing analytical flow from raw traffic inputs to final risk-based decisions. The overall results 
across the three datasets — CICIDS2017, UNSW-NB15, and TON -IoT — are as follows: 98.31% accuracy, 97.89% 
precision, 97.64% recall, 97.76% F1 -Score and 99.02% AUC ROC on CICIDS2017. Additionally, the false positive 
rate was lowered to 2.47%. For UNSW-NB15, the macro-F1 score was increased to 96.21%, which includes 95.48% 
for DOS attacks, 95.72% for exploitation attacks and 94.83% for reconnaissance attacks. Lastly, the TON IoT dataset 
had an accuracy of 97.92%, an F1 -Score of 97.28%, and a Mean Class Correlation coefficient (MCC) of 0.958 with a 
detection latency of 19.6 ms. The framework has shown 92.18% recall for zero-day attacks and narrowed down the 
gap between cross -domain generalization to 5.46%. In addition to this, when subjected to adversarial and 
topological disturbances, it showed an average accuracy of 95.46% aga inst adversaries, an average accuracy of 
93.28% against nodes dropped, an average accuracy of 94.17% against edges tampered with, and a mean stability 
index of 0.928. Furthermore, it shows an Expected Calibration Error (ECE) of 0.028, an Explanation Fideli ty of 
96.73%, and an Outlier Detection F1 -Score of 92.41%, which all support the notion that this model provides 
dependable, interpretable, and deployment oriented intrusion detection. 
 
Future Scopes 
Future research should expand this framework to include distributed and continual security learning in cloud, edge 
computing, vehicular networks, and industrial automation environments. Federated optimization will allow 
collaborative model updating across multiple network domains while preserving data privacy during such updates. 
The use of dynamic graph transformers and online contrastive learning can further enhance adaptability to concept 
drifts, rapid changes in topology, and emerging zero -day attacks. Finally, the current calibration stage should be 
extended using both class conditional and uncertainty aware thresholds. Integrating threat intelligence feeds into 
the system along with MITRE ATT&CK mappings, automated response policies, and security opera tion center 
dashboards can help increase the systems operational usefulness. Compressing hardware aware methods like 
compression/quantization or early exit inference techniques can lower latency and memory consumption for 
constrained IoT gateway devices & deployments. 
 
Limitations 
The framework increases the computational/memory overhead compared to standalone lightweight classifiers. 
Performance of the framework relies heavily upon accurate graph construction, representative adversarial 
examples, and correct labeling at the protoco l layer. As well there exists potential limitations for generalizing the 
framework's performance due to cross -dataset feature differences, large disparities in class sizes and continually 
changing attack surfaces.

Data Availability Statement 
The datasets used in this study were adapted exclusively from publicly accessible academic repositories maintained 
by their originating institutions & deployments. The CICIDS2017 dataset was obtained from the Canadian Institute 
for Cybersecurity at the University of New Brunswick and is available at https://www.unb.ca/cic/datasets/ids-
2017.html. It provides labelled packet -capture files and flow -level CSV records containing benign traffic and 
multiple intrusion categories, including brute -force, denial -of-service, distributed denial -of-service, botnet, 
infiltration, port-scanning, and web-based attacks. 
The UNSW -NB15 dataset was obtained from the Cyber Range Laboratory of UNSW Canberra and is available at 
https://research.unsw.edu.au/projects/unsw-nb15-dataset. The dataset contains approximately 100 GB of 
captured network traffic and nine attack families: Fuzzers, Analysis, Backdoors, DoS, Exploits, Generic, 
Reconnaissance, Shellcode, and Worms. Its packet -level and flow -level attributes were adapted for multic lass 
intrusion detection, cross-dataset validation, and adversarial robustness analysis. 
The TON  IoT dataset was obtained from the official UNSW research repository and is available at 
https://research.unsw.edu.au/projects/toniot-datasets. It contains heterogeneous network traffic, IoT and 
Industrial IoT telemetry, and Windows and Linux operating -system observations suitable for evaluating 
behavioural conformity, topology variation, and zero-day attack detection. 
No proprietary, confidential, restricted, or non -open-source data supplied by a commercial firm, private 
organisation, or third party were used in the reported experiments. Consequently, separate organisational  
permission or data -access approval was not required. The datasets remain subject to the access conditions, 
attribution requirements, licences, and citation policies specified by their respective providers.  
All preprocessing procedures, feature transformations, graph construction mechanisms, adversarial -calibration 
operations, topology -perturbation scenarios, attention -diffusion analyses, behavioural -conformity computations, 
statistical evaluations, interpretations, and conclusions presented in the manuscript constitute the authors’ own 
analytical contributions. The authors have thoroughly reviewed the adapted material to ensure that the manuscript
