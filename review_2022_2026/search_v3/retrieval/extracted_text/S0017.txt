Abstract
As a critical information source in distributed systems and large-scale IoT 
infrastructures, log data serves as the foundation for fault diagnosis and security 
auditing. However, the explosive growth of unstructured and dynamically changing 
log data poses significant challenges for real-time big data analytics and system 
scalability. To overcome these performance bottlenecks, this paper proposes and 
optimizes an end-to-end log anomaly detection method tailored for large-scale log 
data processing. Specifically, to address template inconsistency across distributed 
environments, we propose a distributed Drain parsing algorithm featuring dynamic 
parse tree expansion and global incremental synchronization. Furthermore, we 
optimize the HDBSCAN algorithm by integrating the RTree spatial index and Boruvka 
minimum spanning tree (MST). This algorithmic breakthrough effectively eliminates 
computational bottlenecks in real-time processing and significantly enhances the 
capacity to capture non-spherical clusters. By proposing a fusion of cross-partition 
clustering and the GLOSH scoring mechanism, our method strongly supports 
intelligent analytics in distributed infrastructures. We validate the model on the Spark 
framework using HDFS, BGL, and OpenStack datasets. Experimental results show 
that our proposed method has good performance in terms of scalability, resolution 
efficiency and detection performance, making it highly relevant to modern Internet 
of Things and large-scale systems.

 1 Introduction
With the acceleration of digital transformation, distributed systems (e.g., cloud service 
clusters, IoT networks, and edge computing environments) have become key infra -
structures that support enterprises’ core businesses. As the digital fingerprints of sys -
tem operating states, distributed log data in modern IoT and large-scale systems records 
critical events such as node communications and resource scheduling, serving as the 
core basis for intelligent fault diagnosis and security auditing [1]. However, the explosive 
growth of unstructured and dynamically changing big data has imposed significant chal -
lenges for real-time big data analytics. According to statistics, modern cloud and IoT 
systems generate massive log streams, up to 50 GB per hour [ 2]. If the hidden transient

anomalies (e.g., DataNode communication interruptions and CPU overload) are not 
identified timely in such large-scale environments, they may trigger critical failures such 
as system downtime [3]. Therefore, developing an efficient, accurate, and highly scalable 
log anomaly detection mechanism has become critical to ensuring business continuity 
and empowering intelligent analytics in distributed infrastructures [4].
Current research on log anomaly detection faces the trilemma of efficiency, accuracy, 
and scalability. First, log data generally exhibits the feature of a mixture of constants and 
variables [ 5], and its format dynamically changes with system iterations. Traditional 
parsing methods (e.g., fixed regular expressions) fail to meet the requirements of real-
time parsing and dynamic adaptation, while single-machine algorithms cannot handle 
large-scale log streams [6]. Second, in terms of anomaly detection, existing methods can 
be divided into two categories. The first category is machine learning methods based 
on template counting. These methods suffer from reduced accuracy due to feature spar -
sity when handling high-dimensional log data (e.g., BGL includes more than 300 types 
of events) [ 7]. The second category is deep learning-based methods (e.g., DeepLog [ 8]). 
These methods can capture sequential dependencies but require substantial time for 
training and inference, and lack sensitivity to non-sequential density anomalies. In terms 
of deployment scenarios, although deep learning solutions can be scaled up via frame -
works such as TensorFlow Distributed [9], they rely on GPU cluster support, resulting in 
high hardware and operational costs. Moreover, the communication overhead caused by 
model parameter synchronization is relatively high, thereby impairing operational effi -
ciency in distributed scenarios.
To address the real-time processing and scalability challenges in IoT and large-scale 
big data environments, this paper proposes and optimizes an end-to-end log anomaly 
detection framework. By optimizing the HDBSCAN algorithm for distributed collabo -
ration, our method specifically tackles the computational bottlenecks of large-scale log 
streams, directly enhancing intelligent analytics. In our design, we explicitly address the 
theoretical and computational contradictions that arise when combining log parsing and 
clustering in distributed environments. We distinguish our foundational building blocks 
(adapted from prior work) from our core algorithmic novelties. The main contributions 
and the underlying synergy of this integration are as follows:
  • Original Algorithmic Novelty 1: Distributed Drain with Global Synchronization. 
While the baseline Drain algorithm [ 10] is utilized for single-machine parsing, 
naive parallelization leads to severe template drift. Our original contribution is the 
design of a dynamic parse tree expansion and global incremental synchronization 
mechanism. This ensures absolute semantic consistency across distributed nodes, 
providing high-quality, standardized feature inputs for the subsequent clustering 
stage.
  • Original Algorithmic Novelty 2: Optimized HDBSCAN and Cross-Partition Fusion. 
The traditional HDBSCAN algorithm [ 11] is theoretically robust but inherently 
suffers from an O (n2) complexity, rendering it impractical for big data processing 
even within Spark. Our original contribution is a dual-level optimization: locally, 
we integrate the RTree spatial index and Boruvka minimum spanning tree (MST) 
to reduce search complexity; globally, we propose a Gaussian kernel-based fusion 
strategy to merge cross-partition clusters. This novel integration directly solves the 
"data silo" problem inherent in distributed computing.

2 Related work
2.1 Log parsing techniques
Log parsing aims to convert unstructured text into structured templates, serving as the 
foundation for anomaly detection.
Clustering-based methods, such as Logcluster [12], identify frequent words using hash 
tables and construct candidate groups; LKE [ 13] employs a clustering method based on 
string edit distance. Frequent pattern mining-based methods, including the Logram [ 14] 
algorithm, extract log templates via an n-gram dictionary, while FT-tree [ 15] parses logs 
using frequency information. The iterative partitioning-based IPLoM [ 16] algorithm 
gradually refines log partitions through multiple iterations to obtain structured tem -
plates. The parse tree-based Drain [ 10] algorithm groups logs with a fixed-depth parse 
tree and extracts templates in combination with a trie tree, and TF-Tree also belongs to 
this category. The longest common subsequence-based Spell [17] algorithm manages log 
objects through LCSMap and implements log parsing by calculating the longest com -
mon subsequence between new logs and keys of existing logs. However, most current 
log parsing methods exhibit limited adaptability; they necessitate frequent parameter 
tuning when handling different log formats, which introduces instability into log parsing.
Among these approaches, the Drain algorithm is widely adopted due to its high accu -
racy. Nevertheless, since it is originally designed for single-machine environments, the 
traditional Drain algorithm exhibits limited processing capacity when handling large-
scale distributed logs. Moreover, its parallelization often leads to parsing errors caused 
by inconsistent template states across nodes.
2.2 Anomaly detection techniques
The core objective of anomaly detection is to identify abnormal patterns within struc -
tured log templates. Mainstream technical approaches for this task can be categorized 
into two types: clustering-based unsupervised methods and deep learning-based super -
vised/semi-supervised methods.
2.2.1 Clustering-based anomaly detection
Clustering-based anomaly detection methods mine the intrinsic distribution character -
istics of log data, group logs with similar patterns into clusters, and label samples deviat -
ing from cluster distributions as anomalies. Thanks to their advantages of not relying on

labeled data and being adaptable to low-label log scenarios, this category of methods has 
emerged as an important technical approach for log anomaly detection.
The Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) is a clustering method that assumes data origi -
nates from multiple Gaussian distributions and has been applied to real-time anomaly 
detection tasks [ 18]. Zeufack et al. [ 19] employed OPTICS clustering to learn frequent 
patterns from historical logs and identify anomalies based on vector distances. Wang 
et al. [ 20] combined HDBSCAN with spectral clustering to enhance the ability to clus -
ter log semantic correlations. For the OpenStack system, Niwa et al. [ 21] constructed a 
topological graph based on the parent–child and communication relationships among 
processes, combined K-means++ with MeanShift centroid clustering, and identified 
abnormal processes using process resource statistics. The Log2Vec framework proposed 
by Meng et al. [ 22] integrates lexical contrast information (synonyms and antonyms) 
and relational triples into word embeddings. This integration provides semantically 
rich feature inputs for clustering and effectively addresses the issue of semantic infor -
mation loss, which is caused by traditional clustering methods that rely solely on sta -
tistical features. Farzad et al. [ 23] attempted to combine Fuzzy C-means clustering with 
a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). They screened reliable samples using cluster centers 
to optimize the accuracy of anomaly determination. HDBSCAN [ 11] is a hierarchical 
density-based clustering algorithm. Although it can identify multi-density anomalies, 
the time complexity of its nearest neighbor search is as high as O (n2). In distributed 
scenarios, the algorithm is prone to significant performance degradation caused by data 
skew and lacks an effective cross-node correlation analysis mechanism.
2.2.2 Deep learning-based anomaly detection
With the advances in natural language processing (NLP) technology, deep learning-
based methods have gradually evolved from single sequence modeling to semantic 
enhancement, structure fusion, and large model applications.
In foundational research on sequence modeling and semantic understanding, Deep -
Log [8] pioneered the use of LSTM to capture the sequential dependencies of log indi -
ces but neglected semantic information. To address this limitation, LogAnomaly [ 24] 
introduced template2vec technology to capture both sequential and quantitative anoma-
lies simultaneously via semantic vectors. To tackle the challenge of scarce labeled data, 
PLELog [25] further combined GRU with probabilistic label estimation to achieve effi -
cient semi-supervised detection.
To solve the log parsing bottleneck and environmental noise problems, research -
ers have strived to improve the robustness and representation granularity of models. 
Le et al. [ 26] proposed directly modeling raw log text, thus breaking the limitation of 
the “parse-first, detect-later” paradigm. Building on this idea, the SEN network pro -
posed by Hariharan et al. [ 27] employed a subword-level attention mechanism to effec -
tively address the challenge of capturing out-of-vocabulary (OOV) words and complex 
semantic information. LogRobust [ 28] introduced an adversarial training mechanism to 
significantly enhance the model’s robustness against noise interference. In addition, Log-
Former [29] leveraged the long-range attention mechanism of Transformer to enhance 
the model’s generalization capability across different log domains.
In the cutting-edge exploration of multi-dimensional fusion and large model appli -
cations, research has begun to focus on the implicit structure and reasoning ability

between logs. Log2graphs [ 20] integrated log content and causal topological relation -
ships via a Dual Graph Convolutional Network (DualGCN). LogMS [ 30] further 
improved detection accuracy via a multi-source information fusion strategy. Recently, 
the LogLLM [ 31] framework innovatively combined BERT with Llama, leveraging the 
reasoning capabilities of large language models to provide new insights for complex 
fault diagnosis.Similarly, LLMeLog [ 32] utilizes the in-context learning capabilities of

higher-quality event representations for downstream anomaly detection models.
Although the aforementioned methods have achieved respective breakthroughs, 
reducing computational resource consumption while maintaining high detection accu -
racy remains a major challenge in current research.
2.3 Distributed log anomaly detection methods
To explicitly distinguish our algorithmic contributions and highlight the synergy of our 
proposed integration, it is essential to compare our work against recent distributed or 
hybrid log anomaly detection frameworks. Current distributed frameworks generally fall 
into several paradigms. First, some distributed clustering approaches rely on algorithms 
like K-Means deployed on Spark [33]. However, these methods inherently assume spher-
ical data distributions and require predefined cluster counts, which fail to represent the 
complex topological structures of modern log features. Our improved HDBSCAN over -
comes this by leveraging the Boruvka MST to capture non-spherical anomaly clusters 
dynamically without requiring a predefined k. Second, recent evaluations of distributed 
machine learning algorithms [ 34] demonstrate that supervised models (e.g., Logistic 
Regression, Random Forest on Spark) achieve high accuracy but critically rely on mas -
sive, manually labeled datasets. In contrast, our proposed framework maintains a fully 
unsupervised pipeline, identifying dynamic anomalies without prior label dependency.
Third, compared to hybrid frameworks that deploy distributed deep learning (such as 
Spark-based Autoencoders) [ 35], our approach avoids the severe communication over -
head and prolonged training latencies associated with neural network parameter syn -
chronization across nodes. By optimizing local nearest-neighbor searches with the 
RTree spatial index, we strictly bound our computational complexity to O (log n+ k), 
making it highly suitable for real-time high-concurrency streams.Finally, addressing the 
inherent "data silo" problem in distributed systems, existing real-time streaming anom -
aly detection systems [ 36] aggregate anomalies globally but often struggle with contex -
tual boundary loss across partitions. We specifically resolve this by proposing a Gaussian 
kernel-based cross-partition fusion strategy. Furthermore, while standalone distributed 
parsers like LogParser-SP [ 37] focus solely on expediting unstructured text conversion, 
our framework structurally synchronizes a distributed Drain parser (via global incre -
mental synchronization) directly with the downstream clustering module. This guaran -
tees that no template drift occurs across Spark nodes, proving that our framework is 
not merely an engineering combination, but a strongly coupled, end-to-end algorithmic 
synergy designed specifically for large-scale distributed infrastructures.

3 Proposed work
This section details the design of the proposed log anomaly detection framework. It 
is crucial to clarify that while existing algorithms (e.g., Drain, HDBSCAN, R-tree, and 
Spark) serve as foundational components, our framework is not a simple engineering 
concatenation of these tools. Instead, the core algorithmic novelty lies in resolving the 
inherent friction between distributed data processing and global anomaly correlation.
The strength of this specific integration stems from its complementary architecture: 
we rely on Spark for high-throughput ingestion, but recognize that distributed parti -
tioning destroys the global context required by clustering algorithms. To solve this, our 
modified distributed Drain algorithm establishes a synchronized global parsing stan -
dard. Concurrently, traditional HDBSCAN cannot handle the massive data blocks on 
Spark nodes due to its quadratic complexity. By integrating RTree spatial indexing and 
Boruvka MST construction, we algorithmically break this computational bottleneck. 
Finally, to address the "data silos" created by Spark partitions, we introduce a Gaussian 
kernel-based fusion layer.
As illustrated in Fig. 1, the overall architecture mainly consists of three core stages: 
first, we adopt the improved distributed Drain algorithm to perform parallel parsing and 
structuring of raw log streams; second, we introduce the RTree spatial index and Boru -
vka algorithm at local nodes to optimize HDBSCAN clustering; finally, we implement 
cross-partition cluster fusion via the Gaussian kernel function and generate multi-level 
anomaly scores based on the GLOSH mechanism.
3.1 Distributed improvements to the drain algorithm
Log parsing is a critical preprocessing step that converts unstructured text into struc -
tured data. The traditional Drain algorithm employs a fixed-depth parse tree, which per-
forms well in single-machine environments. However, in distributed scenarios, template 
drift or inconsistency issues frequently arise across different nodes due to the varying 
arrival order of log data. In addition, the static template mechanism fails to adapt effec -
tively to new log formats introduced by system upgrades.
To address the above limitations, we design a distributed parsing module that incorpo-
rates the mechanisms of dynamic parse tree expansion and global incremental synchro -
nization. First, during the parsing phase, we leverage Spark mapPartitions to generate 
local candidate templates in parallel on each Worker node. To eliminate template ambi -
guity in distributed environments, the Driver node introduces a global deduplication 
and synchronization mechanism based on Jaccard similarity. This mechanism uses

3.2 Statistical feature engineering for structured logs
Following the log parsing phase, structured log events must be converted into numerical 
vectors before clustering. As illustrated in Stage 2 of Fig. 1, instead of relying on sparse 
textual representations (such as one-hot encoding, TF-IDF, or semantic embeddings),

sliding windows. This statistical approach effectively captures the operational dynamics, 
temporal transitions, and severity distributions essential for distance-based clustering 
algorithms. Specifically, the feature extraction maps log templates to multi-dimensional

entropy.

3.3 Improved HDBSCAN algorithm
After obtaining structured log feature vectors, the traditional HDBSCAN algorithm can 
identify multi-density clusters, but its nearest neighbor search has a time complexity as 
high as O (n2), leading to severe computational bottlenecks when processing millions 
of log samples. In addition, traditional methods struggle to effectively handle data skew 
problems in distributed environments.
T o break through this efficiency bottleneck, this paper introduces RTree spatial index 
technology in the intra-partition clustering phase. Theoretically, traditional distance-
based clustering suffers from severe computational redundancy in metric spaces, 
requiring exhaustive pairwise Euclidean distance evaluations. By employing RTree, we 
project high-dimensional log vectors into a hierarchical spatial index based on Mini -
mum Bounding Rectangles (MBRs). The theoretical justification lies in the spatial prun -
ing property: when querying the k-nearest neighbors of a target point, the algorithm 
computes the distance to the MBRs rather than individual points. If the minimum 
distance to an MBR exceeds the current nearest neighbor radius, the entire subtree is 
mathematically pruned without precision loss. By aggregating spatially adjacent feature 
vectors through this bounding hierarchy, the upper bound of the time complexity for 
k-nearest neighbor search is strictly reduced from O (n2) to O (logn + k), which greatly 
improves query efficiency. Based on the optimized neighbor relationships, the algorithm

3.4 Global cluster fusion and multi-level anomaly determination
Although distributed computing improves processing speed, it also leads to the data silo 
phenomenon, where the same type of anomaly pattern may be split across different par -
titions, resulting in missed detections from a local perspective. To solve this problem, 
this paper designs a cross-partition cluster merging strategy based on Gaussian kernel 
function. The theoretical justification for selecting the Gaussian (Radial Basis Function, 
RBF) kernel, rather than linear distance thresholds, lies in its capacity to map finite-
dimensional cluster boundaries into an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. In distrib -
uted systems, cross-partition log anomalies often exhibit complex, non-linear geometric 
boundaries. The Gaussian kernel provides a continuous, strictly positive similarity mea -
sure that decays exponentially with spatial distance, thereby mathematically preventing 
the arbitrary cutoff problems caused by rigid distance thresholds.

mathematically distinguish between a genuine anomaly in a dense cluster and a normal 
data point residing in a globally sparse region. T o resolve this theoretical flaw, GLOSH

4 Results and discussion
To comprehensively verify the effectiveness of the proposed log anomaly detection 
method based on improved HDBSCAN and distributed collaboration, we conducted 
experiments focusing on three key metrics: parsing performance, detection accuracy, 
and efficiency. We utilized the publicly available HDFS, BGL, and OpenStack benchmark 
datasets from the LogHub repository [ 38] and strictly followed the universal evaluation 
specifications in the log anomaly detection field to ensure the objectivity and compara -
bility of the experimental results. LogHub also provides a small-scale dataset containing 
only 2000 log messages for each dataset [39], where each log message is manually labeled 
with its corresponding event template. We used these small-scale datasets to evaluate 
the log parsing accuracy.
4.1 Datasets
  • HDFS: A benchmark industrial dataset containing 1.2 billion log records (10GB) 
collected from an HDFS cluster over seven days, involving 10 DataNodes and one 
NameNode. It contains five types of labeled anomalies, such as “Block corruption” 
and “DataNode offline, ” with anomalies accounting for 3.2% of the total. Log 
sequences are naturally partitioned by block_id [5].
  • BGL (Blue Gene/L): Collected from the Blue Gene/L supercomputer at Lawrence 
Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL), which features 131,072 processors and 32TB 
of RAM. The dataset covers over 200 days of operation with 4,747,963 records, of 
which 348,460 are manually labeled as anomalies. As there are no explicit sequence 
labels, time-based sliding windows were used for extraction [40].
  • OpenStack: A collection of logs from core components like Nova and Neutron on 
the OpenStack cloud platform. It contains 207,820 temporal log records across 
normal and anomalous scenarios, including standardized fields such as timestamps, 
log levels, and component IDs.
4.2 Experimental setup
Hardware Environment: The distributed cluster consists of a Spark cluster (1 master 
node + 3 slave nodes) composed of 4 servers equipped with Intel Xeon (R) Gold 6240 
processors and 172 GB of RAM.

clustering algorithm, or those whose GLOSH anomaly scores exceed the normal thresh -

ber of log messages.
Efficiency Metrics: For the log parsing task, efficiency was measured by parsing time. 
For the anomaly detection task, time consumption was divided into training time and 
testing time. Among them, the proposed method is unsupervised, so only the clustering 
and anomaly determination time are counted, while supervised/semi-supervised meth -
ods recorded both model training time and inference time.

4.4.1 Log parsing performance
Log parsing is a critical preprocessing step that converts unstructured text into struc -
tured data, and its efficiency directly determines the usability of the system in large-scale 
distributed scenarios. Tables 1 and 2 show the performance comparison between the 
proposed improved distributed Drain algorithm and classical parsing algorithms (Drain, 
Spell, IPLoM) on the HDFS and BGL datasets.
In terms of parsing efficiency, benefiting from the Spark distributed architecture’s par-
allel processing capability and the RDD memory-disk hybrid persistence strategy, the 
proposed method achieved significant performance advantages. As shown in Table 1, 
on the HDFS dataset, the time consumption of the improved algorithm was only 66.7 s, 
which was about 4.9 times faster than the original single-machine Drain algorithm 
(325.7  s) and more than 10 times faster than the longest common subsequence-based 
Spell algorithm (676.45 s). On the BGL dataset with more complex log templates (Table 
2), this advantage remained evident, with the time consumption of only 48.5 s, far lower 
than that of the comparison algorithms.
In terms of parsing accuracy, despite the introduction of distributed sharding pro -
cessing, the proposed method effectively avoided template inconsistency problems in 
distributed environments through the mechanisms of "dynamic parse tree expansion" 
and "global incremental synchronization". Experimental data showed that the pars -
ing accuracy of the method reached 0.99 and 0.98 on HDFS and BGL datasets, respec -
tively, which was basically equivalent to that of the single-machine Drain algorithm and 
significantly higher than that of Spell (0.87). This result demonstrated that the method 
preserved the high-fidelity restoration capability of log semantic templates while sub -
stantially reducing the computational load.

the architectural superiority of distributed computing over centralized processing. Even 
accounting for advancements in hardware since the publication of these baselines, the 
transition from hour-level to second-level processing underscores the proposed frame -
work’s capability to break the computational bottlenecks inherent in big data analytics.

paper exhibits significant advantages in terms of efficiency. For the HDFS dataset, the 
total time consumption of the whole process was only 59.5  s. In contrast, the LSTM-

algorithmic speed race, this comparison highlights the difference between a "single-

In summary, while our empirical study demonstrates the necessity of tuning fixed 
window sizes to specific datasets, this manual approach inherently lacks generalization 
across heterogeneous systems. A short window of 1–3  min captures high-frequency 
transient anomalies effectively, whereas a long window of over 100 min is required for 
long-cycle cross-component transactions. To overcome the limitations of dataset-spe -
cific tuning, future iterations of this framework would greatly benefit from adaptive 
windowing methods and dynamic segmentation approaches. Unlike fixed manual win -
dows–which risk artificially truncating long-running transactions or diluting bursty 
transient faults with normal background logs–dynamic segmentation adjusts temporal 
boundaries autonomously based on real-time log arrival rates, session density, or seman-
tic contextual shifts. By integrating adaptive mechanisms, the system could dynamically 
scale its temporal receptive field, thereby achieving robust generalization across diverse 
system architectures without requiring manual hyperparameter optimization.

(25%, 50%, and 100% of the full dataset) and the number of RDD partitions (64, 128, 256, 
and 512). Strong scaling analysis, weak scaling analysis, and the impact of partitions are 
shown in Figs. 7, 8 and 9.

uted Drain and localized HDBSCAN optimizations effectively utilize parallel resources 
without severe communication bottlenecks.

5 Conclusion
Aiming at the challenges of low parsing efficiency, difficulty in identifying multi-type 
anomalies, and poor scalability in log anomaly detection for distributed systems, this 
paper proposes an end-to-end detection framework based on an improved HDBSCAN 
algorithm and distributed collaboration techniques. At the parsing level, we introduce 
the mechanisms of dynamic parse tree expansion and global incremental synchroniza -
tion, so that the improved distributed Drain algorithm effectively addresses the issues 
of template inconsistency and memory overflow. When combined with the Spark RDD 
persistence strategy, this algorithm significantly reduces computational overhead. At the 
detection level, the optimized HDBSCAN algorithm leverages the RTree spatial index to 
reduce the time complexity of nearest neighbor search to O(logn+k). Moreover, when 
combined with the fusion strategy integrating Boruvka MST and the Gaussian kernel 
function, this optimized algorithm breaks through the computational bottleneck in 
large-scale data processing and significantly enhances the ability to capture non-spher -
ical clusters and cross-node correlated anomalies. Experiments show that the proposed 
method delivers a parsing speed that is approximately 4.9 times faster than that of tradi -
tional methods on the HDFS, BGL, and OpenStack datasets, with the highest F1-score 
reaching 0.994, thus achieving dual improvements in both accuracy and efficiency. 
Future work will focus on three directions: first, introducing lightweight pre-trained 
models to enhance semantic understanding; second, exploring online incremental learn-
ing strategies to improve the model’s adaptability to system evolution;and third, devel -
oping adaptive dynamic windowing mechanisms to autonomously optimize temporal 
segmentation across diverse and heterogeneous cloud environments.
