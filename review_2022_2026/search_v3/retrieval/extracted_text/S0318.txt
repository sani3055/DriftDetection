ABSTRACT:
The complexity and sophistication of cyber-attacks have risen significantly due to the proliferation of network-based applications. Existing Intrusion Detection
Systems (IDS) are mainly black-box machine learning models which do not provide interpretability regarding the decisions made by the security analyst. In this
paper we present machine learning models combined with Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) methods, resulting in a system that performs explainable
intrusion detection. The System which is proposed utilizes Decision Tree, Random Forest and XGBoost classifiers trained in NSL-KDD dataset to classify the
network traffic into normal or attack. SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) and LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations) were used to
compute global and local predictions, respectively, to improve transparency and trust. The experimental results indicated that XGBoost achieved the best
accuracy of 80.43%, performing better than other models. The use of XAI methods helps security analysts to interpret feature importance, and understand the
behaviour of individual predictions so that the system is perceived not only as an accurate one but also transparent and trustworthy.
Keywords: Intrusion Detection System (IDS), Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI), Machine Learning, XGBoost, NSL-KDD Dataset
Introduction:
The number of cyberattacks is increasing, and the rapid growth of internet usage and network-based applications has made cybersecurity a concern.
IDS is a tool for monitoring network traffic as well as it identifies malicious activities within the system. During an attack, traditional IDS techniques
utilize signature-based detection that can effectively detect known attacks but are not suitable for novel or unknown threats. Hence, smart approaches
based on Machine Learning (ML) are increasingly focused to enhance the performance of intrusion detection.
Due to their capacity to learn patterns from network traffic data and make accurate predictions, machine learning models like Decision Tree, Random
Forest, and XGBoost are frequently utilized in IDS. However, these models are often opaque and make the explanations for their predictions difficult to
evaluate. The inability to explain model outputs makes automated intrusion detection systems less trustable, particularly in applications with high-
security requirements.
In other words, the AI can evaluate data and return an insight (for example, classification), but why it did so is a matter of concern at this stage due to
lack of interpretability. These techniques have been applied to detect the most significant properties for the purpose of intrusion detection, thus
enhancing trust and transparency. In this research, an explainable intrusion detection system is created based on Decision Tree and Random Forest and
XGBoost models over the NSL-KDD database. We assess the performance of the models and implement explainability methods to improve
interpretability and facilincy meaningful cybersecurity decisions.
Literature Review
Recently, a number of studies have used Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) techniques to improve the explanatory as well as predictive
capabilities of the Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS). In their study titled “Explainable Intrusion Detection Using XGBoost and SHAP”, Barnard et al.
[ 1] used XGBoost classifier algorithm along with SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) to propose an explainable IDS that can not only achieve
high accuracy but also can explain the importance of features which led to the classification of an attack by the model. The study demonstrated that the
XGBoost model along with SHAP as an explainability method proposed an explainable IDS that helps in gaining the required insights to the security
analyst about various types of attacks and how the model is classifying them.
In [2], Ikram and Huma proposed an explainable intrusion detection framework, where some popular interpretable machine learning algorithms, i.e.,
Decision Tree and Random Forest are utilized. In addition, SHAP (SHapley Value) explainability techniques are applied to enhance the interpretability

of the MLIDC without compromising the detection accuracy. The authors in this work demonstrated that explainable ML models can provide reliable
intrusion detection, while at the same time enhancing trust in the ML model and its predictions.
In [3], Grabowski and Xu proposed an explainable machine learning-based intrusion detection system. They applied ensemble models (Random Forest
and XGBoost) and explained the prediction results with SHAP and LIME. The proposed framework provides both global and local explanations for
intrusion detection and demonstrated that the ensemble learning models and explainable methods not only achieve a higher detection rate, but also a
higher transparency
of the intrusion detection model.
Gupta et al. in [4] have proposed an explainable intrusion detection approach by utilising machine learning algorithms coupled with SHAP and LIME
techniques. They pointed out the necessity of explaining the working of the AI/ML applications in the Cybersecurity domain. The experiments carried
out in their research proved that the explainable AI (XAI) techniques increased the interpretability of the model, thereby increasing the trustworthiness
of the IDS.
Ayan et al. [5] proposed a Human-Centered Explainable Intrusion Detection System using Machine Learning and Explanations techniques such as
SHAP. The aim was to not only enhance the detection rate of IDS but also to provide explanations so that the accuracy of IDS can be increased. They
also tested the effect of explainable IDS on enhancing transparency, reliability and efficiency in the field of cybersecurity and their experimental results
showed that the explainable IDS yields all the desired benefits.
Based on the literature review, the common machine learning algorithms that are being used in IDS include Decision Tree, Random Forest and
XGBoost to achieve higher accuracy for the intrusion detection. In addition, other explainable AI methods such as SHAP and LIME have also been
proposed to make the model more explainable. Hence, an explainable IDS that can combine the benefits of various machine learning algorithms while
evaluating the performance of the IDS on the standard datasets like NSL-KDD has not yet been proposed. Therefore, this research proposes an
explainable IDS that combines the ensemble model of Decision Tree, Random Forest and XGBoost machine learning algorithms with the SHAP and
LIME techniques to achieve higher accuracy for the IDS while maintaining the explainability of the model.
Significance of the Study
This paper is aimed at improving the performance of IDS using machine learning and explainable artificial intelligence (XAI). IDS traditionally rely on
machine learning and have few meaningful insights into their detection process. Hence, they behave like a “black-box” and lack the transparency
required for identifying the root causes of their alerting decisions. In this paper, we explore the possibilities of combining explanations techniques such
as SHAP and LIME with some common machine learning algorithms such as Decision Tree, Random Forest and XGBoost. This research work
proposes an explainable intrusion detection system (IDS) that explains which features are the most important for identifying an intrusion in a network
environment thereby making the system more trustworthy and reliable and enhancing decision making in the cybersecurity domain. The proposed
model was evaluated using the NSL-KDD dataset, a modified version of the KDD99 benchmark intrusion detection dataset. The NSL-KDD dataset is
one of the commonly used datasets in evaluating the performance of intrusion detection models. This research focuses on developing reliable and
interpretable intrusion detection systems (IDSs) to assist analysts in identifying and understanding attacks, which is highly practical research in the field
of network security.
Methodology
An Explainable Intrusion Detection System (IDS) was proposed in this paper, by combining machine learning models and explainable artificial
intelligence (XAI) methods to detect network attacks and to explain the types of attacks. The proposed approach is designed by five phases: dataset
collection, data preprocessing, model training, model evaluation and explainability analysis. Dataset Collection
The NSL-KDD dataset is used in this study. The NSL-KDD dataset is one of the widely used benchmark datasets for assessing the performance of the
Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS). The NSL-KDD dataset consists of data samples of network traffic and their corresponding labels which are either
normal or attack. The characteristics of the network traffic records in the NSL-KDD dataset are given by 41 attributes such as protocol category, service,
flag, duration, source bytes, destination bytes and so on. The NSL-KDD dataset is divided into training set and testing set for evaluating the
performance of the models.
Data Preprocessing
This step is used to prepare the data so that it can be learned by the machine learning algorithm. The categorical variables in the dataset are protocol,
service and flag which need to be encoded. So, they are encoded using Label Encoding. The output variable is also encoded to 0 and 1 where 0
represents the normal traffic and 1 represents the attack traffic. The encoded features now need to be scaled. StandardScaler is used for scaling the
features. This is done to improve the accuracy of the model. The dataset is divided into two variables X and y. X is the matrix that contains the features
of the dataset. y is the variable that contains the target or output of the dataset.
Model Training
The models of Decision Tree, Random Forest and XGBoost are established in the experiment. According to the previous research, the Decision Tree,
Random Forest and XGBoost models show a high accuracy in the classification process. The models are trained with the trained data and the
engineering characteristics of the data. The Decision Tree algorithm is a tree-based algorithm that determines the characteristic of the feature by
selecting the feature at each level based on the conditions of the features. The Random Forest algorithm generates a large number of decision trees and
uses them to improve the accuracy of the model and prevent overfitting. The XGBoost algorithm also uses the gradient boosting to improve the
performance of the model.
Performance Evaluation
Experiments were conducted by using the testing dataset to evaluate the performance of the proposed models. The accuracy, precision, recall and F1-
score are widely used as the evaluation metrics for intrusion detection. Furthermore, the confusion matrices are plotted to show the classification results
of the normal and attack traffic.

Explainability Analysis
In order to understand the behaviour of the models we used SHAP and LIME techniques on the trained models. SHAP gives us the relative contribution
of each feature in the output of the model and it gives us global and local explanations. LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations) is an
explanation technique that explains the reason upon which the detection was made for a given sample by providing the relative contribution of each
feature. So that the behaviour of the models is better understood and the IDS is more transparent and trustworthy.

Results and Discussions
Accuracy Analysis
The performance of ID system based on different machine learning models will be analyzed by applying three models: Decision Tree, Random Forest
and XGBoost. The criterion of classification model is accurate rate. The experimental results show that XGBoost obtains the highest accurate rate,
which is 80.43%, while the accurate rates obtained by Decision Tree and Random Forest are 78.81% and 77.07% separately.
XGBoost obtains the highest accurate rate due to its deeply learning characteristics of network traffic data and good classification performance of
malicious and normal samples.
Confusion Matrix Analysis
A confusion matrix was used to calculate the accuracy of the classification of the instances done by the models. From the confusion matrix it is derived
that all the models classify correctly a large number of normal and attack instances but also make some misclassifications in the normal as well as in the
attack class.
Among all the models the XGBoost model showed the highest accuracy as it had classified maximum attacks and it also had the least false alarms. So
XGBoost is the better algorithm to classify the intrusions and also to reduce the false alarms. From the confusion matrix it is clear that the tree-based
models are also performing well in case of the intrusion detection.

Classification Report The performance is evaluated with the precision, recall and F1-score metrics. Precision is the ratio of true positives to the sum of
true positives and false positives. Recall is the ratio of true positives to the sum of true positives and false negatives. The F1-score is the ratio of two
times the true positives to the sum of two times the true positives and the false positives and false negatives. From the experimental results, it can be
seen that the XGBoost model achieves a higher precision, recall and F1-score than the Decision Tree and Random Forest models, which indicates that
the XGBoost model can be used to distinguish normal and attack traffic. In addition, the experimental results show that the Decision Tree model
achieves a relatively high accuracy, because the decision tree model is characterized by a simple structure and clear reasoning track, so it is easy to
achieve high accuracy, while the Random Forest model realizes stable performance through multiple decision trees.

Explainable AI Analysis
To better inform the user of Intrusion Detection System outputs, we employed techniques known as Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) to
highlight the contributions of various features. We used this methodology on our top performing model, XGBoost, and produced results using SHAP
and LIME. This helps understand the overall operation of the Intrusion Detection System as well as the decisions made by it. This significantly
increases trust in the Intrusion Detection System.
SHAP-Based Global Interpretation
SHAP-Based Global Interpretation SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) is a game-theoretic approach which provides explanations of machine
learning models by feature importance values. In order to better understand the global behaviour of the employed XGBoost model, SHAP was utilized
to gain more insights into how the model makes predictions. The SHAP summary plot provided the basis to identify the most relevant features
influencing the classification towards an intrusive or non-intrusive activity as well as to examine the contributions of the selected features to the
classification accuracy. The results from the features’ inspection revealed that Feature 4, Feature 32, Feature 22, Feature 5, and Feature 33 have the
highest effect to the model’s decision function. It also globalises the feature importance, and gives positive values for features that indicate that the
traffic is an attack, and negative values for features that indicate normal traffic. This makes it easier to understand how the model works and which
features are the most important for the classification task.

LIME-Based Local Interpretation
In this paper, we employ LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations) to locally and tractably learn an interpretable surrogate model of a
complex model to explain a specific prediction. We employ LIME to locally and accurately interpret the XGBoost based traffic classification results.
For an instance chosen in this study, XGBoost classifies the network traffic into an attack with a probability of 1.00. The features including
protocol_type, service, num_root, hot, num_failed_logins, and is_guest_login contributed most to the prediction outcome. In order to gain local, feature
level insights in how the trained IDS is making its predictions, we utilized LIME to provide more clarity for the security analyst as to why an IDS alert
was generated for a specific network traffic flow. This additionally provided insight into the learned decision rule(s) of the model.
LIME-Based Local Explanation of Predictions
In order to better understand the decision-making mechanism of the IDS, both SHAP for global interpretability and LIME for local interpretability are
adopted in this paper, and are referred to as an explanation framework.
By incorporating such techniques into an IDS, it is no longer a ‘black-box’ and becomes more interpretable and thus more reliable and useful.
Interpretable models are crucial for effective usage in real-world cybersecurity scenarios, where understanding why a specific decision has been made
is of utmost importance.
Conclusion
This paper proposes an explainable IDS by developing machine learning model based on NSL-KDD dataset. For predicting performance of Decision
Tree, Random Forest and XGBoost classifiers, highest accuracy and best results for classification were achieved by XGBoost. In order to enhance
interpretability of proposed IDS, explanations were achieved by applying global and local explainable approaches such as SHAP and LIME. SHAP
highlights top contributing features for global explanation whereas, LIME is used to produce local, interpretable explanations of specific predictions by
