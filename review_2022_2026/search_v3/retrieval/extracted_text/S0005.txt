Abstract
Machine Learning (ML)-based Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) are critical for defending against cyber threats but remain
vulnerable to adversarial attacks, posing risks to system reliability and security. This study investigates the transferability
of adversarial attacks across XGBoost and LightGBM models in IDS and uses Explainable AI (XAI) techniques to identify
features that inﬂuence model decisions. Experiments on the CICIDS-2017 and NSL-KDD datasets demonstrate cross-model
adversarial transferability, with accuracy reductions of 8.75% and 10.59% for XGBoost and LightGBM, respectively. The
XAI-informed analysis further shows that feature-importance patterns can be used to examine model vulnerability under
adversarial perturbations. Under direct adversarial testing, XGBoost exhibited a 6.18% reduction in accuracy, illustrating
the vulnerability associated with perturbations targeting highly ranked features. Building on prior research linking feature
importance to adversarial transferability, this study operationalizes that relationship in the IDS domain through a heuristic
formulation based on correlations between model-speciﬁc feature-importance vectors. The proposed formulation is intended
as a practical approximation for assessing cross-model vulnerability rather than as a deterministic predictor.

The main contributions of this work are as follows:
• We investigate adversarial transferability between two
structurally related gradient-boosting models, XGBoost
and LightGBM, using the CICIDS-2017 and NSL-KDD
datasets.
• We use SHAP and LIME to identify model-relevant fea-

• We introduce a heuristic quantitative formulation that

• We empirically compare the heuristic estimate with the

• We provide an IDS-speciﬁc operationalization of the
broader feature-importance–transferability relationship,
with the scope deliberately restricted to gradient-boosting
tree models.

2 Related work
The domain of intrusion detection has evolved signiﬁ-
cantly with the integration of machine learning techniques,
adversarial methods, and explainability approaches. This
section reviews the relevant literature across four key areas:
fundamental intrusion detection systems, machine learn-
ing applications within IDS, adversarial attacks targeting
these systems, and the role of explainable AI in enhancing
security solutions. The section then highlights the open ques-
tions and research challenges in the ﬁeld. In particular, this
section traces the lineage of feature-importance-based adver-
sarial transferability, from its origins in the computer-vision
domain to its recent applications in network intrusion detec-
tion.
2.1 Intrusion detection systems
Intrusion detection systems (IDS) have evolved signiﬁcantly
to address the growing sophistication of cyber threats. These
systems monitor and analyze network or system behav-
ior to identify suspicious activities, classiﬁed primarily as
host-based or network-based depending on their deployment
location [ 12]. Network-based IDS (NIDS) examine trafﬁc
at network segments, while host-based IDS (HIDS) ana-
lyze indicators such as processes and log ﬁles on individual
machines [ 13]. Modern IDS implementations have transi-
tioned from signature-based approaches, which struggle with
novel threats, to more advanced anomaly-based techniques
that model normal behavior and ﬂag deviations [ 14].
The integration of Big Data Analytics has transformed
how organizations handle the overwhelming volume of net-

2.2 ML techniques in IDS
Machine learning approaches have been widely adopted in
IDS to improve detection capabilities. Traditional ML algo-
rithms such as Support V ector Machines (SVM), Random
Forest (RF), and K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) have demon-
strated effectiveness in various intrusion detection scenarios
[16]. These approaches typically rely on feature engineering
to identify relevant patterns in network trafﬁc data.
Decision tree-based ensemble methods, particularly Light-
GBM and XGBoost, have shown high performance in
intrusion detection tasks due to their ability to handle
high-dimensional data with complex relationships. Light-
GBM’s leaf-wise tree growth strategy offers computational
advantages over XGBoost’s level-wise approach, making it
particularly suitable for large-scale IDS applications [ 17].
While traditional models such as Support V ector Machines
(SVM) continue to be a signiﬁcant and widely adopted
approach in the literature, gradient-boosting ensembles pro-
vide shared feature-importance estimation mechanisms that
facilitate a controlled study of adversarial transferability.
Deep learning techniques have also been applied to intru-
sion detection, with architectures such as Convolutional Neu-
ral Networks (CNN), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM),
and hybrid models demonstrating promising results. For
instance, Lo et al. [ 18] developed a CNN–LSTM hybrid
model that achieved nearly 100% detection accuracy with
minimal false alarm rates for various in-vehicle network
attacks. Similarly, Maheswari et al. [ 19] proposed an opti-
mal cluster-based IDS that achieved 95.01% accuracy on
the KDD Cup’99 dataset by combining Multi-segment with
Chaotic Manta-ray Foraging Optimization.
2.3 Adversarial attacks on IDS
Adversarial attacks on intrusion detection systems exploit
vulnerabilities in machine learning models to evade detec-
tion. These attacks can be categorized as white-box attacks,
where attackers have complete knowledge of the target sys-
tem, or black-box attacks, where attackers have limited
information [1]. Research by Roshan et al. [20] demonstrated

The transferability of adversarial attacks presents a partic-

Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have been uti-

IDS robustness. Y ang et al. [22] introduced DA-GAN, a sys-

increasing detection rates from approximately 60% to over
99% in various scenarios. While these studies have advanced
the understanding of adversarial robustness, they have pri-
marily focused on detection performance without addressing
the interpretability of such attacks.
Recent research emphasizes that Explainable AI (XAI)
techniques can play a dual role in adversarial contexts [ 23,
24]. On one hand, explainability methods such as SHAP and
LIME can inadvertently expose critical features most inﬂu-
ential to model predictions, thereby assisting adversaries in
crafting more targeted perturbations [ 25–27]. On the other
hand, these same tools can be used defensively to identify and
mitigate model weaknesses by revealing sensitive regions in
the feature space [ 9, 28–30].
2.4 Feature-importance-based adversarial
transferability
A distinct line of research links the importance a model
assigns to features with the transferability of adversarial
examples across models. This subsection traces that lineage,
from its conceptual origin in the computer-vision domain to
its recent application in network intrusion detection, in order
to position the present contribution within it.
Only limited work has explored how feature-importance
correlations inﬂuence adversarial transferability between
models. The conceptual foundation for this direction orig-
inates in the computer-vision domain: Wang et al. [ 31]p r o -
posed the Feature Importance-aware Attack (FIA), arguing
that feature importance is strongly correlated with the under-
lying objects of interest and that this correlation remains
largely invariant across different models, which makes
importance-guided perturbations transfer more effectively.
Our work extends this insight to the intrusion-detection
domain along two axes. First, the purpose differs: FIA gener-
ates adversarial images that disrupt critical features, whereas
we use feature-importance similarity to approximate the
transferability of adversarial samples between detectors. Sec-
ond, the importance signal differs: FIA derives importance

from aggregate gradients over feature maps of a source
model, requiring internal access, whereas we rely on post-
hoc, model-agnostic XAI (SHAP and LIME), which is better
suited to the black-box setting typical of network intrusion

dation by operationalizing a heuristic connection between
explainability-based feature relevance and the transferabil-

A closely related line of work uses XAI to guide adver-
sarial attack generation against NIDS. Okada et al. [ 11]p r o -
posed an XAI-driven black-box attack in which KernelSHAP
identiﬁes the features most inﬂuential to a deep-learning
detector, and perturbations are then crafted in the problem
space to evade detection. Our study differs from this work in
three respects. First, the target model class differs: Okada et
al. attack deep feedforward detectors, whereas we focus on
gradient-boosting tree ensembles. Second, the objective dif-
fers: their framework is concerned with generating successful
evasions for a single target, whereas our formulation aims to
approximate how readily an adversarial example transfers
between two models. Third, the explainability mechanism
differs: they rely on KernelSHAP alone, whereas we combine
SHAP and LIME to characterize feature-importance similar-
ity across models.
The work most closely related to ours is ETA, the
Explainable Transfer-based Black-Box Adversarial Attack
framework of Zhang et al. [ 32]. ETA shares our central
premise that adversarial transferability arises when mod-
els rely on similar important features, and, like the present
study, it targets NIDS, uses CIC-IDS2017, and includes tree-
based detectors. Despite this overlap, the two studies are
oriented toward different ends. First, in terms of use case,
ETA is an attack- generation framework: it produces trans-
ferable adversarial examples through an Important-Sensitive
Feature Selection procedure grounded in cooperative game
theory and perturbation interpretation. Our objective is not
to generate stronger attacks but to approximate how readily
an adversarial example transfers between detectors. Sec-
ond, in terms of formulation, ETA explains transferability
qualitatively but does not provide a quantitative approxima-
tion, whereas we introduce an explicit heuristic formulation
(Eq. 7) that approximates transferability from the rank corre-
lation of feature-importance vectors. Third, in terms of model
scope, ETA spans the broader ML space (including neu-
ral networks and tree ensembles), whereas we deliberately
restrict our analysis to structurally related gradient-boosting
models, which share a common split-gain importance mech-
anism and thus permit a controlled study of how importance
similarity governs transfer. The present work can therefore
be read as operationalizing, in a narrower and more analyti-
cally controlled setting, the feature-sharing insight examined
by ETA and earlier feature-importance-aware transferability
research.
Across this line of work, feature importance is con-
sistently treated as a factor associated with cross-model
transferability, yet existing studies use this insight to gener-
ate attacks rather than to estimate transferability in advance.
The present work addresses that gap by operationalizing the
feature-importance/transferability relationship as an explicit
heuristic formulation for approximating cross-model trans-
ferability, with scope restricted to gradient-boosting detec-
tors for a controlled analysis.
2.5 XAI applications in IDS
Explainable Artiﬁcial Intelligence (XAI) has emerged as
a critical approach for enhancing the interpretability and
trustworthiness of IDS. Arisdakessian et al. [ 33] conducted
a comprehensive survey of XAI techniques applied to
anomaly-based IDS within IoT networks, concluding that
XAI signiﬁcantly enhances trust and conﬁdence in cyber
defense mechanisms.
SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) and LIME (Local
Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations) have been widely
applied to interpret IDS decisions. Cui et al. [ 10] discovered
that using these XAI techniques on IDS greatly improved
clarity, particularly in distinguishing normal activities from
attacks like Denial of Service (DoS). Their research revealed
that global explanation methods, such as Permutation Impor-
tance and SHAP , boosted model performance by identifying
the most important features.
Adversarial attacks on XAI explanations themselves rep-
resent an emerging research area. Studies have shown that
explanation methods can be vulnerable to manipulations that
preserve model outputs while signiﬁcantly altering the expla-
nations provided to users [7]. This vulnerability underscores
the importance of developing robust explanation methods
that can withstand adversarial attempts to obscure or mis-
lead interpretations of model behavior.
Recent research has also highlighted that XAI methods
themselves may be susceptible to adversarial manipulations
[23, 34]. Attackers can exploit explanation algorithms to pro-
duce misleading yet plausible explanations that preserve the
original model output while concealing malicious perturba-
tions [7].
While these studies collectively illustrate the value of
XAI in understanding and improving IDS performance,
they rarely examine the relationship between explanation-
based feature importance and the transferability of adver-
sarial attacks across different model architectures. Hence,
this research integrates SHAP and LIME within a uni-
ﬁed adversarial framework, establishing a heuristic connec-
tion between feature importance similarity and cross-model
adversarial success, thereby linking interpretability directly
to robustness in IDS.

2.6 Open issues and research challenges
Despite signiﬁcant advancements, several challenges persist
in developing robust and interpretable intrusion detection
systems. High-quality datasets remain essential for validating
IDS models, yet public datasets often contain missing values,
incomplete features, or redundant information that reduces
model efﬁcacy [35]. Additionally, detection methods require
retraining with minimal time investment to identify emerging
threats.
The computational cost of training deep learning mod-
els presents another challenge, particularly for real-time
applications where rapid detection is crucial. Feature selec-
tion techniques that reduce dimensionality while preserving
discriminative information are essential for optimizing per-
formance [36]. Overﬁtting remains a signiﬁcant concern with
black-box models like Random Forest, SVM, and CNN,
which despite achieving excellent detection rates, often lack
transparency.
Transparency and interpretability have emerged as critical
requirements for modern IDS, as security analysts increas-
ingly base decisions on system recommendations. Develop-
ing novel XAI approaches that provide clear explanations
of model behavior represents a signiﬁcant opportunity for
improving trust and understanding in intrusion detection sys-
tems [37].
3 Methodology
This research employs an approach to investigate adversar-
ial attack transferability across different machine learning
models in intrusion detection systems. Our methodology
encompasses framework design, data preparation, adversar-
ial example generation, and explainability techniques. The
approach is designed to evaluate how adversarial samples
created for one model architecture can affect another, while
using explainable AI methods to identify vulnerable features
and understand attack mechanisms. The following subsec-
tions detail each component of our experimental design and
analytical framework.
3.1 Proposed framework
The proposed framework integrates LightGBM and XGBoost
models with explainable AI techniques to develop a robust
intrusion detection system capable of withstanding adversar-
ial attacks. These two algorithms were deliberately selected
because both are gradient-boosting ensemble frameworks
that build additive decision trees through iterative opti-
mization. They share similar feature-importance estimation
mechanisms based on split gain, which enables a controlled
analysis of how feature-importance similarity inﬂuences
adversarial transferability. While traditional models such
as Support V ector Machines (SVM) and Random Forests
(RF) are widely and successfully adopted in intrusion
detection research, offering high performance across vari-
ous network contexts, they utilize fundamentally different
decision-making structures (e.g., margin maximization in
kernel space or bagging). Consequently, evaluating such
heterogeneous models is beyond the scope of the current
study, as our framework focuses speciﬁcally on transfer-
ability guided by the interpretable patterns shared between
gradient-boosting architectures.
As illustrated in Fig. 3, the system employs a multi-stage
approach combining adversarial testing with XAI-informed
defense mechanisms. The system uses the CICIDS-2017
and NSL-KDD datasets to train the LightGBM model for
detecting normal and malicious network activities. XAI
approaches, speciﬁcally SHAP and LIME, are utilized to
determine signiﬁcant features inﬂuencing prediction results.
These feature importance rankings guide the heuristic-based
search methodology that creates adversarial samples by mod-
ifying vital features to produce inputs which deceive the
model without altering semantic content.
The IDS is evaluated by testing its performance against
these adversarial samples, tracking changes in precision,
accuracy, recall, and F
1 score metrics. The framework
also examines adversarial attack transferability by testing
XGBoost-derived adversarial samples against LightGBM
to identify vulnerabilities stemming from shared decision
boundary areas. This approach allows for a comprehensive
assessment of model robustness while maintaining inter-
pretability.

3.2.1 Data preprocessing
Data preprocessing plays a crucial role in ensuring adver-
sarial attack effectiveness. The implemented preprocessing
pipeline includes:
1. Data cleaning by removing constant features and quasi-
constant features.
2. Applying ordinal encoding to convert non-numeric fea-
tures into numerical representations.
3. Normalizing numeric features using min-max scaling.
4. Identifying and retaining the most relevant features for
model training.
3.3 Adversarial example generation
The research employs a black-box heuristic search approach
for generating adversarial samples that target critical features
identiﬁed through XAI methods. This approach is particu-
larly suitable for real-world scenarios where attackers lack
complete knowledge of the target model’s architecture and
parameters.
3.3.1 Heuristic search method
The heuristic algorithm iteratively modiﬁes input features
to ﬁnd instances that cause model misclassiﬁcation. Unlike
gradient-based methods that require access to model inter-
nals, this approach relies on feature importance rankings
from SHAP and LIME to guide the search process. The
algorithm systematically perturbs high-importance features
within predeﬁned constraints to maximize the probability of
misclassiﬁcation while maintaining the semantic integrity of
the original data.
The perturbations are subject to L
p norm constraints
to ensure that the modiﬁcations remain imperceptible and
realistic. Additionally, feature consistency constraints are
imposed to preserve the logical relationships between net-
work trafﬁc features, ensuring that the adversarial samples
represent plausible network trafﬁc patterns.
3.3.2 Transferability testing
To evaluate the transferability of adversarial attacks, the
methodology involves:
1. Identifying critical features driving prediction results in
the source model (XGBoost) through SHAP analysis.
2. Creating adversarial samples by manipulating signiﬁcant
features to deceive the source model.
3. Testing these adversarial samples against the target model
(LightGBM).
4. Measuring performance degradation using standard met-
rics (accuracy, precision, recall, F
1 score).
5. Analyzing how the target model processes transferred
adversarial samples using interpretability tools.
This approach examines whether shared decision pat-
terns between models enable adversarial samples designed
for one system to cause misclassiﬁcation in another, provid-
ing insights into common vulnerabilities across different IDS
architectures.

4 Results
This section presents the ﬁndings from the XAI-based
feature-importance analysis, the empirical assessment of
the proposed formulation, performance measurements under
adversarial conditions, and the comparison with existing
approaches.
4.1 XAI Feature importance analysis
The SHAP and LIME analyses revealed distinct feature
importance patterns across both datasets. In the CICIDS2017
dataset, the SHAP summary plot showed that Feature 17
exerted the strongest inﬂuence on model predictions, fol-
lowed by Features 9 and 1, while Features 10 and 5 demon-
strated minimal impact. Figure 4 illustrates these global
importance rankings.
Local SHAP analysis for individual samples revealed that
“Bwd Packet Length Max" and “Init_Win_bytes_backward"
were the most inﬂuential features directing predictions
toward Class 0. The feature “min_seg_size_forward" showed
some support for Class 1 but was outweighed by negative
features. Similar patterns were observed in LIME explana-
tions, with “Bwd Packet Length Max" ( − 0.66) and “URG
Flag Count" (− 0.28) demonstrating substantial inﬂuence on
directing predictions toward Class 0.
For the NSL-KDD dataset, SHAP analysis identiﬁed Fea-
ture 36 as the most critical for model predictions, with
Features 53 and 21 also showing signiﬁcant inﬂuence,
where features 31 and 33 exhibited the least impact (Fig. 5).
Local explanations for NSL-KDD samples revealed that
“service_remote_job" (0.30) and “service_domain" (0.26)
strongly inﬂuenced predictions toward Class 1, while “ser-
vice_hostnames" (− 0.69) and “hot" ( − 0.09) acted as nega-
tive inﬂuences toward Class 0.
The effectiveness of SHAP and LIME in identifying
vulnerable features connects to the theoretical concept of
“feature importance sensitivity" described by Ribeiro et al.
[38], who theorized that explanation methods can reveal
model reliance on speciﬁc features that make them suscep-
tible to manipulation. Our ﬁndings empirically support this
theory, demonstrating that features with the highest SHAP
values were indeed the most effective targets for adversarial
manipulation, conﬁrming the link between feature impor-
tance and adversarial vulnerability.
While the proposed feature-importance analysis high-
lights the effectiveness of XAI techniques in identifying
vulnerable features, an operational formulation is needed
to examine how feature-importance similarity may relate to
adversarial transferability across IDS architectures. Build-
ing on these empirical observations, we introduce a heuristic
formulation that approximates this relationship.

Performance metrics for both XGBoost and LightGBM mod-

accuracy and LightGBM reaching 96.22%. However, fol-

degraded signiﬁcantly. XGBoost’s accuracy dropped to

The adversarial sample generation experiment provided
further insights into attack effectiveness. From 10,000 test
samples in the NSL-KDD dataset, 3,256 adversarial samples
were successfully created (32.56%), with an average pertur-

4.4 Transferability of adversarial attacks
Experiments on attack transferability revealed that adversar-
ial samples generated for one model could successfully fool
another model architecture. On the CICIDS2017 dataset,
performance metrics for both XGBoost and LightGBM
decreased substantially when exposed to adversarial samples
created for the other model. Table 2 presents these ﬁndings.
XGBoost’s accuracy decreased by 8.75 percentage points
under transferred attacks, while LightGBM exhibited a
more signiﬁcant drop of 10.59 percentage points. Simi-
lar degradation patterns were observed for the NSL-KDD
dataset, though with slightly less impact: XGBoost’s accu-
racy decreased from 0.9671 to 0.9021 ( − 6.5 percentage
points), and LightGBM’s from 0.9663 to 0.8846 (− 8.17 per-
centage points).
The results conﬁrm the existence of shared vulnerabilities
between different model architectures, particularly for com-
plex datasets like CICIDS2017. The transferability success
indicates that attackers can effectively target multiple IDS
implementations using adversarial samples generated for a
single model, posing signiﬁcant security concerns.
4.5 Comparison with existing approaches
While various studies have investigated adversarial robust-
ness in machine learning-based IDS, direct quantitative
comparisons remain challenging due to variations in prepro-
cessing, feature selection, and evaluation protocols. Many
studies [ 2, 6, 39] have demonstrated the vulnerability of
models such as XGBoost, Random Forest, and Deep Neu-
ral Networks (DNN) to adversarial perturbations on the
CICIDS-2017 and NSL-KDD datasets.
The proposed approach, which integrates a dual-XAI
framework using both SHAP and LIME, observed a perfor-
mance degradation of 6.18%. Rather than claiming absolute
superiority, we highlight that this resilience is achieved by
leveraging explainability-guided insights during the model
evaluation phase. Unlike existing works that primarily uti-
lize a single interpretability method, our dual-XAI approach
provides a more comprehensive identiﬁcation of vulnerable
feature regions. Furthermore, the choice of LightGBM facil-
itates a leaf-wise tree growth strategy that offers speciﬁc

Traditional gradient-based adversarial attacks, such as the
Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM) and the Jacobian-based
Saliency Map Attack (JSMA), are common benchmarks in
the literature. However, these attacks fundamentally rely
on direct access to model gradients, making them suitable
primarily for white-box scenarios. Our study focuses on
explainability-driven transferable attacks where perturba-
tions are guided by feature importance scores instead. This
enables a realistic black-box transferability setting, as the
attack model does not require access to the target model’s
internal parameters. Empirical studies by Jmila et al. [ 40]
have shown that gradient-based methods like FGSM often
yield limited cross-model transferability compared to strate-
gies guided by feature interpretability.
Recent research by Sharma [ 41] evaluated query-based
black-box attacks, including Zeroth-Order Optimization
(ZOO) and HopSkipJump. While these methods can achieve
high success rates, they are computationally intensive, often
requiring tens of thousands of queries, which limits their
applicability to real-time intrusion detection systems. In con-
trast, the proposed SHAP/LIME-guided framework gener-
ates perturbations based on interpretable feature-importance
patterns rather than iterative boundary exploration.
5 Discussion
The experimental results demonstrate signiﬁcant vulnerabil-
ities in machine learning-based intrusion detection systems
when subjected to adversarial attacks. The ﬁndings align with
fundamental frameworks in adversarial machine learning.
123

According to the linearity hypothesis proposed by Goodfel-
low et al. [42], machine learning models exhibit vulnerability
to adversarial samples because they behave approximately
linearly in high-dimensional spaces. The successful gen-
eration of adversarial samples with minimal perturbations
(average Euclidean distances of 1.82 and 1.67 for NSL-KDD
and CICIDS2017, respectively) empirically supports this the-
oretical prediction.
The performance degradation observed in both XGBoost
and LightGBM models corresponds to Szegedy’s [ 43] foun-
dational observation that imperceptible perturbations can
cause models to misclassify inputs with high conﬁdence.
This phenomenon manifested in the current study with
66.9% and 63.9% of attack samples being incorrectly labeled
as benign after adversarial manipulation in the NSL-KDD
and CICIDS2017 datasets, respectively. The susceptibil-
ity to black-box attacks using heuristic search methods is
particularly concerning from a security perspective, as it
demonstrates that attackers do not require detailed knowl-
edge of model architecture to craft effective adversarial
samples.
The transferability results provide empirical evidence sup-
porting Tramèr’s [44] theoretical work on shared adversarial
subspaces. According to this theory, different models trained
on similar data develop overlapping decision boundaries,
creating common vulnerabilities that adversarial samples
can exploit regardless of speciﬁc model architecture. The
observed transferability between XGBoost and LightGBM
models—with accuracy reductions of 8.75 and 10.59 per-
centage points respectively when exposed to transferred
attacks—conﬁrms the existence of these shared blind spots,
suggesting the possibility of “universal" adversarial samples
capable of evading multiple IDS implementations simulta-
neously.
The effectiveness of feature-importance-guided attacks
aligns with the theoretical concept of “feature importance
sensitivity" described by Ribeiro et al. [ 38], who proposed
that explanation methods reveal model reliance on speciﬁc
features that make them susceptible to manipulation. The
current ﬁndings empirically support this proposal, as features
with the highest SHAP values proved to be the most effective
targets for adversarial perturbation. This connection between
explainability and vulnerability represents a fundamental
tension in ML-based security systems: the same features that
enable effective classiﬁcation also create exploitable weak-
nesses.
The formulation presented in this study builds on previ-
ous work by operationalizing a possible relationship between
feature-importance similarity and adversarial transferabil-
ity. The high Spearman correlation (0.831) between the
XGBoost and LightGBM feature-importance vectors indi-
cates substantial similarity in their feature rankings. This
similarity is consistent with, but does not by itself establish,
the observed cross-model transferability. This formulation
provides a probabilistic heuristic for approximating transfer-
ability between the structurally similar model pair evaluated
in this study. The reasonable agreement between the heuristic
estimate (91.55%) and the experimental result (86.4%) sup-
ports the utility of the framework for assessing cross-model
vulnerability while also indicating that the approximation
does not replace direct empirical evaluation. This ten-
sion relates to what Papernot et al. [ 45] described as the
“transferability-security" trade-off, where model properties
that enhance generalization also facilitate adversarial trans-
ferability.
From a theoretical perspective, the integration of XAI
methods like SHAP and LIME represents an application
of what Miller [ 46] terms “explanatory debugging"—using
explanations to identify and mitigate model weaknesses.
The consistency of feature importance rankings across these
methods supports their reliability as interpretability tools
while revealing potential pathways for defensive strategies.
This aligns with theoretical work by Kaur et al. [ 47], who
established that explanation methods can serve as practical
tools for model diagnosis and improvement.
While this study focuses on gradient-boosting architec-
tures due to their shared importance estimation mechanisms,
the heuristic framework provides a basis for future explo-
rations into how feature relevance informs robustness in
heterogeneous models such as SVM or Random Forest.
6 Conclusion
This research has presented an explainability-informed
framework for analyzing adversarial vulnerability and
approximating cross-model transferability in intrusion detec-
tion systems. The ﬁndings demonstrate that while machine
learning-based IDS can achieve high accuracy under normal
conditions, they remain vulnerable to adversarial manipula-
tion, particularly when attackers target features identiﬁed as
important through explainability methods.
The key contributions of this work include:
• Developing explainable XGBoost and LightGBM-based
IDS models that achieve baseline accuracies of 97.41%
and 96.22%, respectively, while providing explanations
through SHAP and LIME.
• Demonstrating model vulnerability to adversarial manip-
ulation, with successful adversarial samples generated
for 32.56% of the NSL-KDD test instances and 30.1% of
the CICIDS-2017 test instances.
• Demonstrating adversarial transferability between the
evaluated XGBoost and LightGBM models, highlight-
ing shared vulnerabilities within the studied gradient-
boosting setting.

159 Page 14 of 15 A. Abdlatef et al.
• Using SHAP and LIME to identify features associated
with adversarial vulnerability and to support the inter-
pretation of model behavior under direct and transferred
attacks.
• Operationalizing the relationship between feature
-importance similarity and adversarial transferability
through a probabilistic heuristic for approximating cross-
model vulnerability within the evaluated gradient
-boosting model pair.
Despite these advancements, several limitations and oppor-
tunities for future work remain. The current approach relies
primarily on static datasets, which may not fully capture the
dynamic nature of network trafﬁc in operational environ-
ments. Additionally, while the proposed defense mechanisms
show promise in increasing robustness, they do not com-
pletely eliminate vulnerability to adversarial attacks.
Some future research directions we believe that researchers
should focus on are as follows:
• Developing advanced data augmentation strategies using
generative models like GANs to improve model diversity
and robustness.
• Implementing more sophisticated adversarial training
techniques that can anticipate and mitigate emerging
attack vectors.
• Creating hybrid detection systems that combine deep
learning with traditional rule-based approaches to lever-
age both statistical learning and domain knowledge.
• Optimizing the proposed framework for real-time appli-
cations by enhancing scalability and reducing computa-
tional costs.
• Further reﬁning XAI methods speciﬁcally tailored to
adversarial scenarios, improving the understanding of
model vulnerabilities.
• While this study focused on gradient-boosting ensem-
bles, future work is needed to assess the extent to
which these transferability patterns apply to heteroge-
neous architectures such as SVM and Random Forest.
