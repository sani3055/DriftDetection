ABSTRACT 
In an era where cyber threats are increasing at rapid rates, it is important to have 
cybersecurity in the form of smart systems that can be trusted. This proposed study will 
create a hybrid deep learning model to detect intrusion in time and with a built -in 
Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) feature to promote trust and explainability in 
its decision-making algorithm. To effectively capture both spatial and temporal features 
of network traffic data for reliable detection of known and zero -day attacks , the 
framework makes use of a combination of a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) and

the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM). The Batch normalization and dropout are being 
used in an attempt to stabilize the training and prevent overfitting in order to make the 
model reliable. SHAP and LIME are integrated in order to give explainability to the 
predictions made by the model showing the impact of each feature of the network on 
the prediction made by the model. This CNN-LSTM with XAI hybrid model shows better 
accuracy in detection abilities, lower false alarm rates, and a transparent decision 
making. This approach leads to the development of a dependable, interpretable, and 
trustworthy network intrusion detection system in the present-day networks.

I. INTRODUCTION 
Cybersecurity threats have become a huge issue in both enterprise and personal 
computing environments. This is mainly because of the rapid expansion of networked systems 
and the growing dependence on digital communication. The modern computer networks have 
to face a variety of cyberattacks non -stop such as denial-of-service attacks, probing activities, 
privilege escalation, and zero-day intrusions. To keep those networks safe, Intrusion Detection 
Systems (IDS) which consist of continuous traffic monitoring and identification of malicious 
activities are essential. Generally, IDS can be split into three categories, namely signature -
based IDS, anomaly-based IDS, and hybrid IDS. Signature-based IDS find intrusions by using 
already established attack patterns and are hence very useful against known thre ats, while 
anomaly-based IDS discover abnormal behavior and thus are able to detect previously unknown 
attacks. Hybrid IDS take advantage of both methods to improve detection accuracy and 
flexibility. 
Large-scale network traffic and changing attack strategies are frequently too much for 
traditional IDS techniques, which rely on pre-established rules or statistical techniques. These 
systems are less effective in dynamic environments because they are prone to high false alarm 
rates and have limited capacity to detect zero -day attacks [1]. The need for intelligent and 
adaptive intrusion detection systems that can recognize intricate patterns directly from network

data and function in real time is growing as cybercriminals continue to develop more complex 
tactics. 
Deep learning methods have shown a great deal of promise in solving these problems. 
While Long Short -Term Memory (LSTM) networks are ideal for simulating temporal 
dependencies and sequential patterns in traffic flows, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) 
are very successful at extracting spatial relationships among network traffic features [2]. A 
hybrid CNN –LSTM architecture can more successfully detect both known and unknown 
attacks by combining these complementary learning capabilities to recognize intrus ion 
behaviors that are complex and dynamic. 
The application of Deep Learning methods has shown considerable promise in 
overcoming such difficulties. The Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) is proven to be highly 
effective for exploring spatial relationships between network traffic characteristics, an d Long 
Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks are effective in exploring temporal relationships in 
network traffic sequences [2]. With this complementing learning capability in both CNN and 
LSTMs, a hybrid architecture is capable of identifying complex and dynamic intrusion attacks 
aptly. 
The objective of this proposed work is to create an intrusion detection system based on a 
hybrid CNN -LSTM model which is capable of accurately identifying both benign and 
malicious attacks in the cyber domain with enhanced robustness and a reduced number of false 
positives. The work intends to integrate Explainable Artificial Intelligence techniques, with 
emphasis on SHAP and LIME, as a means of deepening the transparency, interpretability, and 
trustworthiness of the decision-making process of the deep learning, based intrusion detection. 
Moreover, the real time monitoring and analysis of network traffic is another goal set, whereby 
the detection and subsequent response to the cyber threats that are rapidly evolving would be 
timely. Ultimately, the envisioned model aims at constructing a logical and user - friendly 
security framework that not only makes a smoo th transition between intrusion detection and 
explainability possible but also enables security analysts to efficiently carry out the 
visualization, interpretation, and validation of IDS alerts.

III. PROPOSED SYSTEM 
Our proposed system encompasses two primary components: 
i. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and Long Short -Term Memory (LSTM) 
networks are used in the Deep Learning Intrusion Detection Model Hybrid to 
resolve spatial and temporal characteristics of network traffic data. While 
sequential dependencies are represe nted in the LSTM module to identify 
complicated and dynamic intrusion behaviors, such as zero-day assaults, high-level 
spatial patterns of the input characteristics are identified in the CNN module. The 
techniques of batch normalization, dropout, and feature selection are used to 
improve the models' stability and reduce overfitting. Better generalization and 
reliable operation of benchmark datasets such as NSL -KDD can be achieved with 
the aid of the aforementioned approaches. 
ii. Explainable AI is used by the Elucidation of Artificial Intelligence (XAI) 
Integration System to guarantee interpretability and foster trust in the automated 
intrusion detection procedure. The most important characteristics that influence 
model predictions  are identified using SHAP (SHapley Additive explanations), 
which is applied to global interpretability. Additionally, LIME (Local Interpretable 
Model-Agnostic Explanations) provides instance -level explanations that explain 
why a particular network traffic  sample is deemed malicious or normal. When 
SHAP and LIME are combined, case -specific and comprehensive transparency is 
provided, enabling security analysts to learn more, verify, and have faith in the 
model's conclusions.

METHODOLOGY 
The proposed IDS-XAI architecture for intrusion detection and network traffic analysis 
interpretability is a combination of Convolutional -Based Deep Learning model (CNN) and 
Recurrent Neural Network with Memory (LSTM) models, followed by Explainable Artifi cial 
Intelligence (XAI) modules that use SHAP and LIME. The full methodology, shown in Fig.1, 
is made up of five steps: data cleaning, pattern extraction, model learning, label prediction, and 
model interpretation. 
The step-by-step breakup of the architecture is as follows: 
A. Data Preprocessing and Normalization: 
The input data is made up of network flow records taken from the benchmark dataset -
NSL KDD for intrusion detection. To ensure that the input features are useful and consistent 
for training the model, here data preprocessing is used. Cleaning, encoding, and normalization 
are done to the raw dataset. 
● Data Cleaning: Missing and unimportant attributes are taken out, and categorical 
features are turned into numbers using one-hot encoding or label encoding methods. 
● Scaling Features: Min–Max normalization is used to scale features to a certain range, 
which makes training go more smoothly. 
● Data Splitting: The dataset is segregated into three parts: training, validation, and 
testing. This is done to see how well the model can generalize. 
B. CNN–LSTM Hybrid Model for Intrusion Detection: 
The proposed IDS-XAI framework uses a hybrid deep learning model comprising Feature 
extraction neural network (CNN) and Sequence-learning neural model (LSTM) units to achieve 
spatial as well as temporal learning capabilities. The two-layer structure improves the capacity

Reasons for Choosing CNN–LSTM for Malicious Activity Detection System (IDS-XAI): 
● Comprehensive Feature Learning: LSTM models temporal dependencies, 
allowing for the precise detection of intricate intrusion patterns, while CNN 
captures spatial correlations among network features. 
● High Detection Efficiency and Robustness: The hybrid structure achieves strong 
generalization across a variety of dynamic attack types, improves learning stability, 
and lowers overfitting. 
● Explainable and Transparent Predictions : By ensuring interpretable model 
decisions through integration with SHAP and LIME, network attack detection 
results are increasingly trustworthy and accountable.

Working of CNN-LSTM: 
1) Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) for Spatial Feature Extraction 
The proposed IDS -XAI framework uses a one -dimensional Convolutional Neural 
Network (1D-CNN) as its primary feature extraction component. The input to the CNN is the 
normalized network traffic features, which by recognizing local dependencies among traffic 
attributes learn the discriminative spatial representations. The representations of the learned 


Explainable AI for Trusted Intrusion Detection System using Deep Learning  
https://iaeme.com/Home/journal/IJCA 39 editor@iaeme.com 
features reduce the need for manual feature engineering and provide compact inputs for the 
following LSTM layers for temporal modeling and intrusion classification. 
2) Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) for Temporal Feature Learning 
• To deal with the temporal and sequential dependencies inherent in the network traffic 
data, the Long Short -Term Memory (LSTM) network is used in conjunction with the 
features obtained from the CNN. Treating the traffic observations as ordered sequences, 
LSTM is able to detect the time -varying behavioral patterns, which are precisely the 
ones needed to separate normal activities from the malicious ones. The model's 
capability of sequential learning makes it possible to recognize the attack behaviors 
with multiple phases, such as prolonged connection anomalies and repeated access 
patterns. 
• The features obtained by CNN are given to the LSTM layer, which during its operation, 
learns temporal correlations among the consecutive network events, and this is done 
without needing manual sequence engineering. This combination leads to better 
detection of complex intrusion patterns that are difficult to detect by using spatial 
features alone. The output from the LSTM is then fed into a series of fully connected 
layers for classification, thereby ensuring strong generalization over the various attack 
types in the NSL-KDD dataset. 
C. Dense and Classification Layers: 
The last part of the hybrid model consists of the fully connected (dense) layers that 
combine the temporal features obtained by the LSTM into high -level features that are 
incorporated in the classification. 
● Dense Layers: The thick layers are a combination of the outputs of the LSTM units 
using linear transformations then non-linear activations. This procedure condenses the 
sequential information into small vectors which depict the possibility of each class 
(normal or attack). The dense layers also achieve a dimensional reduction and a feature 
interaction learning, improving the sharpness of decision boundaries across classes. 
● Dropout and Batch Normalization: Dense layers are separated by dropout layers to 
prevent overfitting, and training can be stabilized with the help of batch normalization 
to keep the distributions of activations the same. 
● Output Layer (Softmax Classification): A softmax activation function is used in the 
last layer, which generates a probability distribution of all target classes (e.g., Normal, 
DoS, Probe, R2L, U2R).

The model is able to make interpretable outputs based on XAI analysis frameworks by 
deciding the final result according to which class the model is most confident about. 
D. Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) Interaction: 
The use of the explainability mechanisms in this proposed IDS -XAI model is an 
important innovation that involves SHAP (SHapley Additive explanations) and LIME (Local 
Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanations) frameworks. 
● SHAP Analysis: SHAP can be used to produce the output of the CNN-LSTM model 
by calculating Shapley values which reflect on the amount of contribution made by 
each feature to the final predictions. 
○ Global Explainability: Aggregated SHAP values within a group of samples are 
used to determine features that have the strongest impact on the attack detection, 
and they provide information on how the model decides on a global scale. 
○ Local Explainability: In a specific case, SHAP explains which data points or 
characteristics caused the model to label the instance as normal or anomalous, and 
it helps security analysts to figure out why predictions were taken. 
● LIME Analysis: LIME gives explanations in terms of instances that vary with 
perturbation of input samples and monitoring of model predictions. Local Decision 
Boundary Analysis LIME is an approximation of the complex model by an 
interpretable surrogate (e.g., linear regression), used to demonstrate the effect of small 
changes in features on predictions. Visual Interpretability produces a set of color -
coded feature attribution which clearly differentiates between features affecting attack 
and normal classification. 
SHAP and LIME combined together can be used to ensure that the predictions that the 
deep learning model relies on can be explained to domain experts by making the predictions 
transparent, reliable, and interpretable. 
E. Model Training and Evaluation: 
We have used the preprocessed dataset to fine-tune the hybrid CNN–LSTM model. The 
error function is categorical cross-entropy, and Adam fine-tunes the learning rate on its own to 
help the model learn more effectively. The model is trained repeatedly over many epochs until 
it reaches convergence. 
To see how well the system works, we use performance metrics like accuracy, precision, 
recall, F1-score, and AUC-ROC. The IDS-XAI system reached an efficiency of over 90% after 
hyperparameter tuning and optimization. This showed that it could reliably find  both known 
and new attack patterns.

IV. DATASET AND PERFORMANCE EVALUATION 
A. Dataset Used: 
Intrusion Detection Dataset: NSL-KDD Dataset 
The NSL-KDD dataset has been accepted as the standard benchmark for evaluating IDS. 
It was created to improve upon the original KDD'99 dataset by removing duplicate entries and 
balancing the categories of records enabling fair and accurate model evaluation. 
It consists of network connection records described using 41 attributes that include basic 
TCP/IP characteristics, content-based attributes and patterns of traffic behavior. Each record is 
classified as either normal or attack. The attack records are group ed into four basic categories 
- DoS (Denial of Service), Probe, R2L (Remote to Local), and U2R (User to Root). 
The dataset is partitioned into two subsets (KDDTrain+ and KDDTest+) to enable 
unbiased model training and evaluation reducing overfitting. The NSL-KDD dataset is selected 
as the focus of this research, even though more recent datasets do exist, because it  has been 
widely adopted by the IDS research community; the labelling of the dataset is clear and 
consistent with moderate complexity; it is appropriate for use in reproducing benchmark test 
results and the attacks contained within the dataset represent fundamental attack behaviors that 
will still be present in the normal operating environment of a network in the not -to-distant 
future.

V. CONCLUSION 
This paper presents an IDS-XAI framework designed to improve the detection of unusual 
network behavior in complex cybersecurity settings. We used a hybrid deep learning 
architecture that combines CNNs for extracting spatial features and LSTMs for modeling 
temporal dependencies. This approach effectively captures both the structural and sequential 
aspects of network traffic. 
Results from experiments with the NSL-KDD dataset show that the proposed framework 
achieves a detection accuracy of 99.11%. It also maintains low false alarm rates and strong 
performance across different types of attacks. In addition, by using explainable artificial 
intelligence techniques like SHAP and LIME, we improve both the overall and localized
