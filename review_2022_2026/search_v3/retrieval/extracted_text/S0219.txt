Introduction: Phishing remains a dominant socio-technical threat to digital
infrastructure, serving as an entry vector for credential-theft, ransomware,
and supply-chain incidents. The machine learning literature on phishing-URL
detection remains fragmented across heterogeneous datasets, inconsistent
leakage controls, and explanation pipelines seldom cross-validated by
independent attribution methods.
Methods: An end-to-end, leakage-controlled machine learning framework was
developed over a balanced corpus of 11,430 instances and 87 engineered URL-
lexical, HTML-content, and external-reputation features, with all preprocessing
transforms ﬁt on the training partition only. Eleven heterogeneous classiﬁers
were benchmarked; the best classical model (LightGBM) was repurposed
as a deterministic feature extractor whose out-of-fold posterior probabilities
augmented the input to a hybrid CNN-BiLSTM. Statistical signiﬁcance was
assessedviaWilcoxonsigned-ranktestswithHolm-Bonferronicorrectionon10-
fold cross-validationF
1 scores, and two explainable-AI methods (Tree SHAP and
Permutation Importance) were applied alongside ablation studies.
Results: LightGBM attained F1 = 0.9699 and AUC = 0.9943, signiﬁcantly
outperforming every other classiﬁer (p ≤ 0.002) except XGBoost (p = 0.375).
The CNN-BiLSTM hybrid attainedF1 = 0.9652 and recall = 0.9703. SHAP and
Permutation Importance agreed on 11 of 15 top-ranked features, separating
external-reputation indicators (Google indexing, page rank, domain age, web
traffic) from URL-lexical complexity indicators (length, dots, hyphens, phishing
hints).
Discussion: Strict leakage control conﬁrms that the attainable performance
is intrinsically high rather than artifactual. The convergent dual-method
explanations translate into six candidate cybersecurity-awareness guidelines for
end-user training, connecting technical detection to the human factors that
shape enterprise security posture.

1 Introduction
Thedemocratizationofonlineservices,themigrationofcritical
infrastructuretocloud-nativearchitectures,andthepost-pandemic
normalization of remote work have collectively transformed
the attack surface of contemporary digital infrastructure
(Anti-PhishingWorkingGroup, 2023 ). Within this expanded
surface, phishing has consolidated its position as the dominant
threat vector. The Anti-Phishing Working Group reports that
the volume of unique phishing sites observed quarterly has
roughly tripled over the past 5 years, and the Verizon Data Breach
Investigations Report identiﬁes phishing as the initial-access
mechanism for a majority of conﬁrmed credential-theft and
ransomware incidents ( Verizon, 2023). Although phishing is
fundamentally a social-engineering attack that exploits the trust
heuristics of human users, the technical artifact through which
the attack is delivered, namely the URL, is suﬃciently rich in
distributional structure to enable automated detection through
machinelearning( Aljofeyetal.,2022 ).
The literature on machine learning-based phishing detection
now spans more than a decade. Early approaches relied on hand-
craftedblacklistsandrule-basedheuristicsoverURLstrings,which
suﬀered from poor generalization to unseen domains and required
continual manual curation (Prakashetal., 2010). The introduction
of supervised learning over engineered features, including URL
length, special-character counts, sub-domain depth, and the
presence of brand-mimicking tokens, substantially improved
coverageandisthemethodologicaltraditionintowhichthepresent
study falls (Mohammadetal., 2014 ; ¸Sahingozetal., 2019 ). More
recently, deep-learning approaches that ingest raw URL strings
via convolutional and recurrent encoders have shown competitive
performance, particularly when trained on very large corpora
(Leetal., 2018 ; Tajaddodianfaretal., 2020 ). Hybrid models that
fuse engineered features with learned representations occupy a
middle ground and have been reported to combine the data
eﬃciency of the former with the representational ﬂexibility of the
latter(Ozcanetal.,2023 ).
Despite this maturity, three methodological problems recur
with suﬃcient frequency to warrant systematic attention. First, the
leakage problem: a non-trivial fraction of published pipelines ﬁt
feature-selection or scaling transforms on the union of training
and test data, which inﬂates reported metrics and confounds
comparativeclaims( Kaufmanetal.,2012 ).Second,thesigniﬁcance
problem: model rankings are typically reported on point estimates
of accuracy or F
1-score, with little attention to whether the
observed gaps survive the noise of cross-validation. The Wilcoxon
signed-rank test is the appropriate non-parametric instrument for
this purpose (Demšar, 2006), but its use remains uneven in the
cybersecurity machine learning literature. Third, the explainability
problem: while SHAP (LundbergandLee, 2017) and permutation-
based attributions (Breiman, 2001) are now standard tools, single-
method explanations are vulnerable to method-speciﬁc artifacts,
and convergent attributions across orthogonal frameworks are
seldomreported.
The present study is motivated by the conjunction of
these three concerns and by an additional, application-oriented
observation: The machine learning literature on phishing rarely
closes the loop between predictive ﬁndings and the actionable
cybersecurity-awareness recommendations that constitute the
strongestpracticaldefenseagainstphishing.End-usersusceptibility
to phishing is repeatedly shown to be the binding constraint on
enterprise security posture (Jampenetal., 2020), and any technical
detection pipeline that does not surface its ﬁndings in a form
pedagogically useful for training programmes leaves a substantial
fractionofitspotentialvalueunrealised.
Against this background, the contributions of this study
are 4-fold. First, the study develops and empirically validates
a complete, leakage-controlled machine learning pipeline for
phishing-URLdetectionoverabalancedcorpusof11,430URLsand
87 engineered features. Second, it benchmarks 11 heterogeneous
classiﬁers spanning linear, tree-based, ensemble, instance-based,
probabilistic, and shallow-neural-network paradigms and veriﬁes
the statistical signiﬁcance of the resulting performance gradient
through Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on 10-fold cross-validationF
1
scores. Third, it proposes a hybrid CNN–BiLSTM architecture in
whichthebest-performingclassicalmodelservesasadeterministic
featureextractor,supplyingposterior-probabilityaugmentationsto
thedeepnetwork,anddemonstratesthatthishybridattainsarecall-
favorable operating point of practical security value. Fourth, it
conducts dual-method explainable-AI analysis using Tree SHAP
and Permutation Importance, documents strong inter-method
agreement, and translates the convergent explanations into a
compact set of cybersecurity-awareness rules suitable for end-
user training. A series of ablation studies isolates the marginal
contribution of each major design choice. The remainder of
the study is structured as follows: Section 2 surveys related
work; Section 3 details the proposed methodology; Section 4
reports the experimental results, including ablation analyses;
Section 5 discusses the implications for cybersecurity awareness;
andSection6concludes.
We state the scope of the contribution precisely to avoid
overclaiming. None of the individual ingredients, leakage-
controlled preprocessing, Wilcoxon-based comparison, posterior
augmentation, CNN–BiLSTM hybridization, or post-hoc
explainability, is in isolation novel to phishing detection. The
contribution is instead methodological and integrative: The value
lies in combining strict leakage control with non-parametric
signiﬁcance testing and family-wise error correction, in cross-
validating the explanation through two orthogonal attribution
methods rather than relying on a single method, and in carrying
the resulting convergent feature hierarchy through to an explicit,
falsiﬁablesetofend-userawarenessguidelines.Weareexplicitthat
the CNN–BiLSTM hybrid does not improve aggregateF
1 over the
strongest single classiﬁer; its role is to demonstrate a leakage-safe
mechanism for injecting tree-ensemble knowledge into a deep
model and to expose a recall-favorable operating point, and we
evaluateitonthosetermsratherthanonaggregateaccuracy.
2 Related work
2.1 Engineered-feature approaches to
phishing detection
The earliest generation of machine learning phishing detectors
reliedonrule-basedanddecision-treemodelsoverasmallnumber
of URL-lexical features ( Gareraetal., 2007 ). Mohammadetal.
Frontiersin ArtiﬁcialIntelligence 02 frontiersin.org

Alam et al. 10.3389/frai.2026.1875321
(2014) extended this paradigm by curating a thirty-feature set
drawn from URL syntax, page content, and WHOIS metadata
and demonstrated that an ensemble of neural networks could
exceed 92% accuracy on a balanced corpus. ¸Sahingozetal.
(2019) explored a broader feature universe and reported that
random-forest classiﬁers consistently outperformed linear and
instance-based alternatives. The dataset used in the present study
extends these traditions by combining URL-lexical descriptors
withHTML-contentdescriptorsandexternal-reputationindicators
including page rank, Google indexing, domain age, and web
traﬃc (HannousseandYahiouche, 2021). The empirical regularity
that emerges from this body of work is consistent: Tree-based
ensembles, including random forests, extra trees, and gradient
boosting, tend to dominate other classical model families on
engineered phishing features, in part because they accommodate
the heavy-tailed and discontinuous distributions characteristic of
URL-lexicaldatawithoutrequiringscalingortransformation.
2.2 Gradient boosting in cybersecurity
The XGBoost framework (ChenandGuestrin, 2016 ) and the
subsequent LightGBM framework (Keetal., 2017 ) have become
standard tools for tabular-data classiﬁcation in cybersecurity
applications, including malware detection, intrusion detection,
and credit-card fraud detection. Their dominance reﬂects three
properties:histogram-basedsplit-ﬁndingthatscalestolargefeature
counts, regularized leaf-weight learning that controls overﬁtting,
and native handling of missing values. In the phishing-detection
setting, several studies have reported that LightGBM and XGBoost
achieve test F
1-scores in the 0.96–0.98 range on engineered-
feature datasets of the size used here ( Alsarieraetal., 2020 ;
GandotraandGupta, 2021 ). The present study conﬁrms this
regularity and additionally demonstrates that the gap between
LightGBMandXGBoostisnotstatisticallysigniﬁcantonthecorpus
considered, an observation that has practical consequence for
deployment decisions where inference latency or model size may
diﬀerbetweenthetwoframeworks.
2.3 Deep learning over URLs
Parallel to the engineered-feature literature, a strand of
work treats URLs as raw character sequences and applies
convolutional and recurrent networks directly. Leetal. (2018)
introduced URLNet, a character-level convolutional network
for URL classiﬁcation, and Huangetal. (2019) extended this
approach with attention mechanisms. Ozcanetal. (2023)
developed a hybrid that combines engineered features with
a CNN-LSTM character-level pathway. The CNN–BiLSTM
architecture proposed here diﬀers in two respects. First, it
operates on engineered features augmented with the posterior
probabilities of a high-performing tree ensemble, rather than
on raw URL characters; this design preserves the interpretability
of the underlying features while still allowing the deep network
to discover non-linear interactions among them. Second, the
parallel CNN and BiLSTM branches are merged via global
average pooling and concatenation rather than sequential
composition, reducing parameter count and improving training
stability on the relatively modest dataset size considered.
Bidirectional LSTMs in particular have been shown to capture
context-sensitive dependencies that unidirectional recurrences
miss ( SchusterandPaliwal, 1997 ; GravesandSchmidhuber,
2005), and this advantage carries over to the engineered-
feature setting where feature ordering can be exploited by the
convolutionalbranch.
2.4 Explainable artiﬁcial intelligence in
phishing detection
The use of explainable-AI techniques to interpret phishing
classiﬁers has grown rapidly.LundbergandLee (2017) introduced
SHAP, which provides theoretically grounded local and global
feature attributions through Shapley values from cooperative
game theory.Lundbergetal. (2020) subsequently developed Tree
SHAP, an exact polynomial-time algorithm for tree ensembles.
Permutation Importance (Breiman, 2001) provides an orthogonal,
model-agnostic alternative whose attributions are derived from
observed performance degradation under feature perturbation.
Although both methods are now common in the phishing
literature, they are typically reported in isolation, leaving open
the question of whether their attributions are mutually consistent.
The present study addresses this gap by applying both methods
to the same model and quantifying the overlap between their
top-rankedfeatures.
2.5 Cybersecurity awareness as a defensive
layer
Cybersecurity awareness research ( Jampenetal., 2020 ;
Parsonsetal., 2017 ) consistently ﬁnds that the human user is the
binding constraint on phishing defense: Even highly accurate
technical detectors will be circumvented by some fraction of
attacks that reach end users, and the susceptibility of those users
to social-engineering cues determines the residual risk. The most
eﬀective awareness programmes translate technical insights into
a small number of memorable rules that users can apply during
everyday browsing (Abawajy, 2014). The convergent explanations
producedbySHAPandPermutationImportanceinthisstudylend
themselves naturally to such translation, and Section 5 develops
thisconnection.

3 Methodology
3.1 Dataset and preliminary inspection
The empirical investigation was conducted on a publicly
available, large-scale phishing URL corpus comprisingN = 11,430
instances and d = 89 attributes, of which 87 are engineered
numerical features, one is the raw URL string, and one is the
categorical target (HannousseandYahiouche, 2021 ). The class
distribution is perfectly balanced with 5,715 legitimate and 5,715
phishing samples, eliminating the need for synthetic minority
oversampling and ensuring that accuracy remains a meaningful
evaluationmetric. Figure1 presentstheclassdistributionasaradial
chart. A comprehensive integrity check conﬁrmed the absence of
missing values, duplicate rows, and constant columns. The raw
URL ﬁeld was excluded from modeling because the dataset already
encodes its lexical signal through 87 derived features and retaining
the string would introduce a quasi-identiﬁer capable of causing
trivial memorization. The categorical target was transformed via
labelencodingsuchthat y ∈{0,1},where y = 0denotesalegitimate
URLand y = 1denotesaphishingURL.
3.2 Feature taxonomy

“other”groupcomprisingthetwofeaturesthatdidnotfallintothe

3.10 The CNN–BiLSTM hybrid architecture
The augmented vectors were reshaped to X ∈ RN×52×1,
treating the feature axis as a one-dimensional sequence. We make
explicit that this reshaping imposes a sequential interpretation on
what is fundamentally an unordered tabular feature vector: The
convolutional kernels act on locally adjacent feature positions and
the bidirectional LSTM models order-dependent transitions along
thefeatureaxis,yettheorderingofthe50selectedfeatures(mutual-
information rank, followed by the two appended posteriors)
carries no intrinsic semantic adjacency. The architecture should
therefore be understood as using convolution and recurrence as
generic parametric mixers over the feature set rather than as
exploitingameaningfulsequence,andtheempiricalgainsreported
in the ablation study (Section 4.6) are accordingly evidence of
useful inductive capacity rather than of a recovered feature order.
We did not ﬁnd that permuting the feature order materially
changed performance in preliminary checks, consistent with this
interpretation; a systematic study of whether a principled ordering
(for example, by hierarchical feature clustering) improves the
hybrid is left to future work. The proposed network instantiates
two parallel branches that consume this tensor and merge before
classiﬁcation. Figure9 presentsthefullarchitecturediagram.

4.7 Synthesis
The full experimental campaign supports four principal
claims. First, gradient-boosted ensembles, especially LightGBM
and XGBoost, are the most accurate single-stage classiﬁers on
engineered phishing-URL features, with statistically equivalent
performancetooneanotherbutsigniﬁcantlysuperiorperformance
to all other model families. Second, preprocessing under strict
train-only ﬁtting eliminates leakage as an explanation for the
high reported scores, indicating that the attainable performance
of the engineered representation is intrinsically high rather than
artifactual. Third, injecting tree-ensemble posterior probabilities
into the input sequence of a CNN–BiLSTM hybrid yields
a recall-favorable operating point useful in security-sensitive
deployments without sacriﬁcing overall accuracy, and the ablation
analysis conﬁrms that this augmentation is the dominant single
contributor to the deep network’s performance. Fourth, a small,
semantically coherent subset of features anchored on Google
indexing, page rank, domain age, and URL lexical complexity
explains the bulk of the classiﬁer’s behavior and oﬀers an
operationally meaningful set of awareness indicators for end-user
cybersecuritytraining.

5.1 Implications for cybersecurity
awareness
The convergent explanations produced by SHAP and
Permutation Importance identify a small set of human-
interpretable features whose values discriminate phishing
from legitimate URLs with high reliability. These features sort
cleanlyintotwogroups,suggestingacompactpedagogicalframing

external-reputation indicators that are typically opaque to end

search results?”, are accessible through routine browser checks.
The second group consists of URL-lexical complexity indicators

large numbers of dots and hyphens, the absence of a recognizable
second-levelbrand,andthepresenceofsuspicioustokens.

5.3 Limitations
Three limitations should be acknowledged. First, the corpus,
while large by historical standards, represents a single public
dataset captured at one point in time, and the distribution of
phishing URLs is known to drift as adversaries adapt to deployed
detectors (Tianetal., 2018 ). The present evaluation includes no
temporal split, no cross-dataset transfer test, and no external
validation corpus; consequently, the performance levels reported
here should be interpreted as an upper bound on the steady-
state operating performance of a deployed system rather than
as evidence of cross-distribution generalization. Establishing the
latterrequires(i)achronologicaltrain-on-past,test-on-futuresplit
to quantify concept-drift decay, and (ii) evaluation on at least
one independently collected phishing corpus to measure cross-
dataset transfer; we identify both as essential directions for future
work rather than claims of the present study. Continual retraining

5.4 Future directions
Several extensions of the present framework are immediate.
The feature-extractor construction generalizes beyond LightGBM,
and an ensemble of feature extractors drawn from the top-
three classiﬁers could provide a more robust embedding. The
CNN–BiLSTM architecture could be augmented with a self-
attention layer over the bidirectional hidden states (Vaswanietal.,
2017) to make long-range dependencies between features more
salient. On the awareness side, the convergent feature rankings
could be operationalised as a browser extension that exposes
the dominant indicators to users in real time, providing a
passive training signal during routine browsing. Finally, the
performance evaluation should be replicated on temporally
split corpora to quantify the rate of concept drift and the
correspondingretrainingcadencerequiredtomaintainoperational
performance.

This study presented a complete machine learning framework
for phishing-URL detection over a balanced corpus of 11,430

hybrid CNN–BiLSTM architecture, augmented with the out-of-

improve aggregate F1 over LightGBM but provides a recall-
favorable operating point of practical value in deployments where
missed detections are costlier than false alarms. Ablation studies
